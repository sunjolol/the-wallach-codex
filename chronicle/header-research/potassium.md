# Potassium — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 27 sealed claims · mineral (electrolyte, category blue) · target: 5,000 mg/day, stated directly by Wallach (Immortality, 2008).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — the major cation INSIDE the cell.** Potassium is "essential to all organisms" and the major cation (positive ion) in cell cytoplasm, driving a wide variety of electrochemical and enzyme functions; it makes up **5% of the body's total mineral content** and is the main mineral of the intracellular fluid (WAL-CLM-RARE-000152, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000192, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000094). The body holds **≈140 grams** of potassium total (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000189). This is the identity spine: not abundant like calcium, not trace like selenium — the electrolyte that lives *inside* your cells.

**★ The standout mechanism — you cannot bank it.** Potassium is easily absorbed, but **90% of what you take in is excreted in the urine** and "essentially there is no storage of potassium in the human body," so it must be replaced constantly — Wallach's stated **significant daily intake of 5,000 mg** (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193, echoed WAL-CLM-RARE-000154, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000094). This "flows straight through you" fact is the single most distinctive thing in the pack and the direct reason for the large daily number.

**★ The regulatory gap — 99 vs 5,000.** Wallach records that the **FDA restricts potassium in a supplement to 99 mg** (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000463), a cap he flags alongside his program, not one of his own doses. Against his 5,000 mg/day need (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193) that is a ~50× gap (arithmetic on the two grounded numbers: 5000 ÷ 99 ≈ 50; present as a computed ratio, not a Wallach quote).

**The electrolyte trio.** Wallach groups sodium, chloride and potassium as the three indispensable "electrolytes": **sodium 2%, potassium 5%, chloride 3%** of total body mineral; sodium & chloride are mainly *extracellular*, potassium is *intracellular* (WAL-CLM-RARE-000187). With sodium, potassium maintains water balance, osmotic equilibrium and acid–base (pH) balance; with **calcium** it regulates neuromuscular activity (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000192, WAL-CLM-RARE-000153).

**Deficiency — two features + one gravest event.** Across four books the same pair recurs: **"muscular weakness and mental apathy"** are the features of chronic K deficiency, and **hypokalemic cardiac failure** (the heart failing for lack of potassium) is "the most serious event" (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000094, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000194, WAL-CLM-RARE-000155). The full deficiency table (WAL-CLM-LETS-000025) is long — acne, arrhythmia, cognitive impairment, constipation, depression, ECG changes, edema, fatigue, glucose intolerance, high cholesterol, hyperreflexia, hypotension, insomnia, palpitations, tachycardia, dry skin, etc.

**The real-world cause is drugs, not diet.** A *natural* potassium deficiency is "highly unlikely" — grains, fruits and vegetables are rich in it — **but it can be common in people taking diuretics** for high blood pressure or weight loss (WAL-CLM-RARE-000038). Diuretics (natural and prescribed), plus sweating from colds/flu/exercise, vomiting and diarrhea, all speed the loss of potassium above normal (WAL-CLM-RARE-000156, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000194).

**Both ends bite the heart (tension).** Deficiency's gravest event is cardiac *failure* (above); **toxicity/excess** lists **cardiac arrest**, cognitive impairment, dysarthria (slurred speech), dysphasia, weakness (WAL-CLM-LETS-000026). Too little and too much both hit the heart — a clean "narrow window" story if a concept wants it.

**Curio A — the name is literally "pot-ash."** Plants were humanity's early potassium source; wood and plants were burned to make a potassium-rich ash called **"potash,"** which is where "potassium" comes from. Because potassium is essential to plants and sodium is not, plants take up the potassium and let dissolved sodium wash to the oceans (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000190). Related: potassium nitrate = **"saltpeter,"** a centuries-old preservative and one of the three parts of gunpowder (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000191).

**Curio B — you are (very slightly) radioactive.** Potassium-40, a natural isotope with a **1.25-billion-year half-life**, decays to argon and is used to date the oldest rocks (3.8 billion yrs). Since 0.012% of natural potassium is K-40 and you carry ≈140 g of potassium, everyone holds a small amount — "a natural and life-long contributor to our background radiation" (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000189).

**Chemistry curio.** A silver-white alkali metal so soft it can be cut with a knife, family with lithium and sodium, never found pure in nature, first isolated by Sir Humphrey Davy in 1807 (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000188). Nature's spread (marine plants 52,000 ppm; soft tissue holds the most in animals) is in WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000187.

**Protocols (supporting, not lead material).** Cardiac arrhythmia → potassium 500 mg 3×/day within a B/Cr/Se/Mg stack (WAL-CLM-LETS-000203); depression → potassium 250 mg 4×/day in a large stack (WAL-CLM-LETS-000244); common cold → chicken-rice soup for electrolytes "especially potassium" (WAL-CLM-LETS-000225); muscle cramps = a "mini convulsion" from Ca/Mg/K/Se/vit-E deficiency (WAL-CLM-LETS-000374); obesity → "potassium salt with kelp" (WAL-CLM-LETS-000384); Addison's disease → sodium loss + potassium retention, "salt hunger" (WAL-CLM-RARE-000188).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A — "You can't bank it"
- **The hook** — potassium runs straight through you (90% out in the urine, none stored), which is exactly *why* you need a large dose every single day.
- **Layout shape** — a single left-to-right flow statement: intake → cell → urine, with the "kept: essentially none" beat as the payoff, then the daily-number resolution. No beats-grid, no pull-quote-chassis.
- **Illustration** — ONE simple through-flow figure: an arrow entering (intake), a thin sliver retained, and a large stream leaving labelled "90% → urine." Fewest elements; the visual argument is "in one side, out the other." Keep every label off the flow stroke (route the arrow around text).
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193 ("90 percent… excreted through the urine," "essentially… no storage," "5,000 mg"); WAL-CLM-RARE-000154; WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000094 ("about 90 percent… excreted").
- **Why it wows / best UX** — this is the most potassium-specific fact in the whole pack and it *is* the answer to "why 5,000 mg?", so the header and the daily-target tip reinforce each other. None of the six shipped headers uses a flow-through idea.

### B — "99 vs 5,000" (the regulatory-gap two-lane)
- **The hook** — Wallach says you need 5,000 mg a day; the FDA caps a supplement at 99 mg. Same mineral, ~50× apart.
- **Layout shape** — a two-lane comparison: one short lane (the 99 mg legal cap) beside one long lane (the 5,000 mg daily need), with a single connecting line of copy naming the gap. A comparison, not a chassis.
- **Illustration** — two bars on one shared scale — a tiny 99 mg stub against a 5,000 mg bar ~50× longer. One idea, two marks. Optionally annotate the long bar with "≈ 50 capped pills" (flag: that count is arithmetic 5000÷99, derived from the two claims, not a Wallach quote).
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000463 ("FDA restricts… to 99 mg"); WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193 (5,000 mg daily). Ratio is computed, not stated.
- **Why it wows / best UX** — one glance delivers a genuinely surprising, specific tension unique to potassium, and it motivates the daily-target number for free. Highest instant-impact option.

### C — "Inside vs outside the cell"
- **The hook** — potassium is the electrolyte that lives *inside* your cells, sodium's opposite number outside — the balance is the point.
- **Layout shape** — a single cell-membrane cross-section as the centrepiece; a one-line identity statement above, the trio shares (K 5% inside / Na 2% + Cl 3% outside) read off the figure. A where-it-lives map, distinct from calcium's whole-body abundance map.
- **Illustration** — one membrane line with a labelled interior (K⁺, "the inside cation") and exterior (Na⁺, Cl⁻); a small paired caption "with sodium → water & pH balance · with calcium → nerve & muscle." One divided figure, ≤4 labels, no strokes through text.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-RARE-000187 (Na 2% / K 5% / Cl 3%; K intracellular, Na+Cl extracellular); WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000192 ("major cation of the intracellular fluid"); WAL-CLM-RARE-000153 (sodium/calcium partnerships).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — teaches the one structural fact that separates potassium from the other electrolytes, and the inside/outside geometry is instantly legible. Distinct archetype from all six shipped.

### D — "Pot-ash" (the etymology curio)
- **The hook** — "potassium" is literally named after the ash left when you burn wood — the plant's stored potassium.
- **Layout shape** — a short did-you-know narrative arc (wood → ash "potash" → the element's name), the etymology as the anchor and the "plants keep the potassium, the sea gets the sodium" fact as the turn. Curio-led, like vitamin A's Egypt-liver beat but a full header.
- **Illustration** — one small, clean vignette: a burnt log → a pile of pale ash labelled "potash (K-rich)" → "K." Three marks, one caption. No busy scene.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000190 (potash origin; plants take K, sodium washes to oceans); optional aside WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000191 (saltpeter/gunpowder). **Alternate curio if D is disliked:** the K-40 radioactivity story (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000189) — "you carry ≈140 g of potassium, a pinch of it ticking on a 1.25-billion-year clock" — equally grounded, arguably higher wow.
- **Why it wows / best UX** — memorable and low-stakes; gives the element a story a reader repeats. Weakest tie to the daily number, so it leans hardest on the separate "why this number" tip.

## 3. Proposed lede (PROPOSAL — Luneth ratifies)

1. "The main electrolyte inside your cells — about 5% of all the mineral in you, working with sodium to hold your water and pH balance and with calcium to fire nerve and muscle — yet stored so poorly that most of it leaves in the urine each day and must be replaced." (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000192 · WAL-CLM-RARE-000187 · WAL-CLM-RARE-000153 · WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193)
2. "The positive charge that runs inside your cells — 5% of your body's mineral, the electrolyte paired against sodium — and the one mineral the body barely keeps, flushing most of what you eat straight out in the urine." (WAL-CLM-RARE-000152 · WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000192 · WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193)

_Both name identity (inside-the-cell electrolyte, 5%) so they don't restate concept A/B/C's opening beat verbatim; if the lead concept is A (no-storage), lean lede toward option 1's identity clause and let A carry the flow-through beat._

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

Target is a **direct Wallach statement, not a computed chain.** Wallach says the body stores essentially no potassium — about 90% of what you take in leaves in the urine — so it must be replaced daily, "requiring a significant daily intake of **5,000 mg**" (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193, Immortality, 2008). There is no IU conversion and no body-weight scaling here; the 5,000 mg is stated outright as a maintenance intake, and the target simply carries it forward (provenance: original_low 5,000 mg, upper_taken 5,000 mg). Wallach's earlier Base-Line program lists a slightly higher **5,500 mg** True Supplement Need (WAL-CLM-LETS-000062, Let's Play Doctor, 1995); the target uses the **newer 5,000 mg** figure (favor-newest), so the tip should read the number off Immortality and can note the 1995 figure as the older, marginally higher variant.

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Solo header: YES.** Potassium easily merits its own bespoke header — 27 claims, a distinctive core mechanism (no-storage), a striking regulatory-gap number, two clean curios, and a full deficiency/toxicity spread. It should **not** fold into another element.
- **No superseded claims.** Every claim has `superseded_by: null`. The only number-vs-number choice is 5,000 (2008) vs 5,500 (1995) — resolved by favor-newest to 5,000; flag both to Luneth.
- **claim_text-vs-verbatim watch (minor):** WAL-CLM-RARE-000155's *claim_text* adds "≈90% excreted… ~5,000 mg" but its own *verbatim* is only the two-features + gravest-event sentence — the numbers there are context, not in that quote. For any 90%/5,000 figure, quote WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000193 or WAL-CLM-RARE-000154, whose verbatims carry the numbers. Not a defect, just don't pull the number *by* RARE-000155's verbatim.
- **The ~50× / "50 pills" figure is arithmetic**, derived from two grounded numbers (5000 ÷ 99). Present it as a computed ratio; do not render it as a Wallach quotation.
- **A stray magnesium claim rode along:** WAL-CLM-RARE-000169 is primarily about magnesium body-content (only mentions "second to K as an intracellular cation"). Usable for the "K is *the* #1 intracellular cation" ranking, but its subject is magnesium — don't source a potassium fact from it beyond that ranking.
- **Wished-for but absent:** no claim gives a per-organ potassium distribution the way calcium's 99%-in-bone number does (the 5% figure is total-mineral share, not a tissue map), so a calcium-style "where it lives in the body" abundance map is only weakly supported — concept C uses the inside/outside membrane split instead, which the pack does support. No pump/Na-K-ATPase mechanism is stated (don't invent one).

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**B — "99 vs 5,000."** It delivers the biggest instant, potassium-specific surprise from a single glance and motivates the daily-target number for free — but it *rests on* concept A's no-storage mechanism (that is *why* the need is 5,000), so build B with A's flow-through fact as the one-line explainer beneath the two bars.
