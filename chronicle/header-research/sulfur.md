# Sulfur — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 10 sealed claims · mineral (blue accent) · target: 500 mg/day, a plain Wallach True-Supplement-Need figure.

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — a structural atom in most proteins.**
Wallach's own definition: sulfur is an important structural atom in most proteins, carried by the sulfur amino acids **cystine, cysteine, and methionine**, and central to **glutathione** (a cysteine-containing tripeptide essential to cellular reactions with those amino acids) [WAL-CLM-RARE-000219]. This is the trunk the whole element grows from — sulfur is not a bystander mineral, it is built into the protein alphabet.

**The standout mechanism — the sulfhydryl clasp (-SH → -S-S-).**
Sulfur's signature chemistry: the reduced **-SH (sulfhydryl)** form in cysteine and the oxidized **disulfide -S-S-** form in the double molecule cystine. Wallach says this group is "important for the specific configuration of some structural proteins and for the biological activities of some enzymes" [WAL-CLM-RARE-000220]. In plain terms: sulfur is the *clasp* that locks a protein into its working shape. Uniquely visual and uniquely sulfur.

**Sulfur is hidden everywhere — the roll-call.**
Sulfur-containing proteins "work in indirect ways to maintain life": **hemoglobin, hormones (insulin and the adrenal cortical hormones), enzymes, and antibodies** [WAL-CLM-RARE-000221]. It also appears in carbohydrates — **heparin** (an anticoagulant concentrated in the liver), **chondroitin sulfate** (in cartilage; Knox gelatin) — and in the vitamins **thiamine (B1)** and **biotin**, which contain bound sulfur [WAL-CLM-RARE-000222]. A "you never think about it, yet it's in all of these" abundance-of-roles palette.

**The most striking deficiency picture — connective tissue collapses.**
Wallach: "Deficiency of sulfur results in degenerative types of arthritis involving degeneration of cartilage, ligaments, tendons, Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, Sickle-cell anemia and various 'collagen diseases'" [WAL-CLM-RARE-000223; same verbatim re-mapped to arthritis at WAL-CLM-RARE-000303]. The deficiency picture is a breakdown of the body's *structural scaffolding* — the same theme as the mechanism, seen from the failure side. Sulfur is also named as a co-factor deficiency in Wallach's calcium/osteoporosis cluster [WAL-CLM-RARE-000275], and among the deficiencies he blames for arthritis: calcium, magnesium, boron, copper and sulfur [WAL-CLM-RARE-000303].

**First visible sign — brittle nails.**
Wallach reads fingernails as "good barometers of absorption and nutritional status": bluish = lung/oxygen, white spots = zinc, ridges = iron/calcium, and **brittle nails = sulfur amino-acid deficiency** [WAL-CLM-LETS-000276]. His fingernail protocol adds gelatin (Knox / "diabetic Jello"), essential fatty acids at 5 g three times a day, and betaine hydrochloride + pancreatic enzymes at 75–200 mg three times a day before meals [WAL-CLM-LETS-000276]. This is sulfur's direct analog to copper's premature-gray-hair "first visible sign."

**The closed loop — sulfur builds cartilage; its lack destroys cartilage.**
Two claims lock together into a uniquely-sulfur story: sulfur IS in cartilage as **chondroitin sulfate** [WAL-CLM-RARE-000222], and sulfur deficiency **degenerates that same cartilage** into arthritis [WAL-CLM-RARE-000223]. The atom that builds the scaffold is the atom whose absence collapses it.

**Curio — poison through sulfur's own back door.**
"The toxic action of arsenic comes from its ability to combine with sulfhydryl groups" [WAL-CLM-RARE-000222]. Arsenic kills by hijacking sulfur's own -SH chemistry — a striking did-you-know, though thin (one clause).

**Curio — Ma Lan's vinegar bone soup.**
On fishing dates Ma Lan ate the fish bones and simmered fish/chicken/pork/beef bones in a vinegar solution to leach calcium, magnesium, manganese, boron, copper, **sulfur** and other minerals into the soup [WAL-CLM-DDDL-000309]. A folk-practice curio, but sulfur is only one name in a list — weak as a solo hook.

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A — "The clasp" (mechanism, single annotated figure)
- **Name** — The Clasp.
- **The hook** — Sulfur is the atom that locks a protein into its working shape.
- **Layout shape** — A single centred chemical figure with a short caption; no beats. One line of setup above, the resolution below.
- **Illustration** — Two protein ribbons pulled taut and joined by ONE **-S-S- disulfide bridge** at the middle: left ribbon ends in `-SH`, right ribbon ends in `HS-`, and the clasp `-S-S-` snaps them together. Two elements + one bond. (Route no ribbon-stroke through the `-SH` / `-S-S-` labels — set them off the strands.)
- **Anchored by** — [WAL-CLM-RARE-000220] (-SH sulfhydryl; disulfide -S-S- in cystine; "specific configuration of some structural proteins" + "biological activities of some enzymes"); [WAL-CLM-RARE-000219] (cystine/cysteine/methionine; glutathione).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — The most chemically distinctive thing about sulfur, and nothing in the 6 shipped headers is a molecular-bond figure. A skim-reader instantly gets "this atom is a fastener."

### B — "Builds it, then breaks it" (closed-loop / two-state comparison)
- **Name** — The Cartilage Loop.
- **The hook** — Sulfur IS your cartilage — and when it runs short, that same cartilage degenerates.
- **Layout shape** — A two-state pairing read left→right (or a small closed loop): STATE 1 "sulfur present → chondroitin sulfate holds cartilage, ligaments, tendons"; STATE 2 "sulfur short → they degenerate → arthritis, lupus, the 'collagen diseases'." Resolve on the named collapse.
- **Illustration** — One joint drawn twice: intact on the left (cushioned), frayed/thinning on the right — OR a single arc-loop with sulfur at the top feeding cartilage and the broken return-arc labelled "degeneration." Fewest elements: one repeated object, one difference between the two states.
- **Anchored by** — [WAL-CLM-RARE-000222] (chondroitin sulfate = cartilage; Knox gelatin); [WAL-CLM-RARE-000223] / [WAL-CLM-RARE-000303] ("degeneration of cartilage, ligaments, tendons," SLE, sickle-cell, "collagen diseases").
- **Why it wows / best UX** — The tightest, most uniquely-sulfur narrative in the pack: the builder and the destroyer are the same atom. Emotionally lands the arthritis stakes without a chart.

### C — "Hidden in all of these" (abundance-of-roles constellation)
- **Name** — Everywhere You Never Look.
- **The hook** — You never think about sulfur, yet it's built into your blood, your insulin, your antibodies, even two of your vitamins.
- **Layout shape** — A clean constellation / labelled-chip set radiating from a central "S": each chip is one sulfur-bearing molecule, grouped loosely (proteins · hormones · defence · vitamins). NOT a wired many-station diagram — discrete named tiles, one idea ("all of these contain sulfur").
- **Illustration** — A central sulfur atom "S" with 6–8 satellite chips: hemoglobin · insulin · adrenal cortical hormones · antibodies · heparin · chondroitin sulfate · thiamine (B1) · biotin · glutathione. Keep chips as plain labels, no crossing connectors between them.
- **Anchored by** — [WAL-CLM-RARE-000221] (hemoglobin, insulin, adrenal cortical hormones, enzymes, antibodies); [WAL-CLM-RARE-000222] (heparin, chondroitin sulfate, thiamine B1, biotin); [WAL-CLM-RARE-000219] (glutathione, sulfur amino acids).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Turns an "invisible" mineral into an "oh, it's in everything" reveal. Distinct layout family (abundance/where-it-lives, like calcium) but with a molecular roster rather than a body map.

### D — "Read your nails" (first-visible-sign)
- **Name** — The Barometer.
- **The hook** — The first place a sulfur shortage shows is in your fingernails — brittle nails.
- **Layout shape** — One large annotated figure with a single call-out line and the target/"why" tucked beneath; sulfur's direct answer to copper's gray-hair beat.
- **Illustration** — A single fingernail rendered **brittle / splitting / cracking at the free edge** (NOT white spots — that figure belongs to zinc), one label: "brittle nails → sulfur amino-acid shortage." Optional second micro-line naming the fix (gelatin / Knox).
- **Anchored by** — [WAL-CLM-LETS-000276] (nails as barometers; "brittle nails indicate sulfur amino acid deficiencies"; gelatin, EFAs 5 g ×3/day, betaine-HCl + pancreatic enzymes 75–200 mg ×3/day); [WAL-CLM-RARE-000219] (sulfur amino acids).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Instantly personal and relatable, the proven "first-visible-sign" archetype. **Adjacency risk:** zinc already ships a nail figure (white spots). The illustration MUST read as cracking/brittleness, not spots, to stay distinct — flagged in §5.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

The 500 mg/day target is a **plain Wallach figure taken directly, with no scaling or unit conversion**. It comes from the adult **Base Line Nutritional Supplement Program** in *Let's Play Doctor* (1995), where sulphur's **True Supplement Need (daily maintenance target) is 500 mg** [WAL-CLM-LETS-000067]. The provenance confirms this is a straight lift: `original_low` 500 mg = the posted target 500 mg — no upper-of-range step (the row has no high figure), no IU factor, no ×1.54 body-weight scale. In the source row the numbers appear in column order — government RDA (blank/"?"), then Wallach's 500 mg True Supplement Need, then his **1,000 mg 30-day pharmacologic dose**; the header shows the *maintenance* 500 mg, not the short-term 1,000 mg therapeutic figure, and never the RDA (which Wallach reprints only to argue against).

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Solo header: YES, merited.** 10 claims, a distinctive and visual chemistry (disulfide clasp), a clean maintenance target, and one of the corpus's strongest deficiency stories (connective-tissue collapse). Ample bespoke material — no need to fold into another element.
- **Duplicate verbatim across two claims.** [WAL-CLM-RARE-000223] and [WAL-CLM-RARE-000303] carry the **identical verbatim** sentence, differently mapped (one to the deficiency panel Ch11 p377, one to the arthritis/calcium cluster Ch7 p169). Cite whichever mapping the block needs; do not present them as two independent Wallach statements.
- **[WAL-CLM-RARE-000275] is calcium-centric, not sulfur.** Its claim_text is an osteoporosis/calcium-deficiency claim in which sulfur appears only as one named co-factor. Usable to show sulfur as a *supporting* deficiency, but do NOT hang a sulfur hook on it.
- **No structured deficiency_signs.** The pack's top-level `deficiency_signs` array is empty; the deficiency material lives inside the claims (000223/000303 = diseases; 000276 = brittle nails). Pull signs from the claims, not that field.
- **Zinc-nail adjacency (Concept D).** Zinc's shipped header uses a white nail-spots figure. Sulfur's nail sign is *brittleness*, which is genuinely distinct, but the illustration must visibly read as cracking/splitting, never spots, or it will echo zinc.
- **Curios are thin.** Arsenic-via-sulfhydryl [WAL-CLM-RARE-000222] and Ma Lan's bone soup [WAL-CLM-DDDL-000309] are real but single-clause / list-mention; fine as a small curio beat inside another concept, too thin to carry a header alone.
- **Wished-for but absent:** no claim on dietary sources of sulfur, no deficiency prevalence/epidemiology, and no anecdote of sulfur *reversing* a named case (the arthritis link is stated as cause, not a documented recovery) — so a decline→reversal *timeline* like selenium's is NOT supported; Concept B stays a static two-state comparison, not a reversal rail.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**B — "Builds it, then breaks it" (the cartilage loop).** It is the single most uniquely-sulfur, fully-grounded narrative in the pack (the same atom that forms cartilage as chondroitin sulfate is the one whose absence degenerates it into arthritis), it carries real stakes, and it needs no chart — one repeated object with one difference. **A (The Clasp)** is the strong runner-up and the most visually novel against the 6 shipped headers.
