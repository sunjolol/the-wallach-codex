# Vitamin E (Tocopherol) — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 68 sealed claims · vitamin (fat-soluble) · target: 134 mg/day (Wallach, derived from a 100–200 IU daily-multiple range).

Grounding note: every fact below cites a claim ID from the sealed pack. Nothing here comes from outside the pack. The corpus is lopsided — ~4 identity/mechanism/deficiency claims carry almost all the distinctive material; the remaining ~60 are Let's Play Doctor Ch10 protocol recipes that each list "vitamin E 800–1,200 IU" as one line item. That shape drives the concept choices (see §5).

---

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

### A. Core identity / definition
Vitamin E is not one molecule but a **group of antioxidant compounds — the (alpha-)tocopherols**, sold as "mixed tocopherols." Its defining job: **protect cell membranes from oxidative inflammation and keep red blood cells from rupturing** (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033). It is fat-soluble and behaves as much like a protective *oil* as a vitamin — Wallach uses it both orally and applied directly to skin (see angle E). Category: vitamin (fat-soluble), symbol none, display name Tocopherol.

### B. The standout mechanism(s)
- **The fat-layer antioxidant that slows aging itself.** Beyond guarding membranes and RBCs, Wallach credits vitamin E with **slowing aging, preserving telomere length, and (with selenium) lowering the risk of certain cancers**, plus reducing risk of Alzheimer's, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, muscular dystrophy and cystic fibrosis (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033). The **telomere-length** claim is the single most striking, least-expected mechanism fact in the pack.
- **The selenium partnership ("won't work alone").** Three of E's deficiency diseases are each flagged "**in conjunction with selenium deficiency**": cystic fibrosis, muscular dystrophy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033). Cystic fibrosis is framed as an acquired deficiency disease worsened by diets low in vitamin E and high in polyunsaturated fats (WAL-CLM-RARE-000010). Fibromyalgia = a multiple-deficiency disease of fried foods/vegetable oils + selenium + vitamin E + sulfur amino acids (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000098).
- **Cancer-therapy amplifier.** Beta-carotene taken *with* vitamin E can be pushed to a **600,000 IU vitamin-A equivalent**, of significant benefit especially for carcinomas — the vitamin A so provided also helps the thymus make antibodies and makes laetrile more effective (WAL-CLM-LETS-000196).

### C. Deficiency signs — the striking / visible ones
- **Age spots & liver spots = "rust."** The signature visible sign: brown **age spots / liver spots from lipid peroxidation of fats by free radicals** (ceroid pigment building up in the skin). Wallach explicitly calls liver spots "a more ominous sign than freckles" and reads them as free-radical rust (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033, WAL-CLM-RARE-000231, WAL-CLM-LETS-000282). This is E's equivalent of copper's "premature grey hair."
- **Red blood cells that burst → hemolytic anemia.** Vitamin E keeps RBCs from rupturing; deficiency shows as **RBC fragility** (WAL-CLM-LETS-000041) and **anemia from hemolysis** (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033, WAL-CLM-RARE-000231).
- **Easy bruising / bleeding under the skin.** Capillary fragility; bleeding under the skin may indicate a vitamin E or vitamin K deficiency (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000089; also WAL-CLM-LETS-000173, WAL-CLM-LETS-000261).
- **A neurological cluster.** areflexia (absent reflexes), gait disturbances, ophthalmoplegia (eye-muscle paralysis), proprioception problems, vibratory-sense dysfunction (WAL-CLM-LETS-000041).
- **Muscle & other systemic signs.** muscle weakness with myalgia/fibromyalgia; infertility; depressed immune system; ischemic heart disease; fibrocystic breast disease (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033, WAL-CLM-RARE-000231); alopecia, dermatitis, malabsorption, muscular dystrophy (WAL-CLM-LETS-000041).

### D. The dose ladder (a genuine number story)
Wallach's vitamin-E figures escalate by purpose across the books — a clean, distinctive number progression:
- **100–200 IU/day** — the Epigenetics daily-multiple maintenance range (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000120) — the target's source.
- **400 IU/day** — the "True Supplement Need" in the Base Line Nutritional Supplement Program (WAL-CLM-LETS-000075). (Same row prints 15 IU RDA, which Wallach reprints only to argue against.)
- **800–1,200 IU/day** — the near-universal therapeutic dose across ~40 Ch10 condition protocols.
- **1,200 IU** — the 30-day pharmacologic dose (WAL-CLM-LETS-000075); Alzheimer's (WAL-CLM-LETS-000130), cardiomyopathy (WAL-CLM-LETS-000204).
- **1,200–2,000 IU/day** — escalated after a heart attack (WAL-CLM-LETS-000295).

### E. Signature protocols / anecdotes / curio
- **"Arrested or even cured."** Given selenium + vitamin E **by injection (IM or IV) at the very first onset of symptoms, muscular dystrophy can be arrested or even cured** (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000072). Muscular dystrophy / Keshan disease framed as a preventable selenium-deficiency disease eliminated in livestock ("white muscle disease") by giving selenium to pregnant and young animals (WAL-CLM-LETS-000373).
- **The topical oil — E's dual nature.** Uniquely among the vitamins here, vitamin E is applied *directly to the skin as an oil*: on burns (WAL-CLM-LETS-000175), bedsores (WAL-CLM-LETS-000154), each chicken-pox vesicle (WAL-CLM-LETS-000215), diaper rash (WAL-CLM-LETS-000247), hangnails (WAL-CLM-LETS-000291), acne lesions (WAL-CLM-LETS-000123), breast tenderness (WAL-CLM-LETS-000170). Inside you it guards membranes; outside you it is a wound/skin oil.
- **Ubiquity.** Vitamin E appears in ~40 distinct Ch10 condition protocols (arthritis, anemia, gallstones, PMS, dysmenorrhea, shingles, psoriasis, varicose veins, scoliosis, tardive dyskinesia, poor circulation, etc.) — almost always at 800–1,200 IU. Individually thin, collectively it paints E as Wallach's default antioxidant workhorse.

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### Concept A — "The rust of aging" (first-visible-sign)
- **Name:** Rust Spots.
- **The hook:** Those brown spots on aging hands are fat rusting from the inside — the first place a vitamin E shortage shows.
- **Layout shape:** A quiet, mostly-empty field dominated by ONE illustration of skin with a forming brown spot, and a single explanatory line beneath naming the mechanism ("lipid peroxidation — free radicals rusting the fat in your skin into ceroid pigment"). No 1-2-3 beats. One short pull-line, optionally the "more ominous than a freckle" note.
- **Illustration:** A single patch of skin (a slice of the back of a hand) with one age spot rendered as an oxidation bloom — a clean "rust" gradient, no diagram stations, no strokes through any label. Fewest elements: skin + one spot + one caption arrow that lands *beside* (never through) the word.
- **Anchored by:** age spots/liver spots from lipid peroxidation & free radicals (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033, WAL-CLM-RARE-000231); "ceroid pigment… a more ominous sign than freckles" (WAL-CLM-LETS-000282).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Relatable and self-checkable (readers look at their own hands), and it is E's true signature sign in Wallach — the direct analogue to copper's premature grey. Visually calm, one idea, high recognition.

### Concept B — "How much depends on how sick" (the dose ladder)
- **Name:** The Ladder.
- **The hook:** Vitamin E's dose isn't one number — it climbs from a daily pinch to a post-heart-attack flood.
- **Layout shape:** A vertical escalating measure (four/five rungs bottom-to-top), each rung a dose + its purpose. The header's fixed "why this number" tip lives naturally right at the bottom rung. No pull-quote required.
- **Illustration:** A single vertical scale/column that grows — rungs at 100–200 IU (daily maintenance), 400 IU (true supplement need), 800–1,200 IU (therapeutic), 1,200–2,000 IU (after a heart attack). One rising element; labels sit to the side of the rungs, never crossed by the riser line.
- **Anchored by:** 100–200 IU (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000120); 400 IU + 1,200 IU (WAL-CLM-LETS-000075); 800–1,200 IU therapeutic (e.g. WAL-CLM-LETS-000146, WAL-CLM-LETS-000275); 1,200–2,000 IU post-MI (WAL-CLM-LETS-000295).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Turns the target number from a bare fact into a story, and honestly surfaces that the maintenance target (134 mg / ~200 IU) is the *floor*, not the therapeutic dose. Layout is unlike any shipped header (a scale, not beats/cards/rail).

### Concept C — "Guard within, oil without" (dual-role identity)
- **Name:** Two Surfaces.
- **The hook:** One nutrient, two jobs — inside you it shields cell membranes and red blood cells; outside you it's the oil you rub straight onto a burn.
- **Layout shape:** A single figure split by the body's boundary (an interior side and a skin/exterior side), one caption each — NOT comparison cards (deliberately different from the vitamin-A trade-off cards). Reads as one continuous illustration divided by the skin line, not two panels.
- **Illustration:** Left/inside: a cell membrane (or a red blood cell held intact) with E as a protective layer. Right/outside: a single drop of E oil on skin over a healing lesion. The dividing line is the skin itself; labels sit in the clear space on each side.
- **Anchored by:** protects cell membranes + keeps RBCs from rupturing (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033); topical oil on burns (WAL-CLM-LETS-000175), bedsores (WAL-CLM-LETS-000154), chicken-pox vesicles (WAL-CLM-LETS-000215), hangnails (WAL-CLM-LETS-000291).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** The topical-oil use is genuinely unique to E among the vitamins mined so far — a "did you know it's the same thing?" moment. The inside/outside device is a fresh layout not yet used.

### Concept D — "The pair that can't work alone" (the selenium duet)
- **Name:** The Duet.
- **The hook:** Three diseases only appear when BOTH vitamin E *and* selenium run low — and giving both, fast, can reverse the damage.
- **Layout shape:** Two elements shown as interlocking/overlapping (a Venn-like meeting or two clasped halves), with the three shared diseases named in the overlap, and one payoff line: injected at first symptom, muscular dystrophy can be "arrested or even cured."
- **Illustration:** Two simple tokens (E and Se) meeting; the overlap zone holds three labels — cystic fibrosis, muscular dystrophy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Minimal: two shapes + three words + one arrow to the payoff. No routed strokes through text.
- **Anchored by:** the three "in conjunction with selenium deficiency" diseases (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033); cystic fibrosis worsened by low E (WAL-CLM-RARE-000010); Se + E IM/IV at first onset arrests/cures MD (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000072); fibromyalgia multiple-deficiency (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000098).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Strongly supported and captures a real identity fact — E as a team player. **Caveat (see §5): this leans on selenium, whose header already shipped; it should not be the lead, to avoid reading as a selenium re-skin.**

*(All four are distinct in BOTH layout and illustration: a single skin figure; a vertical scale; an inside/outside split; an overlap/duet. None is the eyebrow → 1-2-3 → big-number → quote chassis.)*

## 3. Proposed lede (PROPOSAL — Luneth ratifies)
Voice-matched to calcium/selenium/copper; states what E IS; sits above "At a glance"; does not restate Concept A's age-spot opening.

- **v1 (identity + partnership):** "The body's fat-guarding antioxidant — a family of tocopherol oils that shield every cell membrane and keep red blood cells from bursting, and the vitamin Wallach pairs with selenium against muscular dystrophy, cystic fibrosis and a failing heart." (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033)
- **v2 (identity + the telomere wow):** "A family of antioxidant oils — the tocopherols — that protect every cell membrane from oxidation and, Wallach says, slow aging itself, even helping preserve the length of your telomeres." (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033)

Recommend v1 (broader, sets up more concepts); v2 if the design leans into the anti-aging angle.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)
Target: **134 mg/day.** Chain (all from the pack's provenance block): Wallach's newest book, *Epigenetics* (2014), gives vitamin E (mixed tocopherols) a daily-multiple range of **100–200 IU** (source claim WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000120). The derivation takes the **upper of the range, 200 IU**, and converts with the standard factor **0.67 mg per IU** (1 IU natural d-alpha-tocopherol = 0.67 mg): 200 × 0.67 = **134 mg**. No body-weight scaling is applied (unlike the mineral targets). Note honestly: this is a **maintenance floor**. Wallach's other figures run higher — 400 IU "true supplement need" and up to 1,200–2,000 IU therapeutically (WAL-CLM-LETS-000075, WAL-CLM-LETS-000295) — but the daily-multiple range from the newest book is the chosen basis (favor-newest-number).

## 5. Gaps / flags
- **Selenium overlap is the biggest risk.** E's most-cited diseases (muscular dystrophy, cystic fibrosis, cardiomyopathy/Keshan) are shared with selenium, whose header already shipped. Concept D leans hardest on this; keep the E header E-centric (antioxidant identity, age spots, topical oil, dose ladder) and treat the selenium tie as one supporting angle, not the spine.
- **Corpus is protocol-heavy, mechanism-thin.** ~60 of 68 claims are Ch10 recipes listing "vitamin E 800–1,200 IU" among many ingredients — low standalone illustrative value. The distinctive weight rests on ~4 claims (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000033 is the workhorse; WAL-CLM-RARE-000231, WAL-CLM-LETS-000041, WAL-CLM-DDDL-000072). Concepts must draw from those, not from the recipe pile.
- **A number typo to avoid.** The chorea protocol (WAL-CLM-LETS-000220) prints vitamin E as "800–12,000 IU" — almost certainly an OCR/typo for 800–1,200. Do NOT surface 12,000 IU anywhere. (The dossier does not use it.)
- **No superseded claims; numbers are internally consistent** across books (the IU figures agree; only the *purpose* changes the dose). No claim_text inflates beyond its verbatim.
- **Absent angles (do not invent):** the pack has **no** discovery, etymology, history, or dietary-food-source claim for vitamin E, and no explicit "what foods contain it." So a "sources" or "discovery" concept is NOT supported — do not build one.
- **Solo header: YES.** 68 claims, a canon fat-soluble vitamin, and rich E-specific material (age-spot "rust," telomeres, the topical-oil dual nature, the dose ladder). It clearly merits a bespoke solo header; no folding into another element.

## 6. Recommended lead concept
**Concept A — "The rust of aging" (Rust Spots).** It is the most visual, most self-checkable, and most genuinely E-specific hook (E's answer to copper's premature-grey sign), anchored by three independent claims with a stated mechanism (lipid peroxidation → ceroid pigment). Concept C (Two Surfaces) is the strongest alternate if a more upbeat, identity-first opening is preferred; Concept B (The Ladder) is the best choice if the number itself should carry the header.
