# Flavonoids — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 17 sealed claims · vitamin (bioflavonoids) · target: 5,000 mg/day (upper of Wallach's 1,000–5,000 mg daily-multiple range), plus condition-specific clinical doses.

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — "vitamin P," named 1936, for sealing the small blood vessels.**
Flavonoids (bioflavonoids) were "once called vitamin P for their role in small-blood-vessel permeability" — antioxidant, anti-inflammatory plant compounds that support vascular and capillary function and lower cardiovascular-disease risk (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`). Wallach dates the name: the rare-earths table row reads literally "Bioflavonoids - 1936" (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000243`, verbatim). This is the strongest single hook — a compound that earned a *vitamin letter* purely because it keeps capillary walls from leaking.

**The three named members.** Wallach names the flavonoids he means: "rutin, catechin, quercetin" (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000128`, verbatim, in the allergy protocol). Quercetin recurs by name in the diabetes and inflammation protocols (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000246`, `WAL-CLM-LETS-000320`), and rutin recurs as its own dosed item alongside bioflavonoids in bursitis and hay fever (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000176`, `WAL-CLM-LETS-000292`).

**Standout deficiency mechanism — the leaky capillary.** The permeability role means a shortage shows up as blood leaking where vessels are thinnest. The `WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046` VERBATIM lists the deficiency results plainly: "Capillary hemorrhage / Reduced immune capacity / Widespread free radical injury and inflammation / Hemorrhoids / Venous insufficiency / Leg ulcers / Bruising / Nosebleeds / Increased cancer risk." `WAL-CLM-RARE-000243` groups the picture into two families in its claim_text — **blood-vessel signs** (capillary hemorrhage, easy bruising, nosebleeds, hemorrhoids, venous insufficiency, leg ulcers) vs **systemic signs** (reduced immunity, free-radical injury/inflammation, cancer risk). (See the §5 flag: its verbatim only supports "capillary hemorrhage, reduced immune function" + the 1936 date.)

**Deficiency signs (structured, from the pack's deficiency_signs list):** capillary_hemorrhage (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`, `WAL-CLM-RARE-000243`), lowered_immunity (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000243`), shortened_lifespan (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046` — but see §5: NOT in that claim's verbatim).

**Signature protocols — one compound, dosed across a dozen conditions.** Bioflavonoids are Wallach's recurring reach in Let's Play Doctor, and the striking thing is the dose SPREAD across conditions:
- Cluster (histamine) headaches — 150 mg t.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000224`)
- Common cold — 150 mg t.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000225`)
- Diabetes — incl. quercetin, 150 mg/day (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000246`)
- Inflammation — 150 mg/day, + quercetin 100 mg/day (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000320`)
- Hay fever — 150 mg q.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000292`)
- Influenza — 150–300 mg/day (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000279`)
- Food allergy — 150–300 mg/day (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000280`)
- Migraine — 200 mg b.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000364`)
- Herpes simplex / cold sores — 200 mg t.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000299`)
- Cataracts — 300 mg (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000207`)
- Varicose veins — 600 mg t.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000460`)
- Bursitis — 1,200 mg/day, + rutin 50 mg t.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000176`)
- Gastric (peptic) ulcers — 1,000 mg q.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000458`)
- Allergies — bioflavonoids as rutin/catechin/quercetin, no mg stated (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000128`)

Range therefore runs from **150 mg** (a cold) to **1,000 mg four times a day** (a bleeding ulcer). The high end (peptic ulcer 4,000 mg/day, bursitis 1,200 mg, varicose veins 1,800 mg/day) clusters on the *vascular/tissue-repair* conditions; the low end (150 mg) on the *allergic/viral* conditions.

**The allergy/viral reflex.** Across the allergic-and-viral conditions — allergies, hay fever, cluster headache, colds, flu, food allergy, herpes — the protocol reliably pairs **vitamin C to bowel tolerance + bioflavonoids** (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000128`, `-000292`, `-000224`, `-000225`, `-000279`, `-000280`, `-000299`). Wallach treats migraine and cluster headache as *food-allergy* problems, not vascular ones (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000364`, `-000224`).

**Curio / surprising fact.** It carries a vitamin letter (P) and a discovery year (1936) yet is a *family of plant compounds*, not a single molecule (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000243`, `WAL-CLM-LETS-000128`). The name comes from **P**ermeability, not from being the 16th vitamin discovered (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A — "Vitamin P: the sealed vessel"
- **Name** — The sealed vessel.
- **The hook** — Flavonoids are the compound that keeps your smallest blood vessels from leaking; run low and they bleed.
- **Layout shape** — A single richly-annotated illustration owning most of the block, with the "vitamin P / permeability" naming as the heading line and the leak-signs as short labelled callouts off the figure. No beats row, no big-number stat.
- **Illustration** — ONE capillary drawn in two adjoining states along its length: a sealed, intact wall on the left; the same wall thinning/leaking on the right with a few dots of escaped blood. Two or three callout labels (capillary hemorrhage · bruising · nosebleeds) placed OFF the vessel, never crossed by it. Fewest elements: one vessel, one transition, three labels.
- **Anchored by** — vitamin P / "permeability of the small blood vessels" (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`, `WAL-CLM-RARE-000243`); leak signs "Capillary hemorrhage / Bruising / Nosebleeds" (verbatim of `WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Same archetype the shipped copper header nails (a shortage with a *first visible sign*), but with a mechanism a reader can literally see. The name "vitamin P" is a genuine surprise and the leaky-vessel image makes the whole element click in one glance.

### B — "One compound, a dozen doses"
- **Name** — The dose ladder.
- **The hook** — Wallach reaches for the same compound again and again — from 150 mg for a cold up to 1,000 mg four times a day for a bleeding ulcer.
- **Layout shape** — A calibrated dose scale (vertical ladder or horizontal axis) with a handful of condition markers pinned at their dose. Grouped so the low rungs read "allergic/viral" and the high rungs read "vascular/repair." No quote, no beats.
- **Illustration** — One axis line with ~5–6 tick markers (don't crowd all 14): cold 150 mg → migraine 200 mg → cataracts 300 mg → varicose veins 600 mg → bursitis 1,200 mg → peptic ulcer 4,000 mg/day. Each marker is a short label sitting beside (never on) the axis.
- **Anchored by** — every dose is a real claim: 150 mg (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000225`), 200 mg b.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000364`), 300 mg (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000207`), 600 mg t.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000460`), 1,200 mg (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000176`), 1,000 mg q.i.d. (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000458`).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Shows the BREADTH of use without a wall of conditions, and the low→high grouping quietly teaches the mechanism (small doses calm allergy/immunity; big doses rebuild leaky tissue). Distinct from every shipped figure.

### C — "Two faces of a shortage"
- **Name** — Two faces.
- **The hook** — Run low and it shows two ways at once: the vessels leak, and the whole body weakens.
- **Layout shape** — A two-lane split (two columns), one lane "In the vessels," one lane "In the body." Each lane holds a short stack of signs. No central figure — the split IS the layout.
- **Illustration** — Minimal: a paired icon per lane (a vessel/drop for the vascular lane, a body/shield for the systemic lane), each heading a short list. Lists kept to 3–4 items so neither is a wall.
- **Anchored by** — vessel lane: capillary hemorrhage, bruising, nosebleeds, hemorrhoids, venous insufficiency, leg ulcers (verbatim of `WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`); body lane: reduced immune capacity, free-radical injury/inflammation, increased cancer risk (verbatim of `WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`); the two-group framing itself (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000243` claim_text).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Uses Wallach's own two-bucket structure. A clean comparison layout not used by any shipped header. Caution: keep both lists to verbatim-supported signs (see §5).

### D — "Vitamin P, 1936"
- **Name** — The 16th vitamin that wasn't.
- **The hook** — It earned a vitamin letter in 1936 — yet it's really a family of plant compounds, and the "P" stands for permeability, not for a place in the alphabet.
- **Layout shape** — A did-you-know curio card: a dated headline ("Vitamin P · 1936") leading a short origin line, then the three named members as small named chips. Compact, text-forward, one small mark — the counterpoint to A/B/C's figures.
- **Illustration** — Almost none: a period-styled "1936" as the visual anchor, with three labelled chips — rutin · catechin · quercetin — beneath. No diagram, no strokes.
- **Anchored by** — "Bioflavonoids - 1936" (verbatim `WAL-CLM-RARE-000243`); "vitamin P" + permeability (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`); named members "rutin, catechin, quercetin" (verbatim `WAL-CLM-LETS-000128`).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Pure curio, the vitamin-A-header "ancient-Egypt liver" energy. Lightest-weight of the four and a good foil if Luneth wants a text-led header rather than a figure-led one.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

The header target is **5,000 mg/day**. It comes straight from Wallach's Epigenetics daily-multiple recommendation, which gives bioflavonoids a range of **1,000–5,000 mg** daily (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000126`, verbatim "Bioflavonoids 1,000 - 5,000 mg"); the derive takes the **upper** of that range (provenance: original_low 1,000, original_high 5,000, upper_taken 5,000). No IU conversion and no body-weight scaling apply — bioflavonoids are dosed in plain milligrams. Worth noting honestly for the tip: this element's coverage kind is *dietary_with_clinical_lever* — the 5,000 mg is the daily baseline target, and the per-condition therapeutic doses in Let's Play Doctor (150 mg for a cold up to 1,000 mg four times daily for an ulcer) are a separate clinical lever layered on top, not the everyday number.

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **claim_text richer than verbatim — read carefully (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000243`).** Its verbatim is only "Bioflavonoids - 1936 / capillary hemorrhage, reduced immune function." The long claim_text (bruising, nosebleeds, hemorrhoids, venous insufficiency, leg ulcers, cancer risk, shortened lifespan, reduced telomere length, cardiovascular-risk reduction) is NOT in that verbatim. Most of that list IS independently supported by the verbatim of `WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046` — EXCEPT "shortened lifespan," "reduced telomere length/viability," and the CVD-risk-reduction detail, which appear in NEITHER claim's verbatim (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046` verbatim ends at "Increased cancer risk"). **Do not put telomere/lifespan/blood-pressure claims on the visible header;** use only verbatim-backed signs. This is why Concept C must draw its two lists from the EPIGEN verbatim, not the RARE claim_text.
- **Deficiency_signs list includes shortened_lifespan (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`)** but that sign is not in the claim's verbatim (see above) — treat as a structured-metadata sign, not header-quotable.
- **"Antioxidant / anti-inflammatory" identity** rests on claim_text; the verbatim support is the deficiency line "Widespread free radical injury and inflammation" (`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000046`) — usable as an implication, fine for a lede, but there is no standalone mechanistic verbatim beyond the permeability/free-radical framing.
- **Allergies claim has no mg (`WAL-CLM-LETS-000128`)** — omit it from any dose-scale (Concept B) or show it as "named, no dose."
- **No superseded claims** — all superseded_by are null; no conflict to resolve.
- **Solo header: YES.** 17 claims, a unique and vivid identity (vitamin P / permeability / 1936), a genuine first-visible-sign, and an unusually broad protocol spread. This element easily merits its own bespoke header and needs no folding into another.
- **Wished-for but absent:** no verbatim explaining *how* flavonoids seal vessels (the "P/permeability" is named, not mechanistically described), and no food-source list (no "where you get it" claims in the pack) — so a sources-map concept is not supported; leave it out.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**A — "The sealed vessel."** It carries the element's single strongest, most surprising hook (a plant compound that earned the vitamin letter *P* just for keeping capillaries from leaking), pairs a see-it mechanism with a copper-style first-visible-sign, and every element is verbatim-anchored. B (the dose ladder) is the strong runner-up if Luneth wants breadth over identity.
