# Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 37 sealed claims · vitamin (water-soluble) · target: 400 mcg/day (Epigenetics daily multiple, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000117).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

### Core identity — a vitamin built around a single cobalt atom
Wallach frames B12's essentiality as unusual: the body does not require cobalt on its own but a cobalt *complex* — cyanocobalamine, i.e. vitamin B12 (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233). A single cobalt atom is the central metal component of the molecule (WAL-CLM-RARE-000114). B12 is a water-soluble red crystalline substance, and **the red colour comes from the cobalt atom at its centre** (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000044). This is the cleanest, most distinctive identity hook in the whole pack — a metal at the heart of a vitamin, and that metal is what colours it.

### The "borrowed vitamin" — we cannot make it ourselves
Wallach calls the need for B12/cobalt a possible symbiosis: **microbes build B12 out of elemental cobalt, and vertebrates — us — require the finished B12** (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233). A requirement for pure cobalt is found only in some bacteria and algae. He ties this to the soil: below 0.07 ppm cobalt leaves crops (and the animals/people eating them) deficient, while 0.11 ppm prevents and cures the deficiency (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084). This gives a genuine soil → microbe → animal → human supply chain, all grounded.

### The standout mechanism — myelin, DNA, and the folate lock
B12 works with folate to synthesize DNA and RNA, supports bone marrow, and supports the production of **myelin — the nerve-fibre insulation** (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000040). It is also required to *release folate from its bound form*, so a B12 deficiency causes a folic-acid deficiency even when folate intake is optimal (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000040) — a striking "one shortage triggers another" mechanism. It works with folic acid, choline and methionine to transfer methyl groups in RNA/DNA synthesis and is required for growth, myelin formation and red-blood-cell synthesis (WAL-CLM-RARE-000114).

### The signature deficiency — nerve insulation stripped away
Wallach's "classic" trio for B12/cobalt deficiency: **spinal cord demyelination, progressive neuropathy, and pernicious anemia** (WAL-CLM-RARE-000238; also WAL-CLM-RARE-000115). The fuller Epigenetics list adds dementia, neuropsychiatric behaviour, demyelination of brain / spinal cord / optic nerve / peripheral nerves, a sore tongue, general weakness and liver disease (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000040). The Let's Play Doctor deficiency table adds achlorhydria, birth defects, constipation, depression, dizziness, dyspnea, fatigue, geographic tongue, headache, irritability, moodiness, numbness, palpitations and psychosis (WAL-CLM-LETS-000035). The demyelination-of-nerve-insulation image is the strongest visual mechanism.

### The absorption gauntlet — eating it isn't enough
Deficiency can be dietary OR secondary to hypochlorhydria (low stomach acid), salt deficiency, or gluten intolerance (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000040). Wallach lists failure-to-absorb causes: surgical removal of part of the stomach or ileum, low stomach acid, parasites (tapeworm), celiac disease, other malabsorption (WAL-CLM-RARE-000115). In his anemia protocol, betaine hydrochloride 75–200 mg before meals is *required to assure absorption of B-12* (WAL-CLM-LETS-000137). Geographic tongue is a "red flag" that you are not absorbing B-12 (and B-3/B-2/B-6/B-5/folate/zinc) from celiac-like small-intestine changes (WAL-CLM-LETS-000284).

### The fragile vitamin — how easily it's lost
About **30% of B12 activity is lost during cooking** (electric, gas, or microwave); it is slowly deactivated by acid, alkali, light, and oxidizing/reducing substances (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000045). **Pasteurized and evaporated milk have lost 40 to 90 percent of their B12** (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000219). Rich food sources: liver, kidney, fresh whole milk, eggs, cheese, muscle meats (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000219, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000232) — the latter noting vegans risk deficiency without such foods.

### Dose / the RDA gap
The government RDA is just **3–4 mcg/day**, which Wallach reprints only to argue against; he recommends **250–400 mcg** for a "respectable safety margin," urging pregnant/nursing mothers especially to supplement (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084, WAL-CLM-RARE-000014). His Let's Play Doctor base-line program lists a True Supplement Need of 200 mcg and a 30-day pharmacologic dose of 1,000 mcg (WAL-CLM-LETS-000072). His newest book's daily multiple gives 400 mcg of methylcobalamin (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000117). So Wallach's figure is roughly **100× the RDA**.

### Signature protocols / anecdotes (all B12 at 1,000 mcg/day unless noted)
- **Amblyopia** (a type of blindness from B-12 deficiency or tobacco): B-12 intramuscular 1,000 mcg/day **to a total of 20,000 mcg usually effects a "cure"** (WAL-CLM-LETS-000132). A strong, quotable curio.
- **Bell's palsy**: B12 1,000 mcg/day to a total of 20,000 mcg (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000086, WAL-CLM-LETS-000158).
- **Meningocele / spina bifida / anencephaly**: caused by a deficiency of folic acid, B-12, or zinc and vitamin A during early pregnancy — prevention is the goal (WAL-CLM-LETS-000359).
- **Anemia**: betaine HCl to assure absorption, then B-12 1,000 mcg/day for 20 days + folic acid (WAL-CLM-LETS-000137).
- Also cited across: diabetes (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000049, WAL-CLM-LETS-000246), canker sores / aphthous stomatitis (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000080, WAL-CLM-LETS-000143), Crohn's — best by injection (WAL-CLM-LETS-000238), depression (WAL-CLM-LETS-000244), epilepsy — IM (WAL-CLM-LETS-000267), hepatitis (WAL-CLM-LETS-000298), shingles (WAL-CLM-LETS-000300), liver disease (WAL-CLM-LETS-000348), neuralgia (WAL-CLM-LETS-000379), carpal tunnel (WAL-CLM-LETS-000383), Reye's (WAL-CLM-LETS-000417), vitiligo at 2,000 mcg/day (WAL-CLM-LETS-000462).

### Interdependence curios
Nickel and B12 are interdependent — optimal B12 is necessary for nickel's function, and B12 deficiency increases the need for nickel (WAL-CLM-RARE-000194). Lithium: short-term supplementation raises serum B12, long-term lowers it (WAL-CLM-RARE-000167). B12 was isolated from liver extract in 1948 and shown to have anti-pernicious-anemia activity (WAL-CLM-RARE-000115).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A. "The red crystal" — the cobalt at the centre
- **Name** — The Red Crystal.
- **The hook** — B12 is a vitamin built around a single atom of a metal, and that cobalt atom is literally what makes it red.
- **Layout shape** — A single, large, centred hero illustration (the molecule/crystal) with a few short annotation labels radiating out; minimal supporting text beneath. No beats, no 1-2-3.
- **Illustration** — One red crystalline form (a stylised faceted red crystal or single ring-molecule) with ONE highlighted atom at its dead centre labelled "cobalt." Fewest possible elements: the shape, the centre atom, two or three call-out words ("red because of the cobalt at its heart"). Route no label-line through the crystal — set labels outside its silhouette.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-DDDL-000044 ("red crystalline… red colour is due to the cobalt in the molecule"), WAL-CLM-RARE-000114 ("single cobalt atom is the central metal component"), WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233 (cobalt complex = cyanocobalamine).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — The single most memorable, uniquely-Wallach fact and a colour hook a skim-reader can't miss. One image, one idea. NOTE the calibration risk under §5 (magnesium already shipped a "metal at the centre" figure).

### B. "The borrowed vitamin" — a supply chain from soil to you
- **Name** — Made By Microbes.
- **The hook** — B12 is one nutrient your body flatly cannot make — you inherit it, finished, from microbes that build it out of raw cobalt in the soil.
- **Layout shape** — A horizontal left-to-right journey/relay in a few discrete stops, each a small vignette with one line of copy; ends on "us." A provenance/handoff spine, not a beats stack.
- **Illustration** — A simple 3–4 node chain: soil (a cobalt speck, "0.07 ppm = deficient · 0.11 ppm = cured") → microbe (builds B12) → food/animal → person (requires the finished molecule). One clean connecting path with the nodes ON it, labels above/below — never a stroke through a word.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233 (symbiosis: microbes generate/manufacture B12 from elemental cobalt; vertebrates require B12), WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084 (soil 0.07 / 0.11 ppm thresholds).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — A genuinely surprising "you can't make this" story with a built-in narrative arc; no other shipped header uses a supply-chain layout, so it reads fresh.

### C. "Fragile by the time it reaches you" — the loss meter
- **Name** — What Survives.
- **The hook** — Even in the right foods, most of the B12 can be gone before you eat it — cooking strips ~30%, and pasteurized milk loses 40–90%.
- **Layout shape** — A compact two-figure comparison panel (two loss readouts side by side), then one short line naming the culprits (heat, acid, alkali, light). A quantitative comparison, not a cycle or beats.
- **Illustration** — Two simple "how much is left" bars/vials: one at −30% (cooking), one showing the 40–90% range (pasteurized/evaporated milk). Draining/remaining fill; numbers as the headline. No decorative clutter; the two figures ARE the illustration.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-DDDL-000045 (~30% lost in cooking; deactivated by acid/alkali/light/oxidizers), WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000219 (pasteurized & evaporated milk lost 40–90%; rich sources = liver, whole milk, eggs, cheese, muscle meats).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Two hard, concrete numbers make an abstract "water-soluble = fragile" fact visceral and actionable (favor fresh/whole foods). Distinct comparison layout.

### D. "The insulation strips away" — the demyelination mechanism
- **Name** — Losing the Insulation.
- **The hook** — B12 builds the myelin that insulates your nerves; without it the insulation is stripped and the nerves fail — Wallach's classic trio is spinal-cord demyelination, progressive neuropathy, and pernicious anemia.
- **Layout shape** — One richly-annotated cross-section figure occupying the block, with a short lede above and a single named "classic trio" callout beside/below it. A mechanism figure, not a card set.
- **Illustration** — A single nerve fibre shown in two states along its length: sheathed (healthy, B12 present) transitioning to bare/stripped (deficient). One fibre, one transition, three small labels for the trio. Keep the trio labels off the fibre line.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000040 (B12 supports myelin production; deficiency → brain/spinal cord/optic/peripheral demyelination, neuropathy), WAL-CLM-RARE-000238 (the "classic" trio: spinal cord demyelination, progressive neuropathy, pernicious anemia), WAL-CLM-RARE-000114 (required for myelin formation).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Turns the deficiency list into one clear "before/after" mechanism a reader feels; the myelin-insulation metaphor is intuitive and grounded. Distinct annotated-figure layout, different figure from any shipped header.

## 3. Proposed lede (PROPOSAL — Luneth ratifies, do not treat as final)

**Option 1 (identity-first):**
"A vitamin built around a single atom of cobalt — the metal that gives it its red colour — and, unusually, one the body cannot make for itself: microbes build it out of raw cobalt, and we require the finished molecule."
(Grounds: WAL-CLM-DDDL-000044, WAL-CLM-RARE-000114, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233.)

**Option 2 (mechanism-leaning):**
"The red, water-soluble vitamin with a cobalt atom at its heart — the one that partners with folate to build DNA and the myelin that insulates every nerve, which is why Wallach reads its shortage as pernicious anemia and nerves stripped bare."
(Grounds: WAL-CLM-DDDL-000044, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000040, WAL-CLM-RARE-000238.)

Both avoid restating any single concept's opening beat; Option 1 is the safer identity statement in the shipped voice.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

The daily target is a plain number, not a shared/mirrored budget: **400 mcg/day**, taken directly from the vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin) line of Wallach's Epigenetics daily-multiple supplement program (source_claim_id WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000117, 2014 — his newest book, so it wins on the favor-newest rule). The provenance is clean and un-transformed: original value 400 mcg, unit mcg, upper_taken 400 — no IU conversion and no body-weight scaling, because B12 is dosed in micrograms directly. It sits at the top of the 250–400 mcg range Wallach recommends elsewhere for a "respectable safety margin" (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084, WAL-CLM-RARE-000014) and well above his earlier 200 mcg "True Supplement Need" (WAL-CLM-LETS-000072). The number's whole point is the gap: the government RDA is only 3–4 mcg/day (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084, WAL-CLM-LETS-000072), so 400 mcg is roughly 100× the official figure — deliberately, for a wide safety margin and for pregnancy/nursing.

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Calibration risk on Concept A.** Magnesium's shipped header already uses "the metal at the centre of chlorophyll." A cobalt-at-centre red-crystal figure is a *different molecule and a different story* (colour + can't-make-it, not photosynthesis), but the design session should make the figure and framing visibly distinct so it doesn't read as a re-skin. If in doubt, lead with B or D.
- **No superseded/mismatch problems.** No claim in the pack carries `superseded_by`; the dose numbers are internally consistent (RDA 3–4, need 200–400, pharmacologic 1,000; the 400 target matches its verbatim). No claim_text over-states its own verbatim.
- **The protocol material is deep but repetitive.** ~14 protocols nearly all use "B12 1,000 mcg/day"; this is corpus breadth, not header material — do NOT try to fan it into beats. The two protocol facts worth a curio are the amblyopia/Bell's-palsy "20,000 mcg total → cure" figure (WAL-CLM-LETS-000132, WAL-CLM-LETS-000158) and the pregnancy/birth-defect prevention angle (WAL-CLM-LETS-000359).
- **Solo header is warranted.** With 37 claims — a strong identity, a unique symbiosis story, a clear mechanism, and two hard loss numbers — B12 easily supports its own bespoke header and should NOT fold into cobalt or the B-vitamin group.
- **Wished-for but absent:** the pack does not describe *intrinsic factor* or the exact biochemical shape of the corrin ring, so any molecule figure must stay at the "red crystal + central cobalt atom" level of detail the corpus actually supports — do not add real-world B12 structural detail that isn't in these claims.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**Concept B ("Made By Microbes" / the borrowed vitamin).** It carries the single most surprising, uniquely-Wallach idea in the pack — a nutrient you cannot make and must inherit from microbes building it out of soil cobalt — with a built-in narrative and a supply-chain layout unlike any shipped header, and it naturally absorbs the cobalt-identity fact without colliding with magnesium's "metal at the centre." Concept D (demyelination) is the strong runner-up if a mechanism-first header is preferred.
