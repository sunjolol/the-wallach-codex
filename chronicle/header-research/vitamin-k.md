# Vitamin K — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 10 sealed claims · vitamin (orange) · target: 300 mcg/day (upper of Wallach's 60–300 mcg Epigenetics range; a second book states 140 mcg).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — the clotting vitamin, isolated 1939.**
A fat-soluble vitamin (menaquinone), isolated in 1939, that the liver requires to produce prothrombin and at least five other clotting proteins — factors VII, IX, and X, plus proteins C and S — for proper blood clotting (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034). This is the richest single claim in the pack and carries the identity, the mechanism, and the deficiency list all at once.

**The standout mechanism — TWO jobs that sound unrelated: seals blood AND cements bone.**
The same vitamin the liver needs for clotting is also required "for the proper deposition of calcium in bones" (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034). The bone link runs through the protein osteocalcin — deficiency causes "osteocalcin deficiency" and "dysfunctional calcium absorption and deposition in the bones, osteoporosis with spontaneous fractures, and osteoarthritis" (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034; osteoporosis also WAL-CLM-LETS-000042, WAL-CLM-RARE-000232). This dual duty — stop the bleed, build the bone — is the wow that separates K from "just the clotting vitamin."

**The curio — you brew about HALF of it yourself, in your gut.**
"About half of the body's requirement is produced by probiotic bacteria in the colon" (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034). Reinforced clinically: Wallach's nosebleed protocol says to "reseed the colon with Lactobacillus acidophilus (the bacteria synthesize vitamin K)" (WAL-CLM-LETS-000382). A genuinely surprising fact — a vitamin you partly manufacture yourself.

**The deficiency signature — bleeding you can see.**
Bleeding/bruising under the skin is the readable first clue: "may indicate vitamin E or vitamin K deficiencies" (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000089). The fuller list: extended/prolonged clotting time (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034, WAL-CLM-LETS-000042, WAL-CLM-RARE-000232), hemorrhage (WAL-CLM-RARE-000232), ecchymoses (bruising), epistaxis (nosebleeds), hematuria, GI bleeding, liver disease, and osteoporosis from osteocalcin deficiency (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034). Wallach carefully separates skin bleeding from bleeding in the stool, which he calls a different and potentially serious sign (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000089).

**The signature protocols — green vegetables, gut, and the fat-soluble partners.**
- Ecchymosis (easy bruising): alfalfa tablets 4–6 twice daily, vitamin C to bowel tolerance, vitamin K at 15 mg/day, vitamin E at 800–1,200 IU/day, and 4–6 cups of green vegetables daily; very common in women and children on low-fiber/low-veg, fast-food diets (WAL-CLM-LETS-000261).
- Nosebleeds (epistaxis): more green leafy vegetables, reseed the colon with Lactobacillus acidophilus, vitamin C to bowel tolerance, calcium and magnesium at 2,000/1,000 mg/day, vitamin E 800–1,200 IU/day, alfalfa 5 three times daily, and the herb toad flax (Linaria vulgaris) (WAL-CLM-LETS-000382). Note Wallach's jab that orthodox ENT doctors instead cauterize the nasal capillaries.

**The historical curio — "Activator X," the lost butter factor.**
Via Weston Price: an "additional fat-soluble nutrient" labeled "Activator X" — now thought to be vitamin K2 — found in fish livers, shellfish, organ meats, and butter from cows grazing rapidly-growing green grass in spring and fall (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000163, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000176). "All primitive cultures had a source of Activator X" in their diets; those diets carried at least 10× the fat-soluble vitamins of the modern American diet, and the fat-soluble vitamins act as catalysts that let the body absorb minerals at all (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000176).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A. "Two jobs" — the seal-and-cement split
- **Name** — Two Jobs.
- **The hook** — One vitamin does two things that sound unrelated: it makes blood clot, and it puts calcium into bone.
- **Layout shape** — A clean two-panel split (left / right), one shared vitamin at the seam feeding both sides. No cascade diagram — two labeled destinations, not a flowchart.
- **Illustration** — LEFT panel: a drop/wound sealing (the clotting proteins — prothrombin + five factors). RIGHT panel: calcium settling into a bone lattice (osteocalcin). A single K glyph sits on the dividing seam, an arrow to each side. Two arrows, two destinations, nothing else.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034 (liver makes prothrombin + factors VII, IX, X, proteins C, S "for proper blood clotting and for the proper deposition of calcium in bones"; osteocalcin deficiency → osteoporosis). Bone/osteoporosis corroboration: WAL-CLM-LETS-000042, WAL-CLM-RARE-000232.
- **Why it wows / best UX** — It is the one fact that upgrades the reader's mental model from "the clotting vitamin" to something richer, and it is the best-anchored claim in the whole pack. Symmetry makes it instantly legible with zero clutter.

### B. "Half is homemade" — the gut co-op
- **Name** — Homemade Half.
- **The hook** — You don't only eat vitamin K; you manufacture roughly half of it yourself, in your own colon.
- **Layout shape** — A single divided-supply figure with a short curio caption — a "did you know" reveal, not beats. Big split as the whole visual.
- **Illustration** — One bar (or two-slice split) labeled ~half FROM DIET / ~half MADE BY YOUR GUT BACTERIA. One idea, two segments, a single label each. No pipes, no organs-diagram.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034 ("about half of the body's requirement is produced by probiotic bacteria in the colon"). WAL-CLM-LETS-000382 ("reseed the colon with Lactobacillus acidophilus… the bacteria synthesize vitamin K").
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Surprise factor: almost no reader expects a vitamin to be self-produced. It also quietly motivates the gut-health thread Wallach cares about (reseeding the colon). Risk: rests mostly on ONE claim's clause — strong but narrow (see Gaps).

### C. "The bruise that talks" — first visible sign
- **Name** — Read the Bruise.
- **The hook** — Unexplained easy bruising or bleeding under the skin can be a vitamin K shortage speaking.
- **Layout shape** — A single richly-annotated figure of skin/bruise with a tight caption and a short list of the other bleeding signs off to the side — deficiency-led, no big-number beat.
- **Illustration** — One bruise under skin, annotated with the readable signs radiating as short labels: extended clotting time, nosebleeds, hemorrhage. Route every label OFF the shape — no line through text (element-headers Rule 2).
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-DDDL-000089 ("Bleeding under the skin may indicate vitamin E or vitamin K deficiencies"); WAL-CLM-RARE-000232 (extended clotting time, hemorrhage, osteoporosis); WAL-CLM-LETS-000042 (poor clotting time); WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034 (ecchymoses, epistaxis, hematuria, GI bleeding). Honesty note: DDDL-000089 pairs K WITH vitamin E — the sign is not K-exclusive; the caption must say "K or E."
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Mirrors copper's "grey hair = first sign" archetype (do NOT reuse copper's figure), which shipped well. Concrete and body-anchored. Weakness: the copper header already owns first-visible-sign, so this risks reading as a re-skin of a shipped idea.

### D. "Activator X" — the lost butter factor
- **Name** — Activator X.
- **The hook** — The mysterious fat-soluble factor Weston Price found in every healthy traditional diet — now believed to be vitamin K2.
- **Layout shape** — A historical curio card: a short framed story with the reveal ("Activator X = vitamin K2") as the payoff, quote-forward. No 1-2-3 beats.
- **Illustration** — A single evocative object — a pat of butter (or a butter-churn) with a small spring-grass motif and an "X" tag that resolves to "K2." One object, one tag. Not a food grid.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000163 (butter from cows on rapidly-growing green spring/fall grass carries "Activator X," now thought to be K2); WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000176 ("All primitive cultures had a source of Activator X… now thought to be vitamin K"; fat-soluble vitamins as mineral-absorption catalysts; primitive diets 10× the modern American diet).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Pure story, very distinct in tone from A/B/C, matches the vitamin A header's "ancient-Egypt liver" curio energy. Caveat: both anchoring claims are `confidence: medium` and the K2 identification is hedged ("thought to be") — the copy must preserve that hedge, not assert K2 (see Gaps).

## 3. Proposed lede (PROPOSAL — Luneth ratifies)

1. "The clotting vitamin — the fat-soluble factor the liver needs to make prothrombin and five other blood-clotting proteins, and the same one that settles calcium into bone; you even brew about half of it yourself, in your gut." (identity + both mechanisms + curio; grounded in WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034)
2. "A fat-soluble vitamin best known for letting blood clot — yet Wallach ties a shortage as readily to fragile bones as to easy bruising, because the same vitamin that seals a wound directs calcium into the skeleton." (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034; osteoporosis link WAL-CLM-LETS-000042, WAL-CLM-RARE-000232)

Both avoid restating any single concept's opening beat verbatim; option 1 leans identity-broad, option 2 leans mechanism.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

The daily target is **300 mcg**. It comes straight from Wallach's Epigenetics daily-multiple program, which lists vitamin K1 or K2 at **60–300 mcg** (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000121); the derive takes the **upper bound**, 300 mcg. No unit conversion and no body-weight scaling apply here — the source is already stated in mcg per day, so the posted number is the range's high end unrounded. One honest wrinkle to surface: an earlier book, Let's Play Doctor (1995), lists a "True Supplement Need" of **140 mcg** (WAL-CLM-LETS-000076); the app favors the newest Wallach number (Epigenetics, 2014), which is why 300 mcg wins over 140 mcg. Separately, the 15 mg/day vitamin K in the bruising protocol (WAL-CLM-LETS-000261) is a short-term therapeutic dose, not the maintenance target — ~50× the daily number, and it should never be conflated with it.

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Two Wallach numbers, different eras.** 300 mcg (Epigenetics 2014, upper of 60–300) vs 140 mcg (Let's Play Doctor 1995). Neither is `superseded_by` the other in the pack, so the "favor newest" rule is doing the reconciling by convention, not by a sealed supersession link. Worth a glance from Luneth.
- **No claim_text/verbatim number mismatch found.** Every dose figure (60–300, 140, 15 mg) is present in its own verbatim. Clean.
- **Concept B rests on a single clause.** The "half is homemade" wow is anchored almost entirely on one sub-clause of WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034 (plus the LETS-000382 reseed line). Strong and quotable, but thin — a whole header on one clause is a design risk.
- **Concept C is not K-exclusive.** WAL-CLM-DDDL-000089 names vitamin E OR K for skin bleeding; copy must not present bruising as a K-only sign, and copper already owns the first-visible-sign archetype.
- **Concept D confidence is medium + hedged.** Both Activator-X claims are `confidence: medium` and say K2 is only "thought to be" the factor. Legitimate as a labeled curio; must keep the hedge.
- **Osteocalcin / osteoarthritis richness lives in one claim.** The bone mechanism detail (osteocalcin, spontaneous fractures, osteoarthritis) is all inside WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034; no second book corroborates the osteocalcin mechanism specifically (osteoporosis-as-outcome is corroborated by LETS-000042 and RARE-000232).
- **Solo header verdict: YES.** 10 claims spanning identity, a genuinely distinct dual mechanism, a strong curio (gut synthesis), a full deficiency signature, two real protocols, and a historical story is ample for a bespoke solo header. This does not need to fold into another element. Note K interacts with calcium, magnesium, vitamin C, and vitamin E (per the pack's `interacts_with`), and vitamin E is its literal deficiency-sign twin (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000089) — a natural cross-link, not a merge.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**A ("Two Jobs").** It is the best-anchored idea in the pack (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000034 carries it fully), it is the fact that most changes the reader's understanding of what vitamin K is, and its seal-a-wound / cement-a-bone symmetry renders as one clean, clutter-free figure. Concept B (gut synthesis) is the strongest secondary and could ride as a curio beat inside A.
