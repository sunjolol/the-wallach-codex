# Chloride — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 6 sealed claims (4 are near-duplicate restatements of one mechanism) · mineral (electrolyte) · target: 2,500 mg/day (True Supplement Need, Base Line program) · solo-header: **marginal** (one genuinely distinctive concept, thin supporting angles).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Angle A — Chloride is the raw material for stomach acid, and stomach acid gates all absorption (THE saturating theme — 4 of 6 claims say this).**
This is the same statement restated across four books; trust it as Wallach's core position on chloride, but note it is ONE idea, not four:
- WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000078 (definition, Immortality p125): chloride "is the basic raw material for our stomachs to make stomach acid (HCl)" — required to (1) activate protein digestion by pepsin, (2) absorb vitamin B12 (via intrinsic factor activation), (3) absorb minerals. Salt (NaCl) is "the universal source of chloride ions for all living things." Also: "activates numerous enzymes," "electrochemical and catalytic functions."
- WAL-CLM-RARE-000113 (mechanism, Rare Earths Ch11 p304): same chain — HCl for protein digestion (pepsin), B12 absorption (intrinsic factor), absorption of metallic minerals.
- WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000076 (mechanism, Epigenetics Ch18): same HCl mechanism, made by the gastric "chief" cells; adds that HCl "keeps the stomach sterile." Also recounts chlorine's public-health legacy — Labarraque's chlorinated lime, Semmelweis's 1847 chlorine hand-disinfection ending childbed fever, and the 1918 US drinking-water chlorination mandate.
- (The mechanism also appears folded into IMMORT-000078 above.)

**Angle B — Chloride is one of the three indispensable electrolytes (proportions).**
- WAL-CLM-RARE-000187 (definition, Rare Earths Ch11 p354): sodium, chloride, potassium are "three indispensable electrolytes." Body mineral-content share: **sodium 2%, potassium 5%, chloride 3%.** Sodium + chloride are mainly extracellular; potassium intracellular. Together they maintain water balance/distribution, osmotic equilibrium, acid-base balance, and muscular irritability. (`interacts_with`: potassium, sodium.)

**Angle C — Wallach's anti-low-salt stance (a punchy editorial quote).**
- WAL-CLM-RARE-000026 (definition, Rare Earths Ch2 p19): rejects "eat little or no salt." Verbatim: *"…the first food item a good husbandryman puts out for his livestock is a salt block!!"* — plus the "multibillion dollar a year snack food industry is well aware of your need and craving for salt."

**Angle D — The dose (target provenance).**
- WAL-CLM-LETS-000047 (dose, Let's Play Doctor Ch8 p73): Base Line Nutritional Supplement Program, adult. True Supplement Need = **2,500 mg/day**; 30-day pharmacologic dose = 500–2,500 mg. The quoted row is three columns: **RDA 1,700 mg | True Supplement Need 2,500 mg | pharmacologic 500–2,500 mg**. The 1,700 is the government RDA, reprinted only to argue against — never Wallach's recommendation.

## 2. Header concepts

### Concept 1 — "The acid maker" (LEAD)
- **The hook:** Chloride's job isn't to be a passive salt counter-ion — it's the raw material your stomach turns into hydrochloric acid, and that acid is the single key that unlocks three separate doors: protein digestion, vitamin B12, and mineral absorption. Wallach's framing: cut the chloride, and everything downstream stalls.
- **Layout shape:** A single left-to-right (or top-down) cascade with one input and three parallel outputs — deliberately NOT the eyebrow→beats→quote chassis. Input node → transformation node → a fan of three labeled "unlocked" outcomes. Optionally a slim caption strip beneath naming HCl's fourth role ("keeps the stomach sterile").
- **Illustration (one idea, few elements):** Salt (NaCl) → a single stomach/vessel labeled "HCl" → three doors/locks springing open, each labeled: PROTEIN (pepsin), B12 (intrinsic factor), MINERALS. Three outputs, one transformation, one input. No stroke crosses any label — the three outputs sit in their own lanes. Keep it to ~7 marks total.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000078, WAL-CLM-RARE-000113, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000076 (the "chief cells make HCl" + "keeps the stomach sterile" detail). All three carry the identical three-way unlock.
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Reframes a "boring" electrolyte as the gatekeeper of digestion. The three-door payoff is instantly legible and memorable, and it's the ONE thing Wallach says about chloride that a reader won't already assume. This is the concept that earns a solo header.

### Concept 2 — "Three electrolytes, by the numbers" (supporting)
- **The hook:** Chloride never travels alone — it's one of a trio (sodium, potassium, chloride) that together run water balance, acid-base, and muscle firing. Chloride is 3% of your body's total minerals.
- **Layout shape:** A compact three-band proportion figure (Na 2% · K 5% · Cl 3%) with an extracellular/intracellular split annotation.
- **Illustration:** Three proportion bars or a single stacked bar, chloride highlighted, with a two-zone tag (Na+Cl outside the cell, K inside).
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-RARE-000187 (2% / 5% / 3%; extracellular vs intracellular; water balance, osmotic equilibrium, acid-base, muscular irritability).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Gives honest context and a real number, but it's the more generic "electrolyte" story a reader half-expects. Better as a supporting band beneath Concept 1 than as the whole header.

### Concept 3 — "The salt block" (supporting / pull-quote)
- **The hook:** Wallach's blunt rebuttal to "eat less salt" — livestock get a salt block for a reason.
- **Layout shape:** A single strong pull quote, minimal chrome.
- **Illustration:** None, or a single spare salt-block glyph. The quote carries it.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-RARE-000026 (the salt-block quote, verbatim).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Memorable voice, but it's really about SALT/sodium craving, not chloride's mechanism — thematically adjacent, not central. Best used as a short quote accent, not the header's spine.

## 5. Gaps / flags + SOLO-vs-GROUP verdict

**Thinness — honest read.** The pack is 6 claims but only ~4 DISTINCT ideas: (A) the HCl-gatekeeper mechanism is stated four times across four books with essentially the same words — it is one concept, not four. (B) the electrolyte-trio proportions, (C) the anti-salt stance, (D) the dose. There are **no deficiency signs, no conditions treated, and no symptoms** in the pack (`deficiency_signs: []`, `conditions_treated: []`) — so unlike selenium or copper there is no "shortage shows up as X" story to build a figure around. The material is mechanism-and-proportion only.

**Flags:**
- claim_text on IMMORT-000078 / RARE-000113 says slightly more than each terse verbatim (e.g. verbatim RARE-000113 is truncated mid-sentence at "pepsin"); the fuller three-way unlock is corroborated by IMMORT-000078's fuller verbatim, so it's safe — but quote the FULLER verbatim (IMMORT-000078) if a pull quote is wanted, not the truncated RARE one.
- EPIGEN-000076's public-health legacy (Semmelweis, 1918 chlorination) is a tempting "wow" detail but it's about elemental chlorine as a DISINFECTANT, not dietary chloride — do NOT let it imply Wallach recommends drinking chlorine. Use it only as a labeled aside if at all.
- Concept 3's quote is really a SODIUM/salt-craving point; it belongs as much to the sodium header. Don't double-spend it as chloride's centerpiece.

**Verdict: MARGINAL solo header — defensible, but only because Concept 1 is genuinely distinctive.** Chloride clears the bar for a solo bespoke header ONLY on the strength of the "acid maker" three-door mechanism, which is a real, non-obvious, Wallach-specific reframing. Everything else (electrolyte %, salt stance) is supporting texture, not a second pillar. If a solo header is built, keep it TIGHT — one strong mechanism figure (Concept 1), a small proportion band (Concept 2), and at most a one-line salt-stance accent. Do not stretch this to four fanned concepts; there is only one.

**If NOT solo:** the natural group is the **electrolyte trio (sodium · chloride · potassium)** — WAL-CLM-RARE-000187 explicitly bundles them ("so intimately associated in the body that they can be presented together"), and `interacts_with` lists both. A shared "electrolytes" concept page with each element's mechanism as a panel would be honest and well-grounded, and would spare chloride from carrying a solo page on a single repeated sentence.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**Concept 1 — "The acid maker."** One line why: it's the only thing in the pack that's distinctive to chloride (the HCl three-door unlock), it's stated in four books so it's unambiguously Wallach's core position, and it turns a forgettable electrolyte into a gatekeeper story a reader remembers. If Luneth judges even that too thin, fold chloride into a **sodium·chloride·potassium electrolyte group** (grounded directly in WAL-CLM-RARE-000187).
