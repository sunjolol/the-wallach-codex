# Boron — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 12 sealed claims · mineral · target: 9.2 mg/day (derived from a 1–6 mg per-100-lb Wallach range).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — the bone-and-hormone mineral.**
Wallach defines boron as essential for bone metabolism: it drives the *efficient use of calcium and magnesium* and keeps the endocrine glands — ovaries, testes and adrenals — working properly (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000042; the same statement, near-verbatim, at WAL-CLM-RARE-000098). It is one of the 60 minerals he counts as essential.

**The curio — science's 1990 latecomer.**
Mainstream science accepted boron as an essential nutrient *for humans* only in **1990** — after it had already been shown essential for growing chicks about a decade earlier (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000042; the "essential-1990" fact also tagged on WAL-CLM-RARE-000098). A clean did-you-know: the body needed it all along; the establishment admitted it last.

**The standout mechanism — the 8-day flip.**
Wallach's most striking boron claim is a supplementation result that lands *within eight days*: women lost **40% less calcium**, **33% less magnesium**, and less phosphorus through their urine; blood **estradiol-17B doubled** to "levels found in women on estrogen replacement therapy"; and **testosterone doubled in both men and women** (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000043; the mineral-retention half restated at WAL-CLM-RARE-000099). This is the wow: a few milligrams both plug the mineral leak and double the sex hormones, fast. It also frames boron as bone-density protector + hormone balancer.

**The deficiency map — one cofactor under four diseases.**
Boron recurs as a *complicating cofactor deficiency* beneath Wallach's calcium-deficiency disease family:
- **Osteoporosis** — root cause chronic dietary calcium shortfall, worsened by co-factor deficiencies of magnesium, **boron**, copper and sulfur, plus excess dietary phosphorus (WAL-CLM-RARE-000275).
- **Arthritis** — listed among the calcium-deficiency diseases with magnesium, **boron**, copper and sulfur as complicating deficiencies (WAL-CLM-RARE-000303).
- **Hypertension** — calcium deficiency as the primary cause (McCarron 1980 + 30+ later studies), with magnesium and **boron** as complicating-factor deficiencies (WAL-CLM-RARE-000304).
- **Bone spurs / heel spurs / calcium deposits** — read as calcium *deficiency*, not excess; reversible with chelated/colloidal calcium *plus magnesium and boron as its co-factors* (WAL-CLM-RARE-000305).

**Signature protocols.**
- Osteoporosis: betaine HCl + pancreatic enzymes 75–200 mg t.i.d. before meals, calcium + magnesium 2,000 mg / 1,000 mg per day, and *plant-derived colloidal minerals that include calcium, magnesium and boron* (WAL-CLM-LETS-000391).
- Arthritis drug replacement: the mineral swap for older patients is Ca, Mg, **B**, Cu, Se, Li + chondroitin sulfate (Knox gelatin) (WAL-CLM-RARE-000279).

**The anecdote — Ma Lan's bone broth.**
On fishing dates, Ma Lan simmered the bones of fish, chicken, pork and beef in a **vinegar solution**, which leaches calcium, magnesium, manganese, **boron**, copper and sulfur out of the bones into the soup — how a dairy-free culture still gets its minerals (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000309). A warm human curio, but boron is one name in a list here, not the subject.

**The dose.**
Epigenetics daily mineral program: boron **1–6 mg per 100 lb** of body weight (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000127).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A — "Eight Days" (before/after two-state)
- **The hook** — A few milligrams of boron, and in eight days your body stops leaking minerals and your hormones double.
- **Layout shape** — A two-state split down the middle: DAY 0 (baseline) on the left, DAY 8 (on boron) on the right, with the four measured shifts crossing the divide. Not beats — a paired comparison read left-to-right.
- **Illustration** — Two slim vertical bar-pairs, one labelled block per shift: calcium loss ↓40%, magnesium loss ↓33%, estradiol ×2, testosterone ×2. Each is a single before/after pair; the "×2" and "↓40%" sit *beside* the bars, never a stroke through a label.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000043 ("Within eight days… 40% less calcium, 33% less magnesium… estradiol 17B doubled… testosterone also double in both men and women"); retention half corroborated at WAL-CLM-RARE-000099.
- **Why it wows / best UX** — This is boron's single most surprising, most concrete claim, and it has *four* clean numbers plus a time-box ("eight days") that make it feel measured, not mystical. Highest wow density in the whole pack.

### B — "The Retainer" (single annotated figure)
- **The hook** — Boron is what makes calcium and magnesium actually stay; without it they wash out.
- **Layout shape** — One central figure with a short caption beneath and the daily-target tip; almost no prose. The figure *is* the argument.
- **Illustration** — A single simplified bone (or a bone cross-section) holding two labelled mineral tokens (Ca, Mg) inside; boron shown as the seal/clasp at the rim that keeps them in. One idea, ~4 elements — no leak arrows crossing text; put "boron" on the clasp and the retained-mineral figures (40% less Ca, 33% less Mg lost) as two side captions.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000042 / WAL-CLM-RARE-000098 (boron → efficient use of calcium & magnesium, bone metabolism); WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000043 / WAL-CLM-RARE-000099 (the 40% / 33% retention figures).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Turns an abstract "cofactor" role into a physical picture a skim-reader keeps: the mineral that holds the other minerals in the bone. Distinct from A (mechanism-as-picture, not mechanism-as-timeline).

### C — "Admitted in 1990" (did-you-know curio)
- **The hook** — Your body always needed boron; science only admitted it was essential in 1990.
- **Layout shape** — A curio-led header: a single dated line/marker as the centrepiece, with a one-sentence "what it actually does" pay-off underneath. Quiet, editorial — closest in spirit to the vitamin-A Egypt curio, but its own figure.
- **Illustration** — A minimal single-axis marker: "chicks ~1980 → humans 1990" — two dated dots on one short line, boron's essential-status arriving late. Two points, one line, labels off the line. Nothing else.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000042 ("accepted as an essential nutrient for humans only in 1990… shown essential for growing chicks about a decade earlier"); essential-1990 fact also on WAL-CLM-RARE-000098; the "what it does" pay-off from the endocrine/bone role in the same claims.
- **Why it wows / best UX** — A genuine surprise fact that reframes boron from "obscure trace mineral" to "overlooked essential," and it's honest — the date is Wallach's, not ours. Lightest-touch of the four; good if Luneth wants a calm, curio-forward page.

### D — "One Mineral, Four Diseases" (deficiency / where-it-appears map)
- **The hook** — The same quiet boron shortage sits under osteoporosis, arthritis, high blood pressure and bone spurs.
- **Layout shape** — A four-cell field (2×2 grid), each cell a named condition with a one-line role for boron; boron named once as the shared spine (a header band above the grid, not a hub with spokes — avoids convergence-line clutter).
- **Illustration** — Restrained: four labelled condition tiles under a single "boron" band. The figure is typographic/tiled, not a wired diagram. No line routed through any condition label.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-RARE-000275 (osteoporosis), WAL-CLM-RARE-000303 (arthritis), WAL-CLM-RARE-000304 (hypertension), WAL-CLM-RARE-000305 (bone spurs) — boron named as complicating cofactor in all four; supported by protocols WAL-CLM-LETS-000391 and WAL-CLM-RARE-000279.
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Shows breadth: boron isn't a one-trick mineral, it's a recurring cofactor Wallach names across a disease family. Best if the goal is "why this mineral matters more than you'd think." Risk: four conditions is closer to the rejected many-station clutter than A/B/C — keep it strictly tiled, few elements.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

Boron's daily target of **9.2 mg** derives from the Epigenetics mineral program (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000127), which states **1–6 mg per 100 lb of body weight**. The derivation takes the **upper** of the range (6.0 mg), scales it from the per-100-lb basis to the project's **154 lb (70 kg) reference body** — a ×1.54 factor — and rounds to two significant figures: 6.0 × 1.54 = 9.24 → **9.2 mg/day**. No unit conversion is involved (the source is already in mg); the only transforms are upper-of-range and the body-weight scale. This is a plain Wallach-sourced number, not a gap or a collective budget.

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **No `deficiency_signs` array populated.** The pack lists conditions (osteoporosis, arthritis, hypertension, bone spurs) but `deficiency_signs: []` — there is **no first-visible-sign** claim for boron the way copper has premature grey hair. Do NOT invent one. A "first sign" concept is unsupported; that archetype is off the table for boron.
- **Boron is rarely the sole subject.** In four of the deficiency/anecdote claims (RARE-000275/303/304/305, DDDL-000309) boron appears *inside a list* of cofactor minerals, not as the headline. The two claims that make boron the true subject are WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000042 and WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000043 (both page 103) — lean on these for identity/mechanism; the deficiency-map (Concept D) is real but "shared billing."
- **Two near-duplicate pairs.** IMMORT-000042 ≈ RARE-000098 (identical bone-metabolism/endocrine definition); IMMORT-000043 ≈ RARE-000099 (the 8-day finding — but note the Immortality version is fuller: it carries the estradiol *and* testosterone doubling, while RARE-000099 verbatim covers only the mineral-retention half). **For the hormone-doubling numbers, cite IMMORT-000043** (its verbatim actually contains them); RARE-000099's claim_text mentions testosterone but its verbatim does not — a mild claim_text-over-verbatim reach, so prefer IMMORT-000043 as the anchor.
- **No superseded claims** in the pack; no numeric mismatches on the dose (EPIGEN-000127 verbatim shows "Boron 1 - 6 mg", matching provenance).
- **Solo header: YES, justified.** Boron has a genuine standout (the 8-day flip) plus a real identity and a clean curio — enough for a bespoke header. It does NOT need to fold into calcium/magnesium, though it is *tightly* coupled to them (its whole job is helping them work); a designer could nod to that pairing without ceding the page.
- **Wished-for but absent:** a mechanism for *how* boron retains the minerals or raises the hormones — Wallach states the result, not the pathway. Keep concepts result-focused; don't imply a mechanism the corpus doesn't give.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**Concept A ("Eight Days").** It carries boron's single most surprising, most concrete claim — four measured numbers inside an eight-day window — anchored cleanly in WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000043. It's the highest-wow, most defensible, and most visually distinct from the six shipped headers.
