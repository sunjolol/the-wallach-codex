# Iron — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 36 sealed claims · mineral (Fe) · target: 46 mg/day (Epigenetics 15–30 mg per 100 lb, upper × 1.54 → 46; corroborated by Let's Play Doctor's 45 mg "True Supplement Need").

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity / what iron IS.**
Iron is an essential nutrient for all land ("terrestrial") animals, first recognised as essential by Boussingault in the 1860s (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000126, WAL-CLM-RARE-000130). A healthy adult carries **3 to 5 grams** of it; a newborn holds nearly double that per kg (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000128, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083, WAL-CLM-RARE-000130). Of that, **60–70% is "functional" iron** doing active work (hemoglobin, the muscle oxygen-store myoglobin, respiratory enzymes) and **30–40% is storage** (ferritin and hemosiderin in bone marrow and liver) (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000128, WAL-CLM-RARE-000130). Wallach's own analogy, repeated across three books: **"iron is to hemoglobin what magnesium is to chlorophyll"** (WAL-CLM-RARE-000130, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000085) — the metal at the centre of the pigment. *(Overlap flag: the shipped magnesium header already owns the chlorophyll image — see §5.)*

**The standout mechanism — iron is the oxygen seat.**
Each hemoglobin molecule is built around **four iron atoms**; oxygen chemically **bonds to those iron atoms** in the lung capillaries and rides the circulation to distant cells (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000126). The vivid twist: **carbon monoxide binds that same hemoglobin iron 200 times more tightly than oxygen**, crowding oxygen off its seat — which is exactly what carbon-monoxide poisoning is (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000127). Iron's listed functions: enzyme/metalloenzyme cofactor, respiratory pigment (hemoglobin), and electron transfer for oxygen utilisation (WAL-CLM-RARE-000130).

**Absorption — heme vs plant, and why the RDA falls short.**
**Heme iron from meat is 10% absorbable; iron from fresh plants only 1%**, because phytates bind it (WAL-CLM-RARE-000131, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083). Stomach hydrochloric acid is required for optimal absorption; **vitamin C increases it; clays and phytates decrease it** (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000021, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000132). Absorption happens mainly in the duodenum while the environment is still acid (WAL-CLM-RARE-000131). Because of this, Wallach argues the official **RDA of 18 mg/day is too low** for vegans/vegetarians on high-fibre, high-phytate diets (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000132, WAL-CLM-RARE-000131). He frames the general rule as the gap between a mineral's *absolute* and *dietary* requirement: **iodine ≈ 1:1, iron 1:10, chromium 1:100** in metallic form (WAL-CLM-RARE-000074). Nickel facilitates GI absorption of iron and zinc (WAL-CLM-RARE-000192).

**The signature deficiency sign — PICA.**
Wallach's most distinctive iron claim: **"pica" is a *specific* sign of iron deficiency** (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000019, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000129). The craving drives children and adults to eat **ice (pagophagia), dirt (geophagia), lead paint**, and (in his broader framing) excess snack food ("munchies," → obesity), alcohol, and sugar ("sweet tooth") (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000129, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083). The hard proof: a controlled trial of **25 children who ate sand — 11 of 13 given iron lost the pica within 3–4 months vs only 3 of 12 given saline** (WAL-CLM-RARE-000037).

**Broader deficiency picture & conditions.**
The full deficiency list: microcytic anemia, angular stomatitis, anorexia, brittle nails, confusion, constipation, depression, dizziness, dysphagia, fatigue, fragile bones, GI upset, growth retardation, headaches, pica, irritability, palpitations (WAL-CLM-LETS-000014). Symptom cluster: listlessness/fatigue, heart palpitations on exertion, reduced cognition & memory, sore tongue, hypochromic microcytic anemia (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000020, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000131). Causes: unsupplemented pregnancy, menstruation/dysmenorrhea, chronic infection, low stomach acid (hypochlorhydria), chronic diarrhea, chronic bleeding, impaired absorption (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000130).

**Wallach's contrarian EXCESS claim (a signature reframe).**
Excess iron is linked to cirrhosis of the liver, pancreatic fibrosis, diabetes, and heart failure — **but Wallach insists these are NOT direct iron toxicity**. The surplus iron *raises the body's need for selenium, copper, zinc (and chromium, vanadium)*, and it is that **driven deficiency** of the other minerals that does the harm (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000022, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000133, WAL-CLM-LETS-000015). He also notes excess iron *itself can be caused by* a shortage of selenium/copper/zinc (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083).

**Diagnostic patterns (hair & nails).**
Hair-analysis disease patterns: schizophrenia = high Ca / **low iron** / high copper; arthritis = high lead/Ca/P, **low iron and copper**; anemia = **low iron**, copper, selenium, cobalt (WAL-CLM-RARE-000081). Ideal hair-mineral ratio **Fe/Cu 2.5:1** = a barometer of hematology, energy production, cellular respiration (WAL-CLM-RARE-000083). Fingernail reading: **ridges = iron and/or calcium deficiency** (white spots = zinc, brittle = sulfur amino acids) (WAL-CLM-LETS-000276).

**The geology / history curio.**
Iron is the **4th most abundant element in the earth's crust and 2nd most abundant metal after aluminum**; it colours most soil; the planet's molten **core is theorised to be magnetized iron**; smelting it opened the **Iron Age (~1100 B.C.)**; today iron is ~90% of all metal extracted from ore (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000125).

**Dose numbers (all consistent, no supersession).**
Maintenance target 46 mg/day derives from Epigenetics **15–30 mg per 100 lb** (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000132). Let's Play Doctor's Base-Line Program lists **RDA 18 / True Supplement Need 45 / pharmacologic 50–100 mg** — the 18 is government RDA he reprints only to argue against, never his rec (WAL-CLM-LETS-000055). Protocol doses seen: canker sores 15 mg (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000080, WAL-CLM-LETS-000143), anemia 20 mg (WAL-CLM-LETS-000137), depression 45 mg (WAL-CLM-LETS-000244), menstruation 25–50 mg (WAL-CLM-LETS-000357), nervous heart 45–100 mg (WAL-CLM-LETS-000377).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### Concept A — "The seat oxygen rides" (single mechanism figure, with the CO punch)
- **Hook:** Your blood carries oxygen by clipping it onto iron — and that one fact is also why carbon monoxide kills.
- **Layout shape:** Figure-forward. A single central illustration carries almost the whole block; one short "why it matters" line beneath. No beats, no 1-2-3.
- **Illustration:** One hemoglobin unit drawn as **four iron "seats,"** an O₂ molecule clicking onto a seat in the lung; a second small state shows **carbon monoxide clamped on the same seat**, labelled "**200× tighter**," with oxygen shut out. One idea (the seat), two states. No stroke through any label.
- **Anchored by:** four iron atoms per hemoglobin, oxygen bonds in lung capillaries → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000126; CO binds the same iron **200×** tighter → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000127; body content 3–5 g → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000128.
- **Why it wows / best UX:** The clearest, most universal "how does it actually work" payoff in the pack, and the CO twist turns a dry mechanism into a memorable danger — a reader leaves understanding *why* iron matters, not just *that* it does.

### Concept B — "The body screaming for iron" (specific-sign card → clinical proof)
- **Hook:** Iron lack produces one bizarre, unmistakable symptom — an urge to eat things that aren't food.
- **Layout shape:** A first-visible-sign reveal (like copper's grey hair) that **lands on a hard trial number** rather than a quote. Sign → "the proof."
- **Illustration:** A tight row of the three canonical craved non-foods — **an ice cube, a clod of dirt, a paint chip** — each labelled once; the payoff stat sits beside them: **"11 of 13 children lost the craving on iron — 3 of 12 on salt."** Fewest possible elements; the number *is* the climax.
- **Anchored by:** pica = *specific* sign of iron deficiency, ice/dirt/lead-paint → WAL-CLM-DDDL-000019, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000129; the 25-children sand trial, **11/13 vs 3/12** → WAL-CLM-RARE-000037.
- **Why it wows / best UX:** The single most surprising, most Wallach-distinctive iron fact, and it comes with a controlled-trial number almost nothing else in the corpus offers — a "specific sign" as strong as copper's premature grey hair, but with harder evidence.

### Concept C — "The metal that built us" (geology/history curio → your blood)
- **Hook:** The same metal running through your blood forged civilization and sits at the earth's core.
- **Layout shape:** A did-you-know curio opener that arcs from **planet → history → you** in one move; a short bridge line connects earth-iron to blood-iron.
- **Illustration:** A simple **earth cross-section** (crust → magnetized molten core) with three quiet annotations — "4th most abundant element," "core: magnetized iron," "named an Age (~1100 B.C.)" — resolving to a single line: the same 3–5 g inside you. One figure, no clutter.
- **Anchored by:** 4th most abundant element / 2nd most abundant metal / magnetized molten core / Iron Age ~1100 B.C. / ~90% of extracted metal → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000125; the 3–5 g in the body for the human tie-back → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000128.
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Pure curiosity hook with a satisfying scale-shift (planet to bloodstream); tonally distinct from every biology-driven header shipped so far, so it broadens the collection's range.

### Concept D — "It was never the iron" (myth-vs-mechanism reframe)
- **Hook:** Too much iron really does cause cirrhosis, diabetes and heart failure — but Wallach says iron isn't the poison; it's starving you of other minerals.
- **Layout shape:** A **blamed-vs-actual reveal**: the conventional charge on one side, struck through; Wallach's mechanism on the other. A reframe, not a list.
- **Illustration:** A two-lane diagram — lane 1 "**Blamed: iron is toxic**" (the four diseases) crossed out; lane 2 "**Actual: surplus iron burns through selenium · copper · zinc**" → the *driven deficiency* does the damage. One arrow of causation, clearly labelled, routed around every word.
- **Anchored by:** excess iron → cirrhosis / pancreatic fibrosis / diabetes / heart failure, *not* direct toxicity but an induced need for Se/Cu/Zn (+Cr, V) → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000133, WAL-CLM-DDDL-000022, WAL-CLM-LETS-000015.
- **Why it wows / best UX:** The most *characteristically Wallach* idea in the pack — a contrarian reframe of a mainstream fear — and it teaches his whole "minerals are a balance, not isolated doses" worldview in one figure.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

The 46 mg/day target comes from Wallach's Epigenetics daily mineral program, which recommends **15–30 mg of iron per 100 lb of body weight** (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000132). We take the **upper end (30 mg)**, scale from the 100 lb basis to a 154 lb / 70 kg reference adult (**× 1.54**), and round to two significant figures: 30 × 1.54 = 46.2 → **46 mg**. This lines up with his Let's Play Doctor Base-Line Program, which lists a **45 mg "True Supplement Need"** for adults (WAL-CLM-LETS-000055) — the same ballpark by a different route. Note both sit far above the government **RDA of 18 mg**, which Wallach reprints only to argue *against*: he holds it is too low for anyone on a high-fibre, high-phytate diet where absorption is poor (WAL-CLM-LETS-000055, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000132).

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Solo header: strongly warranted.** Iron carries 36 claims across 5 books with a rich mechanism (oxygen seat / CO), a signature specific sign with a controlled trial (pica), a contrarian excess reframe, and a geology curio. This is one of the deepest palettes among the minerals — it merits a bespoke SOLO header, not a fold-in.
- **Chlorophyll-parallel overlap.** Wallach's "iron is to hemoglobin what magnesium is to chlorophyll" (WAL-CLM-RARE-000130, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000085) is authentic and repeated — but the **shipped magnesium header already owns the chlorophyll image** ("the metal at the centre of chlorophyll"). Re-using it for iron risks reading as a repeat across the collection. Keep it as an optional callback (Candidate-2 lede), not a figure.
- **EPIGEN-000083 claim_text vs verbatim mismatch (watch item).** Claim WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083 is a very large synthesis (3–5 g, heme 10% / plant 1%, causes, symptoms, excess), but its **own verbatim is only a short deficiency-sign fragment** ("tongue, angular stomatitis, dysphagia (pica), hypochromic microcytic anemia, weight gain, obesity, etc."). Do NOT anchor the big numbers to this claim's verbatim — every one of them has a dedicated, properly-quoted home claim (3–5 g → IMMORT-000128; heme/plant → RARE-000131; causes → IMMORT-000130). Cite those.
- **Peripheral-mention claims — don't build on them for iron.** WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000085 is a B12/cobalt claim (iron appears only in the porphyrin-ring analogy); WAL-CLM-RARE-000192 is a nickel claim; WAL-CLM-DDDL-000040 is a zinc claim (iron as a zinc *blocker*). Usable as absorption/interaction colour, not as primary anchors.
- **No supersession, no dose conflict.** No claim carries `superseded_by`. The two maintenance figures (46 mg derived vs 45 mg stated) are consistent, not competing.
- **Absorption two-lane (heme 10% vs plant 1%) is a strong 5th concept I left out.** WAL-CLM-RARE-000131 / WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000083 would make a clean two-lane comparison figure. If Luneth wants a swap, it is the natural replacement for Concept C — flagged so it isn't re-derived.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**Concept B ("The body screaming for iron" — pica).** It is the single most surprising, most Wallach-distinctive iron fact and the only one backed by a hard controlled-trial number (11/13 vs 3/12), giving it the same first-visible-sign power that carried copper's grey-hair header but with stronger evidence. Close second is **Concept A (the oxygen seat + CO)** — pick it instead if collection-level variety matters, since copper (grey hair) and zinc (nail spots) already spend two "deficiency-sign" figures and A is a fresh mechanism angle.
