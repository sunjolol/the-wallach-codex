# Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 22 sealed claims · vitamin (orange) · target: 100 mg/day (upper of Wallach's 10–100 mg daily-multiple range, Epigenetics 2014).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — the energy cofactor.**
Wallach defines B1 (thiamine) as a cofactor for energy production and the metabolism of carbohydrates, protein, and fat (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000035). Its deficiency "especially disturbs carbohydrate metabolism in the brain (dementia) and heart (congestive heart failure)" — that single line is the spine of everything else in the pack. It is a water-soluble B-vitamin (tagged `water-soluble-vitamins`, EPIGEN-000035).

**The standout mechanism — one shortage, two opposite organs (heart ↔ mind).**
This is the signature angle and it is uniquely B1. WAL-CLM-RARE-000233 says the deficiency "reaches from the heart to the mind": congestive heart failure (beriberi) at one pole; memory loss (Wernicke-Korsakoff), mental confusion, depression, lethargy, muscular weakness, paralysis, emotional instability, loss of appetite at the other. It underlies Wallach's repeated point that "'senility' and heart failure can have a simple vitamin cause" (RARE-000233; mechanism source EPIGEN-000035: brain→dementia, heart→CHF).

**Beriberi — the classic deficiency disease + the man-made-shortage curio.**
WAL-CLM-DDDL-000043: beriberi is "the classic thiamine deficiency disease," a common disease of Asian rice cultures; the name is Sinhalese for "I cannot." It brings weakness in the legs, hands and arms together with congestive heart failure, with death from pulmonary edema and abdominal "dropsy." Critically: it appeared "where the diet was polished white rice with its thiamine-bearing bran removed" — the deficiency is created by refining away the layer that holds the vitamin. Wallach also calls B-1 "the beri-beri vitamin" (WAL-CLM-LETS-000377).

**The most striking deficiency signs.**
Wallach's tables give an unusually broad and specific list:
- WAL-CLM-LETS-000034 (Let's Play Doctor, Table 6-1) — 19 signs: anorexia, brain atrophy (senility), confusion, constipation, coordination impairment, depression, dyspnea, GI upset, edema, fatigue, irritability, memory loss, muscle atrophy, nervousness, numbness of hands and feet, pain hypersensitivity, palpitations, sonophobia (sound sensitivity), weakness.
- WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000035 — anxiety, hysteria, confusion, nausea, depression, mental confusion, loss of appetite, muscular weakness, fibromyalgia, beriberi (muscle wasting, CHF, Korsakoff/dementia, Wernicke-Korsakoff/dementia + MS), paralysis, peripheral neuropathies.
- WAL-CLM-RARE-000233 — CHF (beriberi), memory loss (Wernicke-Korsakoff), mental confusion, depression, lethargy, muscular weakness, paralysis, emotional instability, loss of appetite.
Two of the most vivid/unusual single signs to feature: **sonophobia** (sound sensitivity, LETS-000034) and **pain hypersensitivity** (LETS-000034).

**Conditions Wallach ties to it.**
Beriberi, congestive heart failure, Korsakoff's syndrome, Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, dementia, Alzheimer's, depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, fibromyalgia, cataracts, cardiac arrhythmia, nervous heart, palpitations, tachycardia, parkinsonism, anorexia, edema (from `conditions_treated` + the mapped protocol claims). Note the heart cluster (nervous heart / palpitations / tachycardia / arrhythmia) and the mind cluster (dementia / Korsakoff / Alzheimer's / depression) — the same two poles again.

**Signature protocols / anecdotes.**
- **Reversible dementia (the boldest claim).** WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000014: Korsakoff's is "the second of four dementias," within the beriberi family, and *can be reversed* — eliminate sugar, fried foods, processed meats, oils and gluten, plus a program of all 90 essential nutrients "with a special emphasis on the supplementation of thiamine."
- **Korsakoff's = "false Alzheimer's."** WAL-CLM-LETS-000333: an inability to record new memory; the patient still performs tasks learned before onset but can't learn the simplest new one; confabulation (inventing experiences to fill gaps) is characteristic. Cause: blow to the head (good recovery) OR chronic alcoholism + B-1 deficiency (harder). Treatment leads with B-1 100 mg t.i.d.
- **Nervous heart of seniors.** WAL-CLM-LETS-000377: many senior cardiac symptoms "are the result of B-1 (Beri-Beri) deficiency," anemia and low stomach acid; treat with B-1 100 mg t.i.d.
- B1 recurs as a named ingredient across the heart/mind protocols at therapeutic doses: dementia B-1 100 mg t.i.d. (LETS-000243), Alzheimer's 200 mg t.i.d. (LETS-000130), tachycardia/palpitations/nervous heart 100 mg t.i.d. (LETS-000443, -000394, -000377), parkinsonism 200 mg t.i.d. (LETS-000396), cataracts 50 mg b.i.d. (LETS-000207), plus anxiety, bipolar, depression, cardiac arrhythmia (LETS-000142, -000159, -000244, -000203).

**The curio — B1 repels mosquitoes.**
WAL-CLM-LETS-000370: "vitamins such as B-1 at 500 mg/day will also 'repel' mosquitoes." A genuinely surprising did-you-know, the B1 equivalent of vitamin A's ancient-Egypt-liver curio.

**Dose facts (see §4).** Epigenetics daily multiple: 10–100 mg (EPIGEN-000112). Let's Play Doctor Base-Line: True Supplement Need 50 mg, 30-day pharmacologic 200–500 mg (LETS-000068).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A. "Heart to the Mind" — the two-pole reach
- **Name:** Heart ↔ Mind.
- **The hook:** One vitamin shortage strikes the two organs you'd never connect — the failing heart and the failing memory are the *same* deficiency.
- **Layout shape:** A vertical two-pole composition. A short framing line at the top ("its deficiency reaches from the heart to the mind"), then two anchored poles — HEART and MIND — each with its named failures, joined by a single thiamine thread down the centre. No 1-2-3 beats, no big-number, no pull-quote chassis.
- **Illustration:** One simple figure: a lean silhouette (or just two nodes on a vertical spine) with a heart glyph at the lower pole and a head/brain glyph at the upper pole, linked by a single line labelled *thiamine*. Lower pole tag: "congestive heart failure (beriberi)"; upper pole tag: "dementia · memory · confusion." Fewest elements; keep the connecting line clear of both text labels (route it beside, not through).
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000035 (cofactor; deficiency disturbs carb metabolism "in the brain (dementia) and heart (congestive heart failure)"); WAL-CLM-RARE-000233 ("from the heart to the mind"; CHF at one end, Wernicke-Korsakoff/confusion/paralysis at the other; "'senility' and heart failure can have a simple vitamin cause").
- **Why it wows / best UX:** It is the most information-dense true idea in the pack and it is *only* B1 — no other shipped element spans two organs like this. A skim-reader leaves knowing the single memorable thing Wallach says about thiamine.

### B. "I cannot" — the polished-rice origin story
- **Name:** The refined-away vitamin.
- **The hook:** Beriberi is a *man-made* disease — polishing rice strips off the exact layer that carries the vitamin.
- **Layout shape:** A curio/etymology panel: an etymology callout ("beriberi — Sinhalese for 'I cannot'") beside a small annotated figure, with one line of consequence beneath. Historical, narrative — a different register from A.
- **Illustration:** A single rice-grain cross-section, two states side by side: whole grain with its outer bran layer highlighted and labelled "thiamine-bearing bran," and a polished white grain with that layer removed. One idea, two shapes, three labels. Do not clutter with a mill or a plate.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-DDDL-000043 ("classic thiamine deficiency disease"; Asian rice cultures; "I cannot"; weakness in legs/hands/arms + congestive heart failure; death from pulmonary edema and abdominal "dropsy"; "polished white rice with its thiamine-bearing bran removed"). Etymology + "beri-beri vitamin" reinforced by WAL-CLM-LETS-000377.
- **Why it wows / best UX:** A story, a foreign phrase, and a cause the reader can *see* in a grain of rice. High memorability, low text. Mirrors the vitamin-A curio pattern without reusing its figure.

### C. "False Alzheimer's" — the reframe
- **Name:** The reversible diagnosis.
- **The hook:** What can look like Alzheimer's is, in Wallach's framework, a thiamine gap — and unlike Alzheimer's he says it can be *reversed*.
- **Layout shape:** A reframe / flip. Present the mislabel ("recent memory loss — 'false Alzheimer's'") and then the turn ("a deficiency disease, not permanent damage → reversible"). A single connective sentence carries the turn — not a divider, not a 3-beat rail (avoid the selenium decline/reversal rail visual).
- **Illustration:** A minimal "locked → unlocked" motif for memory: one figure showing a closed state (can't record new memory) turning to an open state, captioned with the reversal condition. Alternatively a single card that visibly "flips" from the wrong label to the real cause. Keep to one metaphor, few elements, no stroke through any label.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-LETS-000333 (Korsakoff's = "false Alzheimer's"; inability to record new memory; performs old tasks, can't learn new; confabulation; alcoholism + B-1 deficiency); WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000014 (Korsakoff's within the beriberi family; "can be reversed" — eliminate sugar/fried foods/processed meats/oils/gluten + all 90 nutrients "with a special emphasis on the supplementation of thiamine").
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Emotionally the strongest — "the dementia that isn't permanent." It lands Wallach's boldest claim about this nutrient. Requires care to stay grounded (the reversal is his stated position; present it as his framework, which the claims are).

### D. "One vitamin, four doses" — the dose ladder ending on a surprise
- **Name:** The dose ladder.
- **The hook:** The same vitamin does escalating things as the dose climbs — and the top rung keeps mosquitoes off you.
- **Layout shape:** A vertical numeric ladder (ascending rungs), each rung a dose paired with what it's for. Distinct from A/B/C in being number-driven. Rungs, low→high: 50 mg maintenance need · 100 mg daily target · 200–500 mg therapeutic (30-day) · 500 mg "repels mosquitoes."
- **Illustration:** A clean ascending ladder/scale — four rungs, one number and one short label each. The surprising top rung (mosquito) is the payoff. No figure clutter; the numbers *are* the illustration.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-LETS-000068 (True Supplement Need 50 mg; 30-day pharmacologic 200–500 mg — verbatim "THIAMINE 1.4 mg 50 mg 200 to 500 mg"); WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000112 (daily multiple 10–100 mg → target 100 mg); WAL-CLM-LETS-000370 (B-1 "at 500 mg/day will also 'repel' mosquitoes"). Therapeutic-dose examples for the middle rung: LETS-000130 (200 mg t.i.d.), LETS-000243/-000333/-000377 (100 mg t.i.d.).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** The mosquito fact is a memorable, share-worthy curio, and the ladder honestly shows the maintenance-vs-therapeutic gap that §4 explains. Lighter/more playful than A–C; good tonal variety for the mockup set.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

Target: **100 mg/day** (`target.kind: wallach`). Chain: the source is Wallach's Epigenetics (2014) daily multiple vitamin-and-mineral program, which lists "Vitamin B1 (thiamine) 10 – 100 mg" (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000112). The derive takes the **upper bound of that range, 100 mg** (`provenance.original_low 10, original_high 100, upper_taken 100`, unit mg). No IU conversion and no body-weight ×1.54 scaling apply — thiamine is dosed directly in mg, so the posted figure is the range's ceiling, unrounded. Context to fold into the prose: his earlier Base-Line program (Let's Play Doctor, 1995, WAL-CLM-LETS-000068) sets a maintenance "True Supplement Need" of 50 mg with a 200–500 mg 30-day therapeutic dose — so 100 mg sits at the top of the everyday range and well below the short-term therapeutic doses (100–200 mg t.i.d.) he uses for the heart/mind conditions. Newest book wins for the headline number (Epigenetics 2014 over Let's Play Doctor 1995).

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **No molecular mechanism.** The pack explains B1 only as a "cofactor for energy production / carbohydrate metabolism" (EPIGEN-000035). There is NO enzyme-level detail (no TPP, no specific pathway) in any claim. Do not add any — a real-world biochemical mechanism not in the pack is exactly the forbidden injection. Concept A must stay at Wallach's level of "cofactor the brain and heart run hardest on."
- **Protocols are B1-among-many.** Most ch10 materia-medica claims (LETS-000130/142/159/203/207/229/243/244/333/377/394/396/443) list B1 as one ingredient in a long stack. They are rich for the *conditions* and for dose examples, but thin as standalone "what B1 is" material — lean on them for the heart/mind condition clusters and the therapeutic doses, not for identity.
- **No claim_text vs verbatim mismatch found.** LETS-000068 claim_text (50 mg TSN, 200–500 mg pharmacologic) is fully supported by its verbatim ("THIAMINE 1.4 mg 50 mg 200 to 500 mg" = RDA / TSN / pharmacologic). EPIGEN-000112 (10–100 mg) matches its verbatim. No superseded claims in the pack.
- **RDA trap (don't surface).** LETS-000068's "1.4 mg" is the government RDA Wallach reprints only to argue against; it is never his recommendation. If any concept shows numbers, do not present 1.4 mg as his figure.
- **Solo header: YES, clearly merited.** B1 carries a distinct, non-generic identity (beriberi vitamin, heart↔mind reach, reversible "false Alzheimer's," mosquito curio) with 22 claims across 4 books. It does not need to fold into another element.
- **Wished-for but absent:** a clean single figure of *how* thiamine converts food to energy — the corpus supports the claim but not a depictable mechanism, so Concept A uses the two-organ reach as the visual instead.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**A — "Heart to the Mind."** It is the pack's densest true idea, it is uniquely B1 (no shipped header spans two organs), and it is anchored by the two strongest claims (EPIGEN-000035 + RARE-000233). B (rice curio) is the best second mockup for tonal contrast.
