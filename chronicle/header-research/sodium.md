# Sodium — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 14 sealed claims · mineral · target: 3,300 mg/day (Wallach "True Supplement Need," ~3× the government RDA).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — one of three indispensable electrolytes.**
Sodium, chloride and potassium are the three "indispensable electrolytes," so intimately linked Wallach presents them together. Sodium is **2%** of the body's total mineral content (K is 5%, Cl is 3%); sodium and chloride are mainly **extracellular** while potassium is intracellular. Together they run normal water balance and distribution, osmotic equilibrium, acid-base balance, and muscular/nerve excitability — all under **adrenal-cortex and pituitary** hormonal control. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000187, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000102, WAL-CLM-RARE-000153, WAL-CLM-RARE-000188]

**The defining editorial throughline — Wallach's war on low-salt advice.**
The single loudest angle in the whole pack: mainstream medicine tells you to eat "little or no salt," and Wallach reads that advice as not just wrong but lethal. His tell is the rancher's salt block — "the first food item a good husbandryman puts out for his livestock is a salt block" — and the multibillion-dollar snack-food industry that quietly profits from the same craving. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000026]

**Standout mechanism — "salt hunger," the basic craving.**
Salt hunger is "one of the most basic cravings" of animals and man — strongest in **herbivores and human vegetarians**, because grains, vegetables and fruit contain little sodium chloride, while carnivores crave it less (meat is already rich in salt). Average western intake is **5–12 g/day**; the Japanese, who outlive Americans by ~4 years, average **28 g/day**. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000186 (confidence: medium), WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000102]

**Two-sided signs — sodium has BOTH a full deficiency list and a full toxicity list.**
Unusual for the corpus: Wallach gives sodium a complete deficiency roster *and* a complete excess/toxicity roster.
- **Deficiency** (strikingly neurological/psychiatric): abdominal cramps, anorexia, ataxia, confusion, crying jags, depression, dermatosis, dizziness, fatigue, flatulence, **hallucinations, illusions**, headaches, hypotension, infections, lethargy, memory loss, muscular weakness, nausea & vomiting, seizures, taste loss, weight loss. [WAL-CLM-LETS-000032]
- **Toxicity/excess**: anorexia, cognitive dysfunction, congestive heart failure, edema (esp. low-protein diets), hyperactivity, hypertension, hypertonia, irritability, polydipsia, polyuria, renal failure, seizures, tremors, weight gain. [WAL-CLM-LETS-000033]

**Signature anecdotes — three deaths from too LITTLE salt.**
Wallach's proof-by-body-count that the low-salt paranoia kills:
- **Heat stroke, July 1993 East Coast heat wave** — deaths he frames as "a simple salt or sodium deficiency… that any boy scout could diagnose and remedy with water and salt," a direct result of doctors putting patients on reduced/salt-free diets for blood pressure or heart disease; survivors were treated with IV saline. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000003, WAL-CLM-RARE-000027]
- **"Water intoxication" in infants** — babies fed low-sodium formula (out of "the allopathic doctor's paranoia about Na") whose **brains swelled**, "causing death from a simple Na deficiency." [WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000102, WAL-CLM-RARE-000189]
- **Addison's disease** — failure of the adrenal cortex loses sodium (and retains potassium) with weakness, muscle cramps, weight loss and a marked "salt hunger," relieved by salt. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000188, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000102]

**Protocol.** Treatment for sodium deficiency is plainly **water and salt** — orally or IV as 0.9% saline. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000190]

**Curio — the mineral signature of rage.**
A hair-analysis pattern for a violent, explosive "Dr. Jekyll/Mr. Hyde" temperament: low copper, zinc, **sodium**, chromium, vanadium and lithium. (Thin tie — sodium is one of six low minerals here, not the star.) [WAL-CLM-RARE-000129]

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A. "The mineral they told you to fear" — the low-salt indictment
- **The hook** — Doctors say cut salt; Wallach says that advice has a body count.
- **Layout shape** — A prosecution/evidence stack: a top thesis line (the low-salt paranoia) followed by three short "exhibit" cards, each a death Wallach pins on too little salt, closing on the one-line remedy. Not beats-then-quote; the cards ARE the argument.
- **Illustration** — A single stark motif: an empty medical salt-restriction cross / "no salt" mark, with three small case tags beside it (a heat-wave sun, an infant's bottle, an adrenal gland) — one idea (three deaths, one cause), few elements, no line routed through any label.
- **Anchored by** — low-salt thesis + salt block [WAL-CLM-RARE-000026]; heat wave 1993 "any boy scout could remedy with water and salt" [WAL-CLM-RARE-000003, WAL-CLM-RARE-000027]; infant "water intoxication," brains swelled [WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000102, WAL-CLM-RARE-000189]; Addison's salt loss [WAL-CLM-RARE-000188]; remedy = water + salt / 0.9% saline [WAL-CLM-RARE-000190].
- **Why it wows / best UX** — This is the most distinctly *Wallach* thing about sodium and nothing else in the six shipped headers does a "conventional advice is backwards" indictment. High emotional charge, instantly memorable, and every exhibit is a real sealed claim.

### B. Too little / too much — the narrow window
- **The hook** — Sodium is one of the few minerals Wallach maps in BOTH directions: deficiency and excess each have a full list, and both ends carry seizures, weight change and heart trouble.
- **Layout shape** — A true two-lane comparison (a balance): a "DEFICIENCY" column and a "TOXICITY/EXCESS" column facing each other across a centre axis, with the shared/overlapping signs (anorexia, seizures, heart involvement) called out on the axis itself.
- **Illustration** — A simple beam-balance or two-pan scale, deficiency signs on one pan, excess signs on the other; the few signs common to both float on the pivot. One figure, symmetrical, no strokes crossing labels.
- **Anchored by** — deficiency list incl. hallucinations/illusions/hypotension [WAL-CLM-LETS-000032]; toxicity list incl. hypertension/CHF/edema/renal failure [WAL-CLM-LETS-000033]. (Overlap called out only where BOTH lists literally share a term — e.g. anorexia, seizures.)
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Clean, honest, and genuinely rare in the corpus: it shows sodium is neither villain nor hero but a band you must stay inside. The symmetry is satisfying to read and the psychiatric deficiency signs (hallucinations, illusions) are a jolt readers won't expect.

### C. Salt hunger — the oldest craving
- **The hook** — Your body demands salt for a reason; the craving is ancient, and it's loudest in exactly the people (plant-eaters) whose diet gives them least.
- **Layout shape** — A curio-led cross-species spread: a single lead statement about "salt hunger" as a basic craving, then two contrasted lanes (carnivore vs herbivore/vegetarian) and a standout stat block (western 5–12 g/day vs Japanese 28 g/day, +4 years).
- **Illustration** — Two facing silhouettes — a predator and a grazer at a salt block — the grazer's craving arrow larger; beside it the intake stat as a plain two-bar contrast (12 vs 28). One concept (who craves salt, and how much), minimal elements.
- **Anchored by** — salt hunger, carnivore vs herbivore/vegetarian, 5–12 vs 28 g/day + 4-year longevity [WAL-CLM-RARE-000186 (medium confidence)]; salt hunger strongest in vegetarians [WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000102]; rancher's salt block [WAL-CLM-RARE-000026].
- **Why it wows / best UX** — The "even cows get a salt block" and "Japanese eat 28 g and live longer" facts are sticky, surprising and warmer than the death-toll concept. Good if Luneth wants a lighter, curiosity-driven header. (Note the 28 g stat is medium-confidence — see Gaps.)

### D. The extracellular trio — where sodium lives and what it runs
- **The hook** — Sodium is 2% of your minerals, sitting mostly *outside* your cells, one leg of a three-part electrolyte system that keeps your water, pH and nerves in balance under hormone command.
- **Layout shape** — An identity/where-it-lives map: a compact proportion strip (Na 2% · K 5% · Cl 3%) paired with an inside-vs-outside-the-cell diagram, plus a short "what the trio maintains" label set (water balance · osmotic equilibrium · acid-base · nerve/muscle) and the hormonal controllers (adrenal cortex + pituitary).
- **Illustration** — A single cell drawn simply: Na and Cl dots clustered *outside* the membrane, K dots *inside* — the extracellular/intracellular split made literal in one clean figure; the 2/5/3% proportions as a slim inline bar. No crossing strokes.
- **Anchored by** — trio + 2/5/3% + extracellular/intracellular + functions [WAL-CLM-RARE-000187]; K+Na maintain water/osmotic/acid-base, K+Ca run neuromuscular [WAL-CLM-RARE-000153]; adrenal-cortex/pituitary control [WAL-CLM-RARE-000188, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000102].
- **Why it wows / best UX** — The most "textbook-solid," calmest option: teaches what sodium actually IS before the drama. Closest in spirit to the calcium where-it-lives header, so it reads familiar — which is also its weakness (least surprising of the four).

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

The 3,300 mg/day target is Wallach's own **"True Supplement Need"** for an adult, taken straight from the Base Line Nutritional Supplement Program table in *Let's Play Doctor* [source: WAL-CLM-LETS-000066]. That row prints three numbers in column order — the government RDA (1,100 mg), then Wallach's True Supplement Need (**3,300 mg**), then his 30-day pharmacologic dose (300–3,000 mg). We use the middle figure: **3,300 mg — roughly three times the RDA Wallach reprints only to argue against.** There is no range to take an upper of, no IU conversion and no body-weight scaling here; the number is Wallach's stated maintenance target verbatim, so it ports 1:1 with no rounding. (The verbatim confirms 3,300 mg — no claim_text/verbatim mismatch.)

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Solo header: YES, comfortably.** 14 sealed claims spanning identity, mechanism, a full deficiency AND a full toxicity list, three named anecdotes, a protocol, and a strong editorial throughline. Sodium easily merits its own bespoke header and does not need to fold into potassium/chloride.
- **Medium-confidence stat.** The "Japanese average 28 g/day and outlive Americans by ~4 years" figure [WAL-CLM-RARE-000186] is the pack's only medium-confidence claim. It's the centrepiece stat of Concept C — usable, but flag it to Luneth as slightly softer evidence than the rest, and don't build the whole header's credibility on it.
- **Charge check.** The low-salt indictment (Concept A) is editorially hot by design — it's Wallach contradicting mainstream medical advice with a body count. That's on-thesis, not fringe by the charged-content rules, but it IS the strongest-flavoured option; Concept B or D is the calmer fallback if he wants less heat.
- **Thin tie, don't feature.** The hair-analysis "violent temperament" signature [WAL-CLM-RARE-000129] lists sodium as one of six low minerals — too diffuse to anchor a sodium header; leave it to the Full Record, not the illustration.
- **No numeric mismatch, no superseded claims.** All 14 claims are current; target 3,300 mg matches its verbatim.
- **Wished-for but absent:** the pack has no claim on the sodium–potassium *pump* or on sodium's specific role in nerve-impulse firing at the membrane (only the general "muscular/nerve excitability" framing), so Concept D's cell diagram must stay at the extracellular/intracellular-location level and NOT depict an active pump — that mechanism isn't in the corpus.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**A — "The mineral they told you to fear."** It's the one angle unique to Wallach, it unifies the pack's strongest anecdotes (heat wave, infants, Addison's) under a single memorable thesis, and no shipped header uses the "conventional advice is backwards" shape. Concept B (too little/too much balance) is the best calmer alternative if the indictment reads too hot.
