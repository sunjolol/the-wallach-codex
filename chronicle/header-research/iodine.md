# Iodine — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 32 sealed claims · mineral · target: 230 mcg/day (derived from Epigenetics 50–150 mcg per 100 lb; a Let's Play Doctor row independently lists 250 mcg).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — the thyroid's raw material, pulled from the sea.**
Iodine is essential to red and brown algae and to all vertebrates; in land animals it collects in the thyroid gland and the hair (WAL-CLM-RARE-000144, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000173). The body combines it with the amino acid tyrosine to manufacture thyroxin, the main thyroid hormone (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000023, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000177, WAL-CLM-RARE-000145). It is a trace need — Wallach's daily range is only 50–150 mcg per 100 lb (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000131).

**The standout mechanism — thyroxin is the master metabolic dial.**
Thyroid hormones "control and regulate digestion, heart rate, body temperature, sweat gland activity, nervous and reproductive systems, general metabolism and body weight" (WAL-CLM-RARE-000145, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000181). Because one hormone governs so many systems, an iodine/thyroid shortfall produces a very broad symptom mix — that breadth IS the iodine story.

**The copper twist — goiter can be a copper problem, not an iodine one.**
Copper is a required cofactor to use iodine (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000025). Wallach's evidence: three Adriatic island populations with *identical soil-iodine content* but sharply different goiter — 46% of Pisila, 40% of Polje, only 3% of Milahnici — the difference traced to a severe copper deficiency in the high-goiter soils (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000180, WAL-CLM-RARE-000149).

**The goitrogen block — iodine present but jammed by food.**
Even with abundant iodine, certain foods produce goiter. Japanese living on the sea coast get goiter despite high iodine intake; in a feeding study, subjects given Chinese cabbage, turnips, buckwheat, noodles, a fixed 2.0 mcg of iodine, or soybean all developed goiter — every group *except* the one fed seaweed (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000179, WAL-CLM-RARE-000147). The goitrogens Wallach names: nitrates/nitrites in cured meats (ham, bacon, sausage, bologna, salami, pastrami, pepperoni, jerky, deli meats) and cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, kale) (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000184, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000160, WAL-CLM-RARE-000148).

**The two failure directions — hypo vs hyper.**
Hypothyroidism / Hashimoto's (thyroid too slow): fatigue, cold intolerance, muscle aches, heavy periods, low sex drive, brittle nails, weight gain, hair loss, muscle cramps, depression, constipation, elevated cholesterol, puffy face, dry skin/hair, poor concentration, poor memory, goiter, infertility, cretinism (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000182, WAL-CLM-RARE-000150, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000093; short list also WAL-CLM-LETS-000013 adds low basal body temperature). Hyperthyroidism / Graves' (thyroid too fast): insomnia, heat intolerance, excessive sweating, lighter periods, hand tremors, rapid pulse, exophthalmos ("bug eyes"), weight loss, increased appetite, muscle weakness, frequent bowel movements, irritability, nervousness, goiter (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000183, WAL-CLM-RARE-000151). Note the two lists are near mirror-images: cold vs heat intolerance, weight gain vs loss, constipation vs frequent bowel movements, fatigue vs insomnia — goiter sits on both.

**Goiter as a visible, mechanical sign.**
Wallach frames goiter as a straightforward deficiency disease: starved of its nutrients the thyroid gland swells "50 to 100 times its normal size," creating the visible lump at the front of the neck (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000001, WAL-CLM-LETS-000287). Historically iodine deficiency occurred in communities living at a distance from the sea (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000001).

**The "physician-caused" epidemic — a signature Wallach stance.**
Iodine intake is usually low to begin with, and since Americans began restricting salt on doctors' advice (iodized salt being a key source), goiter and hypothyroidism have become epidemic (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000024, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000177).

**Intake / loss / safety numbers (a strong numeric palette).**
Average American intake 170–250 mcg/day (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000178, WAL-CLM-RARE-000146). Lost in sweat up to 146 mcg/day with only moderate exercise (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000178, WAL-CLM-RARE-000146). Not toxic up to 2,000 mcg/day long-term (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000178, WAL-CLM-RARE-000146). Roughly 11 million Americans have an under- or over-active thyroid (WAL-CLM-RARE-000150, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000093) — but see the prevalence flag in §5.

**Curios / surprising facts.**
- Iodine is one of the *few* trace minerals absorbed almost completely: the ratio of absolute to dietary requirement is nearly 1:1 for metallic iodine, vs 1:10 for iron and 1:100 for chromium (WAL-CLM-RARE-000074).
- Where it lives in nature (ppm): seawater 0.06, soil 5, terrestrial plants 0.42, but marine plants 30–1,500 — and "large areas of earth are known to be devoid of iodine" because it binds tightly to humus (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000173).
- Commercial iodine comes from sodium iodate in Chilean saltpeter, historically from the ashes of burnt seaweed, from giant kelp off California, and from underground brines in Arkansas and Oklahoma (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000175).
- Everyday/medical uses: alcohol-tincture wound antiseptic; iodine tablets to purify water (military, campers, scouts); radioactive I-131 for thyroid cancer/hyperthyroidism, I-125 for prostate cancer — the last noted as standard medical practice, not a Wallach protocol (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000176).

**Signature protocols/anecdotes.**
- Goiter: remove all goitrogens, iodine 250 mcg/day, thyroid USP 2–5 grains each morning, kelp as a natural source (WAL-CLM-LETS-000287).
- Hypothyroid: basal-temperature test (underarm below 98°F on waking = low thyroid), thyroid 3–9 grains, kelp, iodine 250–500 mcg/day, quercus marine / Fucus vesiculosis (WAL-CLM-LETS-000309).
- Fibrocystic breast disease: avoid methyl xanthines, vitamin E 800–1,200 IU, vitamin A 300,000 IU as beta carotene, EFA 5 g t.i.d., iodine 200 mcg/day, selenium 500 mcg/day (WAL-CLM-LETS-000275).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A. The metabolic dial (two-lane hypo ↔ hyper)
- **Name** — "The dial."
- **The hook** — Iodine sets the *speed* of your whole metabolism; run the thyroid too slow OR too fast and the same systems break in opposite directions.
- **Layout shape** — A single central dial/gauge with a needle, and two symmetric lanes reading outward: LEFT = too slow (hypo), RIGHT = too fast (hyper). Each lane carries three mirror-paired signs, not a long dump. The mirror is the whole point, so pairs sit on one row.
- **Illustration** — One simple speedometer-style dial (cold/slow on the left, hot/fast on the right), needle in the "just right" centre. No routed lines through labels — the paired signs sit in clean cells beside each arc.
- **Anchored by** — the master-regulator list (WAL-CLM-RARE-000145 / WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000181: "heart rate, body temperature… metabolism… body weight"); hypo signs (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000182, WAL-CLM-RARE-000150) — cold intolerance, weight *gain*, constipation, fatigue; hyper signs (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000183, WAL-CLM-RARE-000151) — heat intolerance, weight *loss*, frequent bowel movements, insomnia. Goiter as the sign shared by both (present in every list).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — This is the single most iodine-specific idea in the corpus and no other shipped header uses it. The mirror ("cold vs heat, gain vs loss") is instantly graspable and memorable, and it teaches the real mechanism (one hormone = one dial for many systems) without a wall of symptoms.

### B. The copper twist (a detective single-figure)
- **Name** — "Same soil, different fate."
- **The hook** — Goiter isn't always a lack of iodine — three islands with *identical* soil iodine had wildly different goiter, because iodine needs copper to be used.
- **Layout shape** — A three-tile case study: three island cards side by side, each a big goiter percentage; a single caption underneath delivers the twist (identical iodine, the variable was copper). A short "copper is the key" note anchors the mechanism.
- **Illustration** — Three simple island silhouettes with a shared "iodine level: identical" band across the top, and each island's goiter % as the dominant number: 46% · 40% · 3%. A small copper key/cofactor mark next to the low-goiter island. One idea, few elements.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000180 / WAL-CLM-RARE-000149 (Pisila 46%, Polje 40%, Milahnici 3%; "identical iodine content of the soil… severe copper deficiency"); WAL-CLM-DDDL-000025 ("copper is a required cofactor to utilize iodine").
- **Why it wows / best UX** — A genuine mystery-with-a-payoff: the numbers do the work, and the reveal (it was copper, not iodine) is surprising and true to Wallach's cross-mineral thinking. Distinct from every shipped figure — an evidence/case-study layout, not a body diagram.

### C. Sea to gland (where-it-lives geography)
- **Name** — "A sea mineral in one small gland."
- **The hook** — Iodine is a gift of the ocean that the land loses and your body hoards in a single gland.
- **Layout shape** — A left-to-right gradient journey in three stations: OCEAN (rich) → INLAND SOIL (poor) → THE THYROID (concentrated), each labelled with its grounded figure. The "distance from the sea = goiter" idea threads the middle.
- **Illustration** — A simple horizontal gradient strip: dense/blue at the sea end (seaweed up to 1,500 ppm), fading to near-empty inland ("large areas devoid of iodine"), then a single highlighted thyroid glyph at the end where the body concentrates it. No cluttered map — one gradient, three anchored labels.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000173 (marine plants 30–1,500 ppm; seawater 0.06, terrestrial plants 0.42; "large areas of earth… devoid of iodine"; concentrates in thyroid and hair); WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000001 (deficiency "occurred in communities living at a distance from the sea"); WAL-CLM-RARE-000144 (concentrates in the thyroid gland and hair).
- **Why it wows / best UX** — A clean abundance/where-it-lives map (an archetype no iodine-adjacent header has used) that explains *why* iodine deficiency is geographic. Visually calm, one gradient carries the whole arc.

### D. Present but blocked — the goitrogen saboteur
- **Name** — "Enough iodine, still a goiter."
- **The hook** — Some everyday foods jam the thyroid even when iodine is abundant.
- **Layout shape** — A "block" figure: iodine arriving, then a barrier of named goitrogen foods, then a stalled thyroid. Anchored by the feeding-study punchline — every food group tested caused goiter except seaweed.
- **Illustration** — A simple gate/barrier motif: iodine on one side, a short row of the named culprits (cured meats/nitrates · cruciferous veg) forming the block, the thyroid stalled behind it. One "except seaweed" callout as the twist. Keep it to a handful of icons; no busy pantry.
- **Anchored by** — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000179 / WAL-CLM-RARE-000147 (Japanese seacoast goiter despite high iodine; feeding study — cabbage, turnips, buckwheat, noodles, 2.0 mcg iodine, soybean all → goiter, only seaweed spared); WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000184 (nitrates/nitrites + cruciferous); WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000160, WAL-CLM-RARE-000148.
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Counterintuitive and practical ("you can eat iodine and still be short"), with a memorable exception (seaweed wins). Distinct mechanism-figure. Caveat: thematically adjacent to B (both = "iodine present ≠ working") — see §5; the two should not both ship, or they'd read as one idea told twice.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

Wallach's Epigenetics daily mineral table lists iodine at 50–150 mcg per 100 lb of body weight (WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000131). Taking the upper bound (150 mcg) and scaling to a 154 lb reference body (×1.54) gives 231, rounded to two significant figures = **230 mcg/day**. This lands squarely inside his other stated figures: his Let's Play Doctor Base Line program independently lists a 250 mcg "True Supplement Need" (WAL-CLM-LETS-000054), and his goiter/hypothyroid protocols use 250 mcg/day (WAL-CLM-LETS-000287) — all far under the 2,000 mcg/day he calls non-toxic (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000178). For scale, the average American already takes in 170–250 mcg/day but loses up to 146 mcg of it in sweat (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000178). Target.kind is a plain Wallach number — no gap, no mirror.

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Prevalence number conflict — do NOT blend.** WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000181 says "*one* million Americans" have a thyroid condition (its claim_text faithfully carries "one million"), while WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000093 and WAL-CLM-RARE-000150 both say "*11 million*." No claim is marked superseded. Trust each verbatim; if a figure appears in a header, use the 11-million pair (two books agree) and never average the two.
- **No superseded claims in the pack** — all `superseded_by` are null. No claim_text carries a number bigger than its own verbatim supports (the LETS-000054 dose row is correctly read: 150 = government RDA reprinted to argue against, 250 = Wallach's True Supplement Need, 1,000 = 30-day pharmacologic — the header must not present 150 as Wallach's recommendation).
- **B and D overlap in theme.** Both say "iodine present but not working" (copper missing vs food blocking). They're mechanistically distinct and either is strong, but shipping both would read as one idea repeated. Pick one for the four-mockup set, or use the other only as a small secondary beat.
- **Alternative concept the corpus supports but I didn't make a top-four:** a "first visible sign" figure on goiter itself — the gland swelling "50 to 100 times its normal size" into a neck lump (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000001, WAL-CLM-LETS-000287) is genuinely visual and mirrors copper's gray-hair "first sign" archetype. Held back only because A/B/C/D already cover more ground; worth offering as a fifth if Luneth wants a body-sign angle.
- **Rich enough for a solo bespoke header — clearly yes.** 32 claims spanning identity, a clean mechanism, two mirror-image failure states, a copper cofactor story, a food-block paradox, geography, vivid intake/safety numbers, and three protocols. Iodine does NOT need to fold into another element.
- **Nothing wished-for is missing for the lead concept.** The only thing the corpus lacks is any figure for *color/appearance of iodine itself* — irrelevant here; the thyroid-dial and copper stories are the strengths.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**A — the metabolic dial.** It's the most iodine-specific idea in the whole pack (one hormone = one dial governing many systems), the hypo/hyper mirror is instantly memorable and unlike any shipped figure, and it teaches the real mechanism rather than dumping a symptom list. B (the copper twist) is the strongest runner-up and the best "wow" curio if a more narrative header is wanted.
