# Cobalt — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 13 sealed claims · mineral · target: MIRRORS vitamin B12 (no standalone cobalt number — carries B12's verdict).

Cobalt is the rare case where the essential's whole identity is another essential. Wallach's position (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233): the body does not require cobalt on its own — it requires a *cobalt complex*, the finished molecule vitamin B12. Every strong concept below leans on that single, unusual fact rather than treating cobalt as a free-standing mineral. This is what keeps a cobalt header from being a re-skin of the B12 page: the angle is always the ATOM and the METAL, never B12's downstream biochemistry.

---

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — the one atom at the center of B12.**
A single cobalt atom is the central metal component of vitamin B12; B12 is itself the cofactor/activator (the cobamide coenzymes) for several essential enzymes (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000079). Wallach calls cobalt's essentiality *unusual*: the requirement is not for cobalt but for the *cobalt complex* cyanocobalamine = B12; a pure-cobalt requirement exists only in some bacteria and algae (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233, WAL-CLM-RARE-000114). This is the definitional hook — "one atom → the whole vitamin."

**The red comes from the cobalt.**
B12 is a red, water-soluble crystalline substance, and "the red color is due to the cobalt in the molecule" (WAL-CLM-DDDL-000044; corroborated WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000085). A vivid, literally-visible fact: the mineral is what colours the vitamin.

**The porphyrin-ring family — three metals, three rings, three colours.**
B12's cobalt "is chelated in a large tetrapyrrole ring similar to the porphyrin ring found in hemoglobin (iron) and chlorophyll (magnesium)" (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000085). Same ring architecture holds iron in blood (red), magnesium in leaves (green), cobalt in B12 (red). A ready-made comparative family. *(Caution: the shipped magnesium header already used chlorophyll; here the angle is the three-way FAMILY, not chlorophyll itself.)*

**Symbiosis / essential to all life.**
Cobalt is essential for all forms of life — blue-green algae, some bacteria and fungi, plants, insects, birds, reptiles, amphibians, mammals including man; it acts as an enzyme cofactor/activator and "fixes" nitrogen during amino-acid production (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000079). The need for B12 cobalt "is thought by some to represent a symbiotic relationship" — microbes build B12 from elemental cobalt, vertebrates require the finished B12 (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233).

**The discovery curio — "bush sickness."**
Cobalt's essentiality was discovered from a fatal disease of cattle and sheep in Australia and New Zealand — "bush sickness" — that could be successfully treated and prevented with cobalt supplements. It was marked by emaciation (Wallach likens it to unsupplemented vegans), a dull stare, a listless/starved look, pale mucous membranes, anorexia, and pernicious anemia (macrocytic/hypochromic) (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000082; also WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000077). A genuine origin-story with a striking image.

**The soil threshold.**
In the soil, less than 0.07 ppm cobalt leaves crops (and the animals and people who eat them) deficient, while 0.11 ppm prevents and cures the deficiency (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084). Two tiny, concrete numbers that carry the whole soil-to-body chain.

**Human deficiency — pernicious anemia + demyelination.**
Pernicious anemia and demyelination of the spinal cord and large nerve trunks are the classic signs of B12/cobalt deficiency (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000083, WAL-CLM-RARE-000115, WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000077). Causes of malabsorption: surgical removal of part of the stomach (kills intrinsic-factor zones) or ileum, small-intestinal diverticulae, parasites (tapeworm), celiac disease, hypochlorhydria, other malabsorption (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000083).

**The absorption gate — acid + intrinsic factor.**
Metallic cobalt is absorbed 20–26.2% *only if* intrinsic factor (Castle's factor, a stomach mucoprotein) is present AND stomach pH is 2.0 or lower. With hypochlorhydria (low stomach acid — usually a salt/NaCl deficiency) intrinsic factor fails and B12/cobalt is not absorbed — hence B12 shots for older people on salt-restricted diets (sublingual/oral-spray B12 are alternatives). Plant-derived cobalt is very bioavailable, but low-salt diets + cobalt-depleted soils leave vegetarians frequently deficient (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000081).

**What B12/cobalt does (the payload).**
With folic acid, choline and methionine it transfers single-carbon (methyl) groups to build RNA and make DNA from RNA — gene function, tied to preconception nutrition and preventing birth defects; it drives growth, myelin formation (converting cholesterol into the nerve-insulating sheath in brain, spinal cord and large nerve trunks), and red-blood-cell synthesis; cobalt is also a required cofactor for the thyroid hormone thyroxine (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000080, WAL-CLM-RARE-000114).

**Dose picture.**
RDA for B12/cobalt is just 3–4 mcg/day; Wallach recommends 250–400 mcg/day for a "respectable safety margin," urging pregnant/nursing mothers especially to supplement (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084, WAL-CLM-RARE-000014). At the far extreme, cobalt excess in humans (20–30 mg/day) can over-stimulate red-cell production (erythropoiesis) via erythropoietin (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084).

**Cyanocobalamine curio.**
The first B12 isolated in the lab carried a cyanide group — hence *cyanocobalamine*; cyanocobalamine and hydroxycobalamine are the most active forms; B12 was isolated from liver extract in 1948 with anti-pernicious-anemia activity, and ~30% of its activity is lost in cooking (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000085).

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A. "One atom, the whole vitamin" — the molecule reveal
- **Name:** The Atom at the Center.
- **The hook:** You don't need cobalt — you need the one cobalt atom locked inside B12.
- **Layout shape:** A single, richly-annotated central figure with one connective line beneath it; no beats, no big-number block. The whole header is the reveal.
- **Illustration:** One B12 molecule rendered as a red crystalline ring with a single glowing cobalt atom at its dead center — one label ("one cobalt atom") pointing in, one label ("→ the entire vitamin B12") pointing out. The red of the crystal IS the cobalt. Fewest possible elements: ring + centre dot + two labels.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000079 ("a single cobalt atom is the central metal component of vitamin B12"); WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233 (requirement is for the *cobalt complex*, not pure cobalt); WAL-CLM-DDDL-000044 ("the red color is due to the cobalt in the molecule").
- **Why it wows / best UX:** It is the truest, most bespoke thing about cobalt and it dovetails perfectly with the mirrors-B12 target — the reader instantly understands *why* cobalt has no number of its own. One image, one idea, zero clutter.

### B. "Three metals, three rings" — the porphyrin family
- **Name:** The Ring Family.
- **The hook:** The same ring shape holds iron in your blood, magnesium in green leaves, and cobalt in B12.
- **Layout shape:** A three-lane parallel comparison (three equal panels side by side), each panel: metal · its ring-molecule · its colour/role. No chassis, no quote — pure comparison.
- **Illustration:** Three simplified tetrapyrrole rings in a row, each with a differently-coloured metal at centre — iron/hemoglobin (red blood), magnesium/chlorophyll (green), cobalt/B12 (red crystal) — cobalt's panel visually emphasised as "you are here."
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000085 ("B12 cobalt is chelated in a large tetrapyrrole ring similar to the porphyrin ring found in hemoglobin (iron) and chlorophyll (magnesium)").
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Places a "minor" trace mineral into the most elite company in biochemistry — the same architecture as blood and leaves. Memorable and dignifying. *(Design caveat: keep it a three-way FAMILY so it does not read as a rerun of the magnesium/chlorophyll header; cobalt is the subject, the other two are context.)*

### C. "Bush sickness" — the discovery curio + soil threshold
- **Name:** How We Found Out.
- **The hook:** We learned cobalt was essential from sheep dying in the Australian bush — and a pinch in the soil cured them.
- **Layout shape:** A did-you-know origin card: a short narrative beat leading into ONE small figure — the soil threshold — then the human echo. Historical/story voice, no big-number stat block.
- **Illustration:** A single minimal soil-threshold gauge: a bar from 0.07 ppm ("deficient — the herd wastes") to 0.11 ppm ("prevents & cures"), the gap between them tiny and dramatic. One line, two ticks, two labels — no routes through text.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000082 (bush sickness discovery — emaciation, dull stare, anorexia, pernicious anemia, cured/prevented by cobalt); WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084 (soil <0.07 ppm deficient, 0.11 ppm prevents/cures); WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000077 (bush sickness in Australian/NZ livestock).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** A true, vivid, human story most readers have never heard, with two astonishingly small numbers that make the soil-to-body chain concrete. Distinct register from A/B/D (narrative, not molecular).

### D. "The lock" — why you can eat cobalt and still starve of it
- **Name:** The Absorption Gate.
- **The hook:** Cobalt only gets in when two keys are present at once — stomach acid at pH 2 and intrinsic factor. Miss either and it passes straight through.
- **Layout shape:** A mechanism figure that IS the header — a gate/checkpoint with two required conditions, and the failure branch (hypochlorhydria → B12 shots) shown as the consequence. One connective sentence, no quote.
- **Illustration:** A simple gate with two "locks" that must both be satisfied — `acid pH ≤ 2.0` and `intrinsic factor present` — cobalt passing through only when both are met; a greyed "low stomach acid → nothing gets in" state beside it. Two conditions, one gate, one fail-state — few elements, no stroke through any label.
- **Anchored by:** WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000081 ("absorbed at 20–26.2% only if intrinsic factor … present and stomach pH is 2.0 or lower"; hypochlorhydria → not absorbed → B12 shots; plant cobalt very bioavailable but vegetarians frequently deficient).
- **Why it wows / best UX:** Reframes deficiency as a *plumbing* problem, not a diet problem — the counter-intuitive "you can consume it and still lack it," which is exactly the kind of insight the app exists to deliver. Mechanistically the richest single claim in the pack.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

Cobalt's target is not a number of its own. `target.kind = "mirrors"` → it mirrors **vitamin B12**: Wallach states no separate cobalt requirement, because in his framework the need is met entirely through B12 (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000233 — "the requirement is for a cobalt complex known as cyanocobalamine or vitamin B12"). So the coverage verdict cobalt shows is B12's verdict, and the amount it points to is B12's amount: against a conventional RDA of just **3–4 mcg/day**, Wallach prefers **250–400 mcg/day** for a "respectable safety margin," especially when preparing for pregnancy and while nursing (WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084, WAL-CLM-RARE-000014). There is no cobalt-specific range, IU factor, or body-weight scaling to derive — the honest statement is: *cobalt is covered when B12 is covered.* (For context only, not a target: soil below 0.07 ppm cobalt is deficient / 0.11 ppm sufficient — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084; and human cobalt excess is 20–30 mg/day — same claim.)

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Solo header justified — but stay on the COBALT angle.** With 13 rich claims and a genuinely bespoke identity (the atom, the red, the ring family, bush sickness, the absorption gate), cobalt merits its own header and is not a re-skin. The risk is duplication with the vitamin-B12 page: B12's biochemistry payload (methyl transfer, myelin, DNA/RNA — WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000080) belongs more to B12. Keep cobalt's header on the *metal/atom/mineral* story (Concepts A, B, C, D all do this) and let B12 carry the payload. This is a deliberate design boundary, not a content gap.
- **`claim_text`-vs-`verbatim` mismatch on WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000077 (flag).** Its `claim_text` is a large, fact-dense summary (250–400 mcg, 3–4 mcg RDA, 20–30 mg excess, tetrapyrrole ring, thyroid cofactor), but its `verbatim` is only a tiny fragment: "…parasites (tapeworm), celiac disease, gluten intolerance, and other malabsorption diseases. Pernicious anemia". The big numbers in that claim_text are NOT supported by its own verbatim — this is the [[claim-text-numbers-unguarded]] pattern. They ARE independently supported elsewhere with real verbatim (250–400 & 3–4 mcg in WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000084; thyroid in WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000080), so at design time pull those numbers BY THE VERBATIM-BACKED claim IDs, never by EPIGEN-000077's verbatim.
- **`deficiency_signs` array is empty at top level**, but deficiency material is abundant inside the claims (pernicious anemia, demyelination, bush-sickness signs). No real gap — just don't rely on the summary field; the claims carry it.
- **Ring-family overlap with the shipped magnesium header.** Chlorophyll/magnesium already appeared on magnesium's "cycle of life" header. Concept B reuses that fact as ONE of three lanes — acceptable because the subject and framing differ (a comparative family with cobalt as protagonist), but note it so the two headers don't visually echo.
- **No dramatic decline→reversal timeline in the corpus.** Cobalt's deficiency is described as states (pernicious anemia, demyelination) and an origin story, not a time-course of decline and recovery — so a selenium-style rail figure is NOT well supported here. Don't force it.
- **Thin on personal/patient anecdote.** The corpus gives a veterinary discovery (bush sickness) but no human case-story anecdote. Concept C uses the animal origin honestly; do not invent a human vignette to match.
