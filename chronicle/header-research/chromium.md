# Chromium — header research dossier
> status: RESEARCH (concepts only — NOT designed). 67 sealed claims · mineral · target: 620 mcg/day (Wallach, derived from the Epigenetics 200–400 mcg/100 lb range).

## 1. The material (grounded, by angle)

**Core identity — the working core of glucose tolerance factor (GTF).**
Chromium activates phosphoglucomutase and other enzymes, but its headline job is being the metal at the centre of GTF — the compound that lets insulin do its work of controlling blood sugar. GTF is a three-part complex: chromium-III + dinicotinic acid (a niacin / vitamin-B3 derivative) + glutathione (the body's built-in antioxidant). The whole body holds only about **1.5 mg** of chromium; hair concentrates it roughly **10×** higher than blood, so hair analysis reads tissue stores better than a blood test. [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000087; enzyme + GTF + deficiency list also WAL-CLM-RARE-000116]

**The signature paradox — sugar both flushes chromium out AND is what a deficiency makes you crave.**
Eating sugar (colas, fruit juice, honey, candy, table sugar, fructose) raises the rate of urinary chromium loss by **300% for 12 hours**. [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091, WAL-CLM-RARE-000117] And Wallach reads a craving for sweets not as appetite but as a deficiency alarm: the "munchies," alcohol and candy cravings (chocolate especially) are "sure signs of a chromium and vanadium deficiency." [WAL-CLM-RARE-000039, WAL-CLM-RARE-000313] Deficient people reach for HFCS soft drinks first and plain water last. [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000467] This is a self-reinforcing loop — sugar strips chromium, low chromium drives sugar hunger — and it is the most chromium-specific story in the pack.

**The absorption gauntlet — chromium is the hardest trace mineral to keep.**
Ordinary inorganic chromium is barely used: an average intake of **50–100 mcg** yields only **0.25–5 mcg** the body can actually retain. [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091; 0.25–0.5 mcg in WAL-CLM-RARE-000117] Form decides everything — **25% of chelated** chromium is absorbed, versus **up to 98% of colloidal**. [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091] Wallach frames the absolute-vs-dietary requirement gap as a ratio: iodine ≈ **1:1**, iron **1:10**, chromium **1:100** — chromium is the extreme case. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000074]

**The wow-stat — one trace mineral bought a third more life.**
Gary Evans (Bemidji State University, Minnesota) showed a **33.3% increase in lifespan** of laboratory animals supplemented with chromium — overturning the prior gerontology view (Roy Walford) that severe calorie restriction was the only way to extend life past the average. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000124, WAL-CLM-DDDL-000016, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000090]

**Prevalence + the long decline.**
Wallach cites USDA's Richard Anderson: **"90% of all Americans are deficient in chromium."** [WAL-CLM-DDDL-000015, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000089, WAL-CLM-RARE-000118] Tissue chromium falls with age (newborns carry more per pound than adults), and average American serum chromium has been sliding since 1948 — tabulated in mcg/L: **28 (cities) / 1000 (rural) in 1948 → 13 (1971) → 10 (1972) → 4.7–5.1 (1973) → 0.73–1.6 (1974) → 0.16 (1978) → 0.43 (1980) → 0.13 by 1985.** [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000092] A related figure: abnormal glucose tolerance in **77%** of clinically "normal" adults over 70. [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000089, WAL-CLM-RARE-000118]

**"A whole specialty of medicine wiped out."**
Since **1958** it's been known that supplemental chromium prevents and treats both diabetes and hypoglycemia (Walter Mertz, USDA, in Federation Proceedings); Wallach calls diabetes "the ultimate case of a whole specialty of medicine which could be wiped out by universal chromium supplementation." [WAL-CLM-DDDL-000046] In his framework adult-onset diabetes and hypoglycemia "in reality" are chromium + vanadium deficiencies, often from celiac-type malabsorption. [WAL-CLM-RARE-000307]

**Deficiency signs — metabolic, behavioral, and visible.**
The deficiency table runs from low blood sugar, reactive hypoglycemia, prediabetes and type-2 diabetes, hyperinsulinemia through hyperactivity, ADD/ADHD, autism, depression, "manic depression"/bipolar, up to violent **"Dr. Jekyll / Mr. Hyde rages,"** plus impaired growth, peripheral neuropathy, elevated triglycerides/cholesterol, coronary disease, infertility, obesity, and a shortened lifespan. [WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000062, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000088, WAL-CLM-RARE-000116] Two *visible* signs: **"allergic shiners"** — dark circles under the eyes — flag Cr/V/Li deficiency [WAL-CLM-RARE-000042]; a hair-analysis "violent temperament" signature is low copper, zinc, sodium, chromium, vanadium, lithium [WAL-CLM-RARE-000129, WAL-CLM-RARE-000081]. Simpler lists: anxiety, fatigue, hypoglycemia, diabetes, retarded growth / short lifespan, high cholesterol [WAL-CLM-LETS-000006].

**Curio — the infant-formula indictment.**
"Science Diet dog food has 40 minerals, Purina rat pellets have 28, and not one human infant formula has more than 12 — chromium, vanadium and lithium are totally absent — a dog's life may not be so bad." [WAL-CLM-RARE-000043]

**Anecdote + toxicity (minor).**
Zoo marmosets, alligators and shrews died of diabetes at autopsy — a chromium + vanadium deficiency. [WAL-CLM-DDDL-000008] Toxicity/excess signs: dermatitis, GI ulcers, kidney and liver dysfunction. [WAL-CLM-LETS-000007]

## 2. Four header concepts (genuinely distinct)

### A. The Sugar Loop
- **The hook** — Sugar and chromium are locked in a doom loop: sugar washes chromium out of you, and running low on chromium makes you crave sugar.
- **Layout shape** — A single centred circular figure that IS the whole block, with one short framing line above and a one-line payoff below. No beats, no big-number stack, no quote — the loop carries the argument by itself.
- **Illustration** — A clean two-node cycle: node 1 "SUGAR," node 2 "CHROMIUM FLUSHED OUT," two curved arrows closing the circle. One arrow labelled "urinary loss up 300% for 12 hrs"; the return arrow labelled "low chromium → crave sweets." Two nodes, two arrows — nothing else. (Route both labels *outside* the arrows, never across them — Rule 2.)
- **Anchored by** — 300%/12 hr urinary loss [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091, WAL-CLM-RARE-000117]; cravings-as-deficiency-signal [WAL-CLM-RARE-000039 ("munchies… sure signs"), WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000467 (soft drinks first, water last), WAL-CLM-RARE-000313].
- **Why it wows / best UX** — This is the one story that is chromium and nothing else — mechanistic, counter-intuitive, and instantly personal (everyone has felt a sugar craving). A causal *loop* is a figure none of the six shipped headers use, so it reads as fresh. The skim-reader leaves understanding *why* they crave sweets.

### B. A Third More Life
- **The hook** — A single trace mineral extended animal lifespan by 33% — and overturned the belief that only starvation-level calorie restriction could do it.
- **Layout shape** — A curio/finding layout built around one dominant statistic: a short setup line ("Until 1980s gerontology said only near-starvation extended life…"), the **33.3%** stat as the visual anchor, then a one-line resolution naming Evans vs Walford. Deliberately NOT a beats-then-quote chassis.
- **Illustration** — Two simple horizontal life-span bars stacked: a baseline bar, and a second bar **one-third longer** with the extra segment tinted and labelled "+33.3%." Two bars, one highlighted extension — the fewest possible marks. (Both bars share the same left origin so the extension reads by construction — Rule 4.)
- **Anchored by** — 33.3% lifespan increase, Gary Evans / Bemidji State [WAL-CLM-RARE-000124, WAL-CLM-DDDL-000016, WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000090]; Roy Walford calorie-restriction context [same claims].
- **Why it wows / best UX** — The biggest, most quotable number in the whole pack, tied to a genuine "they were wrong" reversal. A two-bar comparison is honest and legible where a busy diagram would not be. Risk: the concept leans on one finding, so the surrounding copy must not pad (Rule 8).

### C. The 1-in-100 Mineral
- **The hook** — Chromium is the trace mineral you keep almost none of — form is the whole game.
- **Layout shape** — A comparison-led block: a tight "absorbability ladder" figure as the centrepiece, with a single sentence above naming the stakes and a "so what" line below about form (chelated vs colloidal). No pull quote.
- **Illustration** — A three-rung ladder / three-bar comparison of how much survives absorption: **iodine ≈ 1:1**, **iron 1:10**, **chromium 1:100** — chromium the shortest bar by far. One idea, three marks. (Optionally a single small "form lever" caption: 25% chelated → up to 98% colloidal — as text, not a second figure, to avoid clutter.)
- **Anchored by** — 1:1 / 1:10 / 1:100 ratios [WAL-CLM-RARE-000074]; 50–100 mcg in → 0.25–5 mcg kept, 25% chelated vs 98% colloidal [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091; 0.25–0.5 in WAL-CLM-RARE-000117].
- **Why it wows / best UX** — Answers the reader's unspoken "so why do I need so much?" and directly motivates the daily-target/why-this-number tip. The 1:100 contrast is a striking, self-explaining visual. Distinct from A and B in both layout (comparison ladder) and subject (absorption, not blood sugar or lifespan).

### D. The Insulin Keystone
- **The hook** — Chromium is the small metal core that lets insulin work — a three-piece molecule your blood-sugar control is built on.
- **Layout shape** — A single richly-annotated figure of GTF as the anchor, with a short identity line above and a one-line consequence below ("without it, insulin can't clear blood sugar"). The figure IS the block.
- **Illustration** — GTF drawn as a three-piece assembled badge/keystone: a central **Cr³⁺** stone flanked by **dinicotinic acid (a B3 relative)** and **glutathione**, captioned "= glucose tolerance factor." One assembled object, three labelled parts. (Keep it an emblem, not a wiring diagram — no arrows through labels.)
- **Anchored by** — GTF = chromium-III + dinicotinic acid + glutathione, activates phosphoglucomutase, ~1.5 mg body total [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000087]; "a whole specialty of medicine… wiped out" framing available as the consequence line [WAL-CLM-DDDL-000046].
- **Why it wows / best UX** — The most *educational* concept: it makes the abstract "chromium helps blood sugar" concrete by showing the actual named parts. Good for a reader who wants the mechanism. Distinct illustration (a molecular emblem) from the loop, the bars, and the ladder. Slightly more technical, so lower on immediate wow than A or B.

## 4. Proposed "why this number" (PROPOSAL)

Target: **620 mcg/day.** Wallach's *Epigenetics* daily mineral program lists chromium at **200–400 mcg per 100 lb of body weight** [source claim WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000129]. The build takes the **upper** of that range (400 mcg), scales it from the per-100-lb basis to the app's **154 lb (70 kg) reference** body weight (×1.54), giving 400 × 1.54 = 616, and rounds to **two significant figures → 620 mcg**. It lands in the same neighbourhood as his *Let's Play Doctor* Base-Line program, which lists a 200 mcg daily "true supplement need" and a 300–600 mcg 30-day pharmacologic dose [WAL-CLM-LETS-000049] — so the recommended amount is Wallach-sourced end to end (target.kind = wallach), not a gap. (Worth a one-line nod that ordinary food delivers far less usable chromium than the target — the 1:100 absorption story — so the number reflects what must be *supplied*, not eaten.)

## 5. Gaps / flags

- **Number-vs-verbatim mismatch to respect (Rule 5):** the *claim_text* of WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000062 asserts the "90% deficient," "300% urinary loss," and "33% longer" figures, but that claim's own **verbatim is only the deficiency-disease list** — those three numbers are NOT in its quoted words. Do not pull them by that ID. They ARE independently grounded: 300% → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091 / WAL-CLM-RARE-000117; 90% → WAL-CLM-DDDL-000015 / WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000089 / WAL-CLM-RARE-000118; 33% → WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000090 / WAL-CLM-DDDL-000016 / WAL-CLM-RARE-000124. Cite those.
- **No superseded claims** in the pack; nothing to reconcile there.
- **Solo header: YES, strongly warranted.** Chromium has a signature mechanism (GTF), a signature paradox (the sugar loop), a signature wow-stat (33% lifespan), a signature absorption story (1:100), and a signature curio (infant formula). It is one of the richest single-element palettes in the set — it does not need to fold into or lean on another element.
- **Vanadium is chromium's constant partner** in the corpus (nearly every deficiency/protocol claim names "chromium and vanadium" together). The header should stay chromium-first; vanadium can appear as a supporting mention (e.g. in the loop or deficiency copy) but the figure should not split focus onto two minerals.
- **Protocol volume is huge but low-value for a header** — dozens of Ch10 materia-medica protocols (anxiety, bedwetting, autism, cataracts, Crohn's, depression, insomnia, etc.) all route through "treat the hypoglycemia with chromium." Good breadth evidence that chromium = blood-sugar keystone, but no single protocol is header material.
- **A concept the corpus does NOT support well:** a clean "food sources of chromium" angle — the pack is about supplementation and depletion, and explicitly argues food delivers almost none usable [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091]. Do not build a "eat these foods" figure; it would fight the source.
- **Decline-timeline caution:** the 1948→1985 serum series [WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000092] is genuinely striking, but a decline *rail/timeline* figure is already the selenium/copper visual language — if used, it must be a distinctly different form (e.g. a single collapsing sparkline curio), not another rail. Left out of the four concepts for that reason; available as a curio if a mockup wants it.

## 6. Recommended lead concept

**A — The Sugar Loop.** It is the single most chromium-specific idea in the pack (nothing else in the corpus produces this causal loop), it is instantly personal (everyone knows a sugar craving), the mechanism is counter-intuitive and memorable, and a two-node causal cycle is a figure none of the six shipped headers use. **B (A Third More Life)** is the strongest runner-up for raw wow if Luneth prefers a number-led block.
