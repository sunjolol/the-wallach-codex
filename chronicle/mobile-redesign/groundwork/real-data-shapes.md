# The real shape of the data the mobile UI must hold

Groundwork for the mobile-first redesign. Every number below was **measured**, not estimated. Each
table names the file or the command that produced it, so any of it can be re-derived. Nothing here
adds, changes, or interprets a Wallach amount — this is a measurement of what already exists.

Measured 2026-08-22 against `master` (`dashboard/assets/data/*.json`, bundle
`dashboard/assets/js/dist/main.js` built 2026-08-22 23:14).

Two kinds of measurement appear here:

- **Static** — reading the generated JSON. Scripts live in the session scratchpad and are quoted
  inline so they can be pasted anywhere: `ds_measure.py`, `ds_measure3.py`.
- **Driven** — the real app, launched headless from `file://` at a 390x844 viewport, clicked
  through with Puppeteer. Per the project's own doctrine ("drive the app, don't reason about the
  data"), every count about *coverage states* and *search result volume* below came from the
  running app, never from re-implementing its engine. Probes: `ds_probe.js`, `ds_probe2.js`,
  `ds_probe3.js`.

> Re-derive the static half:
> `cd "<repo>" && PYTHONUTF8=1 python <scratchpad>/ds_measure.py`
> Re-derive the driven half:
> `cd "<repo>" && node <scratchpad>/ds_probe.js` (and `ds_probe2.js`, `ds_probe3.js`)
> The probes require `node_modules/puppeteer` at the repo root; they take the repo path as a
> hardcoded `ROOT` constant at the top of each file.

## 2. Coverage states

`state/coverage.ts:103` defines the type exactly:

```ts
export type CoverageStatus = 'covered' | 'partial' | 'trace' | 'gap' | 'present' | '';
```

Six states, of which `''` is "pending / no target" and renders grey. The rank order used to merge
verdicts is `'' < gap < present < partial < trace < covered` (`STATUS_RANK`, coverage.ts:760). The
five ledger labels rendered on screen are **COVERED, PARTIAL, PRESENT, NOT COVERED, NO WALLACH
NUMBER YET**, and they always sum to 90.

### 2.1 A plausible profile, measured step by step

Driven run (`ds_probe.js`): name entered, one goal picked (`stronger-bones`), then the five pinned
starter-pack products added in curated order, reading the ledger after every add.

| after adding | COVERED | PARTIAL | PRESENT | NOT COVERED | NO WALLACH NUMBER YET | rows |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| (empty regimen) | 5 | 0 | 0 | 35 | 50 | 0 |
| ultimate-classic | 12 | 51 | 0 | 12 | 15 | 1 |
| ultimate-daily | 14 | 50 | 0 | 12 | 14 | 2 |
| beyond-tangy-tangerine-2-5-canister | 63 | 12 | 0 | 11 | 4 | 3 |
| ultimate-efa-plus | 63 | 14 | 0 | 11 | 2 | 4 |
| btt-2-0-tablets | **70** | **9** | 0 | **9** | **2** | 5 |
| (all 215 products) | 85 | 1 | 0 | 4 | 0 | 215 |

The denominator never moves: `.ledger__recon` reads `90 counted · 91 shown` at every step.

**The realistic mobile design target is the 5-row row of that table**: about 70 covered, 9 partial,
9 gap, 2 pending. Not the empty state, and not the all-215 state.

**The empty state is the hostile one for layout**: 50 of 91 tiles are grey/pending and only 5 are
covered. A first-run mobile screen shows a wall of grey. Whatever the mobile design does with an
empty field, it has to make 50 grey tiles read as "not measured yet", not as failure.

### 5.2 The four text fields of a claim card — character counts

This is the height budget. All four render on one card.

| field | n | min | p25 | median | p75 | p90 | p99 | **max** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| `answer_short` | 2,579 | 52 | 141 | **171** | 212 | 260 | 374 | **892** |
| `answer` (full) | 2,579 | 61 | 298 | **436** | 598 | 846 | 1,434 | **2,381** |
| `verbatim` (book quote) | 2,579 | 60 | 169 | **258** | 372 | 501 | 922 | **1,186** |

Every claim has a verbatim — **2,601 of 2,601**, never null.

Two more facts about verbatims that matter for layout:

- They are byte-exact OCR slices with **hard line breaks inside sentences**. Newline count per
  verbatim: median 3, p90 9, **max 57**. If the CSS honours those newlines (`pre-wrap`), a
  1,186-character quote becomes an 18-line block; if it collapses them, the quote reflows but is no
  longer byte-faithful on screen. Both choices are defensible; the design must make one on purpose.
- The longest verbatim is `WAL-CLM-DDDL-000529`: 1,186 chars, 18 hard lines.

The **worst single card** in the corpus is `WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000485`:
question 35 + answer_short 235 + answer 2,381 + verbatim 805 = **3,456 characters on one card**.
At 375px wide, ~38 characters per line at 16px body text, that is roughly **91 lines ≈ 2,200px of
text** for one claim if every field is expanded.

The longest `answer_short` alone is **892 characters** — an "answer_short" that is longer than the
median full answer. `state/search.ts:181` sets `LEDE_MAX = 340`, so the lede is soft-clamped; the
card body is not.

Other per-claim list fields: `essentials` (median 0, p90 2, **max 16**), `conditions` (median 0,
p90 2, **max 29**), `topics` (median 5, p90 7, **max 14**), `also_about` (median 1, p90 3,
**max 16**). 755 of 2,579 claims carry a `page` number; the other 1,824 do not — a citation line
must render both "Book, p.29" and bare "Book".

The calcium essential page carries: 150 claims, 201 distinct claims, 40 synonyms, 93 linked

## LAYOUT IMPLICATIONS

Concrete constraints these measurements impose on any mobile design. Each one is a consequence of a
number above, not a preference.

1. **No fixed-width nutrient chip.** Canon names run 3 to **50** characters and the distribution is
   bimodal — 60 short mineral names, then a tail of 14 long vitamin/omega names. Either the chip
   wraps, or the design commits to the uppercase tile name (max **16**, "PANTOTHENIC ACID") and
   accepts that "VITAMIN D2 (ERGOCALCIFEROL) + D3 (CHOLECALCIFEROL)" appears only on the detail
   surface.

2. **A claim card must survive 3,456 characters.** Median is ~900 across the four fields; the worst
   real card (`WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000485`) is question 35 + short 235 + answer 2,381 + verbatim 805. At
   375px that is roughly 2,200px of text. Progressive disclosure is not a nicety here — it is the
   only way the card fits, and the collapsed state must be driven by `answer_short` (median 171,
   **max 892**), which is itself sometimes longer than a median full answer.

3. **A verbatim block must survive 1,186 characters across 18 hard line breaks.** Verbatims are
   byte-exact OCR with newlines inside sentences. Decide `pre-wrap` vs reflow deliberately and state
   the decision; do not let it be an accident of a `white-space` default.

4. **Search cannot render everything it currently renders.** One query ("calcium") produces 201 rows
   and **268,497 characters** in the panel, 186 rows collapsed but present in the DOM, re-rendered on
   every keystroke. On mobile this needs either virtualization or a hard render cap; the visible
   count is already only 15.

5. **The empty Coverage state is 50 grey tiles out of 91, and 12.3 screens of scroll at 390px.**
   The first thing a new user sees is mostly "no Wallach number yet". The mobile design has to give
   that state its own treatment rather than shipping the same grid dimmed.

6. **Five tiles have a hard ceiling that no product can lift** — chloride, sulfur, silica,
   flavonoids stay NOT COVERED and sodium stays PARTIAL with the entire 215-product catalogue in the
   regimen. Any "close your gaps" affordance must route those five to food, or it will send a user
   shopping for something that does not exist.

7. **56 of 91 essentials have no numeric target.** More than half of all detail screens must render
   "no Wallach target stated" as a first-class state, not an error or an empty slot. Three distinct
   flavours exist and read differently: dietary ("air, water, food, nothing to take"), trace_pdm
   ("one shared group verdict"), and collective (omega-3 + omega-6 share ONE amount). Silver has a
   *ceiling*, which must never render in the same slot as a target.

8. **Every numeric target drags provenance with it.** A book-attribution line plus, for 26 of them,
   `other_claims` from older books. Budget a second block of text under every number — the project
   rule requires it.

9. **A product sheet is a page, not a card.** Up to **129** ingredient rows, up to **34** quantified
   nutrient rows in one component, and a proprietary blend whose `as_labeled` prose reaches **971
   characters**. Names reach 69 characters; prices need 7 digits plus a symbol, twice if both retail
   and wholesale show.

10. **A food card is 4 chips but must stretch to 13**, and its `portion_label` can be 54 characters
    ("1 large (2-1/4 per pound, approx 3-3/4\" long, 3\" dia.)") while its `usda_description` reaches
    106. The short `name` (max 29) is the only field safe in a tight row.

11. **ORAC cannot share one linear axis.** The tables span 949 to 314,446 — a 331x range. The data
    already ships a per-row `bar` percentage and per-category grouping with two different bases (per
    serving vs per 100 g). Keep the grouping; render the basis.

12. **157 hover-only tooltips on one screen is a touch-platform bug waiting to happen.** The gloss
    system emits `.gloss` spans inline in prose (`tabindex="0" role="button"`), 79 on the Absorption
    surface and 157 on ORAC. Glosses average 68 characters and reach 451 — long enough to need a
    sheet, not a tooltip, on a phone.

13. **An entity page must scale from 2 claims to 201.** Median entity claim_count is 2; calcium is
    58 in the search index and 201 distinct on its entity page, with 103 linked conditions. One
    layout has to serve both without looking broken at either end.

14. **The whole dataset is resident in memory at boot** — 13.1 MB of JSON inside a 14.1 MB bundle,
    no fetch, no service worker. Mobile perf work belongs in render volume and DOM node counts (the
    Conditions surface alone builds 3,059 nodes; Explore builds **149 buttons**), never in loading
    strategy.

15. **The ledger always sums to 90 and shows 91.** `90 counted · 91 shown` is a real string the app
    renders and omega-9 is the reason. Any mobile summary that prints a single number has to carry
    that reconciliation, or it will silently claim 91 essentials.

## What I could not determine

- **"How many results does a *typical* query return"** has no single answer, because the app resolves
  most single-word queries to an *entity page* (hero + family groups) rather than a flat result list.
  I measured seven real queries spanning that behaviour rather than inventing an average. Queries
  that resolve to nothing (`zzz`) render 92 characters and no groups.
- **Per-surface rendered height on the Knowledge drawer** — my probe's root element reported
  `scrollHeight == clientHeight` for the drawer because the scrolling element is an inner child I did
  not identify. The text-volume and DOM-node counts in §8.1 are sound; the pixel heights for
  Knowledge sub-surfaces are not, and are omitted. Coverage's 9,631px is measured on
  `.app-workspace` and is reliable.
- **Whether the 4 unreachable tiles (chloride, sulfur, silica, flavonoids) are unreachable by
  design or by data gap** is a Wallach/curation question, not a data-shape question. I measured the
  ceiling; I did not attempt to explain it, and no one should read a health conclusion into it.
- **`entity-copy.json` has `conditions: {}`** — an empty object where essentials (36) and topics
  (143) are populated. I did not determine whether that is intentional or an unfilled slot.
- **`product-recommender-data.json` covers 51 essentials**, not 90. I did not trace which 51 or why;
  it may be exactly the set with a numeric target plus group members, but I did not verify that and
  will not guess.
