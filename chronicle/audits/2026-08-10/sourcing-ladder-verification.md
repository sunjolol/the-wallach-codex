## VERDICT

**The ladder holds.** Metallic → chelated → plant-derived colloidal is Wallach's own, stated in his
own words, with his own numbers, in **five of the seven books** (Rare Earths 1994, Let's Play Doctor
1995 [protocol mentions only], Dead Doctors Don't Lie 2011, Epigenetics 2014, Immortality 2008,
Hell's Kitchen 2015). It is safe to build on.

**But the document that asserted it — `dashboard/components/trace-mineral-tile-detail.html` — carried
a wrong citation and an unattributed derivation.** Both fixed 2026-08-10. It is a design-reference
file, not a shipped surface — nothing was reaching users. Details, and a correction to my own
over-stated first framing of it, in §4.

And the sweep turned up something the ladder as drawn does not capture, which changes the shape of
the whole system: **"colloidal" on a label is not the winner.** §2.

## 1. THE LADDER, AS HE ACTUALLY STATES IT

| Tier | Wallach's figure | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Metallic** | **8–12%** in younger animals and humans; **plummets to 3–5% after age 35–40** | Immortality p.88 (`WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000292`, `-000310`) · Hell's Kitchen p.216 (`WAL-CLM-HELLS-000017`) · Rare Earths p.213 (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000061`) |
| **Chelated** | **40–65%**, which he calls "a 400% increase over the availability of metallic minerals" | Immortality p.88 (`WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000023`) · Hell's Kitchen p.217 |
| **Plant-derived colloidal** | **up to 98%** | all six books; canonical statement at Rare Earths p.213 (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000061`) |

His single cleanest sentence, byte-exact (Rare Earths, `WAL-CLM-RARE-000061`):

> Organic colloidal minerals derived from plants are 98 % available to humans as compared to 8 to 12 % availability for the metallic minerals.

**The taxonomy chapter is Rare Earths ch. 10** ("1. METALLIC MINERALS / 2. CHELATED MINERALS /
3. COLLOIDAL MINERALS", pp. 255–257), restated in Immortality pp. 88–92, Hell's Kitchen pp. 216–221,
and Epigenetics ~p. 647. **Let's Play Doctor carries no taxonomy at all** — only protocol-line
mentions ("plant derived colloidal minerals are 98% absorbable and give excellent results!").

### What he says metallic *means*, on a label
His own examples, so a layman can read a label (Hell's Kitchen p.216, Immortality p.88):
gluconate · lactate · sulphate · carbonate · oxide · limestone · coral calcium · dolomite · oyster
shell · eggshell · sea water · Great Salt Lake water · "rock flour" · clay · sea-bed minerals ·
and antacids such as Rollaids and Tums.

### What he says chelated *means*, on a label
An amino-acid suffix: **selenomethionine · chromium picolinate · selenium aspartate**, or the words
**"amino acid chelate"** (Immortality p.88, Rare Earths p.256, Hell's Kitchen p.217).

### The arithmetic he uses to make it visceral
Calcium carbonate, 1,000 mg tablet → 400 mg elemental calcium; at a 10% availability rate you net
**80 mg**. To get 2,000 mg usable you would need **25 capsules a day** (Immortality p.88,
`WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000292`). Epigenetics runs the same argument on calcium lactate and lands on
**90 tablets a day** — "and you would still have to supplement with an additional 59 minerals."

## 2. ★★★ THE FINDING THAT CHANGES THE SYSTEM — "colloidal" is not one thing

Hell's Kitchen pp. 219–220 and Immortality p.92 (`WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000026`) give **four kinds of
colloidal mineral supplement**, and only one of them is the 98% form:

1. **Unprotected colloids — bare "rock flour."** Sea-bed minerals, clays, "soils," and "glacial
   milk." In his own words: **"This form of inorganic colloid is in fact a metallic mineral"** and is
   only available *to plants*, and only in microbe-healthy soil.
2. **Organic mineral colloid from living systems** — bacteria, fungi, green food crops, animals,
   humans — coated in a hydrophilic substance (gelatin, albumin, albuminoids, collagen). This coating
   makes it a "crystalloid," **"maximizing its bioavailability up to 98 percent."** ← **the winner**
3. **Carbon-coated** (a 10–12 carbon chain), also from living systems, "thought to be the most stable
   form of natural organic mineral colloid." **He gives no percentage for this one.**
4. **Industrial** — metallic colloid coated with sulfated castor oil to form commercial detergents.
   **"not found in nature."**

**Why this is the system's centrepiece:** a product can say *colloidal* on the label and be type 1 —
which is metallic by his own classification. Sea-bed mineral, clay and "glacial milk" products are
sold as colloidal today. This is the sharpest "search for X / AVOID Y" he gives, and the current tile
does not carry it.

He proves it on the Hunza themselves (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000072`, Rare Earths p.253):

> The long lived centenarians drink the heavily mineralized water containing large quantities of "rock flour" known as "Glacial Milk" to be sure; however, they are only able to absorb and biologically utilize from five to 12 per cent of these suspended metallic colloids (ground up rocks).

The longevity does not come from drinking the mineral water. It comes from **irrigating with it** —
the plants do the conversion. That is the whole thesis of plant-derived, and it is a far better
teaching device than a bar chart.

### Humic shale — the vehicle he actually endorses
Prehistoric plants dried under volcanic ash without fossilizing; ground to a flour and soaked 3–4
weeks in filtered spring water (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000319`, `WAL-CLM-HELLS-000069`–`72`).
⚠ **The two books disagree on its numbers** — see §5.

metallic bad" would be misrepresenting him.

**The vehicle dose** (the one Wallach number the whole colloidal argument terminates in):
**1 fl oz of liquid plant-derived colloidal minerals per 100 lb of body weight per day**
(`WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000089`, Epigenetics). This is already the goal model in
`dashboard/assets/data/trace-mineral-vehicles.json`, which is clean — it holds editorial judgment
only and joins amounts from the sealed pillars.

## 4. DEFECTS IN `trace-mineral-tile-detail.html` — ① and ② FIXED 2026-08-10

⚠ **CORRECTION TO MY OWN FRAMING.** An earlier draft of this section was headed *"DEFECTS ON THE
LIVE SURFACE."* **That was wrong.** `dashboard/components/` is a folder of design-reference and
PROPOSAL files; `dashboard/dashboard.html` never loads it, and the only `src/` mentions are code
comments pointing at it as the design bar. **Nothing here was shipping to a user.** It still matters
— this file is *the BAR* other surfaces copy from, and it is the document the sourcing ladder was
asserted from — but the severity was overstated and is corrected here.

**① The citation was wrong. FIXED.** The 98% stat read *"per Let's Play Doctor, p. 82."* LPD p. 82
(lines 4327–4390) is the alphabetical materia medica — ALCOHOLISM, ALLERGIES, ALLERGIC SHINERS,
ALZHEIMERS, ALOPECIA. It contains no 98% figure and no taxonomy. LPD's 98% mentions sit on printed
pp. 86, 95, 131, 132 and 138, all inside treatment protocols.

*How the page mapping was validated, since the whole finding rests on it:* LPD's bare-number lines
were tested as page markers — **164 of them, spanning p.16→p.203, with ZERO non-monotonic
transitions.** 17 markers are missing (OCR), but p.82 and p.83 both survive, so page 82's content is
fully bounded and provably contains no 98% statement.

→ Now cites **Rare Earths, p. 213** (`WAL-CLM-RARE-000061`).

⚠ *Why that page and not a better-placed one:* the ch.10 four-forms passage carries the ideal
sentence (*"maximizing its bioavailability to 98 %"*, line 20671), but **I could not honestly derive
its printed page.** Testing 225 sealed Rare Earths claims, `recorded_page − marker_before` is **+1 on
134 of them but +0 on 25**, including `RARE-000319` mined from that very passage — the scan's markers
sit at top or bottom inconsistently. Any page number I computed would have been a guess. So the
citation points at a page **the corpus itself already records**, not one I derived.

**② The multiplier line. FIXED — but my first description of it overstated the case.** I reported it
as *"a number Wallach never wrote."* Literally true, but it implied fabrication: *"eight to twelve
times"* is **arithmetically correct** (98 ÷ 12 = 8.2, 98 ÷ 8 = 12.25). It was a sound derivation from
his own two figures, not an invention.

The genuine defect is narrower: it is an **unattributed derivation sitting inside a citation's
scope**, and its numerals **collide with a different quantity of his** — 8–12% is the metallic
absorption *rate*, not a multiplier — so a reader meets "8–12" twice meaning two different things.

→ Replaced with his own comparison instead of our arithmetic: *"Against 8–12% for the metallic
category — his own comparison, in the same sentence."* True, his, and no collision, because the
number is now labelled as what it actually is. Both figures live in the single sentence of
`RARE-000061`, which is why one citation now covers the whole stat.

**③ The chelated tier is blank where he has a number.** The tile shows only "Intermediate."
Wallach says **40–65%** (§5b). Left alone — a gap, not an error, and outside what was authorised.

**④ "livestock-industry origin, 1960s" — CORRECT. Do not change it.** I flagged this as contested
and would have moved it to 1940s under `favor-newest`. **Luneth's ruling (§5a) is that 1960s is
right and 1940s is flat wrong**, so the flag was the error, not the tile. Left untouched.

**⑤ The pull quote is very slightly softened.** Cited to Hell's Kitchen p. 207 — **the page is
correct** (verified, marker at line 7151, quote at 7171–7174). But his sentence ends in an

## 5. CROSS-BOOK CONFLICTS — RULED BY LUNETH 2026-08-10

**(a) When were chelated minerals invented? → 1960s. RULED.**

| Book | Year | Says |
|---|---|---|
| Rare Earths | 1994 | **1960's** |
| Immortality | 2008 | 1940s |
| Epigenetics | 2014 | **1960s** |
| Hell's Kitchen | 2015 | 1940s |

Luneth's ruling, from outside knowledge: *"it began in the 1950s and by the early 1960s it had been
developed. 1940s is flat wrong."* The app shows **1960s**, citing `WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000060`.

★ **This is a case where `favor-newest-wallach-number` LOSES.** The two newest books carry the wrong
date. `books-are-riddled-use-outside-info` outranks it: recency only breaks ties between statements
that are each defensible. Where outside knowledge shows one side is simply wrong, recency is
irrelevant. The 1940s claims stay byte-exact in the corpus — the corpus is not wrong, the books are.

**(b) Chelated bioavailability → 40–65%. RULED.** Rare Earths 1994's flat 40% (with the septic-tank
line) is superseded by Immortality 2008 and Hell's Kitchen 2015. Clean `favor-newest` supersession —
both readings are defensible, so recency decides. Contrast with (a).

**(c) Humic shale → NO CONTRADICTION. My error.**
I read "no less than 60" (Rare Earths) against "no less than seventy seven" (Hell's Kitchen) as a
revision. **Both are FLOORS, not counts** — 77 satisfies "no less than 60", so the two statements are
consistent, and the extract simply carries additional minerals beyond the 60. Luneth: *"the total
amount is 77 but not all 77 are part of the 60 minerals, there's additional ones not part of the 60."*

⚠ **Concentration (38 g/L vs 19,000 mg/quart) is OUT OF SCOPE** for the sourcing ladder and was me
wandering off-task. It is not part of this system and should not be raised again under it.

**(d) Chromium usable-yield → OCR defect. RULED, and CORRECTED.** Rare Earths 1994 and Epigenetics
2014 both read **0.25 to 0.5 µg** in an otherwise word-for-word identical sentence; Immortality 2008
read **0.25 to 5 µg** — a dropped "0.". Corrected under `source-correction-policy`; see §6.

## 6. DEFECTS IN THE MINED CORPUS

**① `WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000023` states the wrong war.** Its `claim_text` says calcium EDTA was
"a German **pre-WWI** antidote." **All four books that carry the sentence say WWII** (Immortality
l.4155, Hell's Kitchen l.7419, Rare Earths l.20392, Epigenetics l.21359). The verbatim is correct;
only the authored answer is wrong. This is the class of defect no gate catches — `claim_text` is not
byte-checked against the book.

**② `WAL-CLM-RARE-000072`'s verbatim does not carry its own headline number.** The claim_text leads
with "5 to 12%" but the stored verbatim is a *different* sentence from the same passage. The real
sentence is at Rare Earths line 20156 and is quoted in §2 above. **Verbatim-widening job — the claim
is true, the anchor is just pointing at the wrong sentence.**

⚠ *Method note on ②:* my first probe reported this number as absent from the corpus entirely. It was
a bad probe — the book writes "five to 12 per cent", mixing a word and a numeral, which my pattern
missed. This is exactly gotcha #5 in CLAUDE.md. I only caught it by re-running with a negative
control. Had I reported the first result, I would have handed you a fabrication finding on a claim
that is perfectly sound.

**③ None of this material is approved.** All **66** claims tagged with a form/absorption tag are
`review_state: "draft"`. The entire evidentiary base of the new system is unreviewed. Given
`no-ceremony-pragmatism` I am not proposing a ceremony, but you should know the state before the
system quotes them at users.

**⑤ Immortality's chromium yield was an OCR defect — CORRECTED 2026-08-10 under his ruling.**
`eden/corpus/books/immortality.txt` read *"supplies only 0.25 / to 5 ug of usable and retainable
chromium"*; Rare Earths and Epigenetics carry the identical sentence with **0.5**. Corrected to

`WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000091`'s `claim_text` corrected from `0.25-5 mcg`, and the draft re-synced.
**`draft_offset_failures() == []` verified before handing the seal over.**

⚠ *Method note:* the drafts were **0/446 failing before the edit and 249/446 after** — a 2-byte
insertion shifts every downstream offset. I confirmed that split against `git show HEAD:` rather than
assuming, because the failure list spans the whole book and looks alarming. This is the documented

**④ Tag hygiene will break any tag-driven query.** The same concept is spelled several ways:

## 7. HONEST GAPS — what he does NOT say

- **No percentage for the carbon-coated colloid** (type 3), even though he calls it the most stable.
- **No general chelated figure per element** — the 40–65% is a class statement; per-element chelated
  numbers exist only for vanadium (40%), chromium (25%) and phosphorus (40–50%).
- **No brand names, ever.** He endorses a *category* and a *vehicle* (humic-shale extract), never a
  product. The standing rule in `recommend-generic-supplements-when-ygy-cannot-reach` is consistent
  with the corpus.
- **No guidance on the forms that dominate shelves today** — glycinate, malate, citrate as *chelates*,
  liposomal, "ionic," picometer. He names citrate only in the calcium elemental-content table, as a
  metallic. Any advice about a modern form he did not name would be outside §00.A.
- **No absorption figure for most of the 90.** The per-element table in §3 is eleven elements. For the
  rest the honest answer is "no Wallach form claim stated."
