# Salt — first blind eval of v2.1, presentation upgrade to v2.2

## The questions

**Q1 (blind eval, agent had not seen the answer key):**
> *"Is salt actually bad for you or is it just a meme? Should I cut back? Should I worry about it raising my blood pressure?"*

**Q2 (gotcha probe, follow-up):**
> *"Is there any chance too much salt causes kidney stones?"*

Q2 was designed to test whether the agent would collapse to "salt is fine per Wallach" reflexively and miss the actual nuance.

## The "best known correct answer"

Wallach's position on salt is **strongly pro-salt** with a U-shaped risk curve and a calcium-status root cause that explains both ends of the curve. Resolution:

1. **Substance.** Sodium is on the 90 essentials list. Supplement-table baseline (*Let's Play Doctor*): 300–3,000 mg/day. *Rare Earths* Na entry: Na/K/Cl are three indispensable electrolytes with four core physiological functions.
2. **Dose.** Wallach acknowledges sodium toxicity (CHF, edema, hypertension at extreme excess — *Let's Play Doctor* SODIUM TOXICITY list) but emphasizes restriction as the bigger risk. He cites 1997 Sodium Task Force: 1g/day restriction → 600% more heart attacks vs 2g+/day. Average western intake (5–12 g/day) and Japanese intake (28 g/day) both treated as fine.
3. **Source.** Form barely matters. His only form-level preference is iodine content. Iodized table salt, sea salt, Himalayan, seasoned — all acceptable.
4. **Context.** Default: "Salt your food to taste" (*Hell's Kitchen* p.257). Pregnancy: explicitly against restriction (eclampsia risk per DDDL). 1993 Chicago heat-wave: low-salt diets killed people. Hypertension specifically: **~85% calcium deficiency, not salt excess** (DDDL "Lost Video"). Salt sensitivity exists per *Rare Earths* but upstream cause is Ca/K restriction, not salt itself — fix Ca/K. Mechanism behind anti-restriction stance: salt is raw material for HCl → restriction causes hypochlorhydria → mineral malabsorption + B12 deficiency cascade.

**On kidney stones (Q2):** salt IS a contributor in his framework but as an aggravator, not a root cause:
- *DDDL Appendix A:* heavy salting raises urinary Ca loss 96 → 148 mg/day. Kidney stones listed under "Common Calcium Deficiency Diseases" alongside osteoporosis, bone spurs, deposits.
- *Hell's Kitchen p.257:* low salt also causes kidney stones via hypochlorhydria → impaired mineral absorption → deficiency.
- **U-shaped** for stone risk; calcium status is the root cause at both ends.
- Fix: calcium repletion + Ca:P ratio toward 2:1, not salt restriction.
- **Framework gap:** Wallach discusses calcium oxalate only as a GI absorption blocker (spinach, rhubarb, chard binding Ca in gut), never as the urinary precipitation chemistry that mainstream urology identifies as the most common stone-forming mechanism. **Unknown** in corpus for that specific pathway.

**Net answer to Q1:**
- *Bad?* No — essential. The "salt causes hypertension" framing is what he explicitly rejects across the entire corpus.
- *Cut back?* No. Restriction is what he warns against.
- *Worry about BP?* In his framework, no — fix calcium status instead.

## Lesson

The fluoride retrospective (v2.0) fixed the **reasoning** failure modes. The salt eval revealed the next layer of failure: even when reasoning is correct, **presentation can destroy value**. Dense, comprehensive, citation-laden answers feel rigorous but produce skimming. v2.2 separates form from substance: substance discipline holds (Pre-Answer Checklist still runs the same gates), but presentation defaults to plain English bottom-line first with scannable exceptions for the contexts that flip the default.

Also: the gotcha-probe pattern (Q2 testing whether Q1 missed a case) is a sign the agent should have anticipated the case in Q1. v2.2's "Anticipate the exception" rule operationalizes this. If a question keeps producing follow-up gotchas, the original answer wasn't doing its job.

## Verdict

v2.1: **Substance pass. Form fail.**
v2.2: queued. Should preserve substance (no Pre-Answer Checklist changes) while replacing dense Topic Brief output with Quick Answer + scannable Exceptions. Future brain versions must outperform v2.2 on form (concise, scannable, exceptions-surfaced-proactively) without regressing substance (corpus coverage, U-shape detection, framework-gap flagging).
