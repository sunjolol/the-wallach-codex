Wallach Corpus Trace Minerals

Not how much. From where.

In Wallach's framework, the question for the thirty-five trace minerals isn't milligrams. It's the source category itself. Three direct passages from the corpus, set side by side.

01
The baseline number

Sixty essentials, one source.

Wallach names the count explicitly. The framework's mineral baseline is sixty — not a figure he arrives at theoretically, but the floor below which he says human biology decompensates. The source he names for those sixty is equally specific.

PQ·01a

We require a minimum of sixty minerals to be optimally healthy, physically fit and to maximize our life spans. Unfortunately, with the advent of electric, gas and propane cooking stoves and heating furnaces, we have given up the traditional and ideal source of nutritional minerals — plant minerals derived from the wood ashes that supported the human need for minerals since before written history.

Hell's Kitchen · pp. 207–210

The point lands structurally: modern food doesn't carry the mineral spectrum it once did because the agricultural soil and wood-ash cooking mechanisms that delivered it were replaced. The cure isn't to enumerate sixty minerals on a label. It's to restore the source.

02
The pivot

Not quantity. Absorption.

If the source matters more than the number, the metric that matters isn't intake. It's what your body actually picks up. Wallach is sharp on this:

PQ·02a

It doesn't matter how many minerals or how much of each mineral you consume — what counts is how many minerals and how much of each mineral you absorb.

Hell's Kitchen · p. 207

This is why the dashboard's coverage rule for the thirty-five trace minerals is binary — a plant-derived source is in your regimen, or it isn't. Per-element milligram targets would misrepresent the corpus.
