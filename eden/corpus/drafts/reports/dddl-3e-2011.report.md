### WAL-CLM-DDDL-000295 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach describes cerebrovascular disease -- what he equates with senile dementia -- as more common in the United States than anywhere else. It develops when the middle cerebral artery (the brain's main blood supply) 'hardens,' cutting oxygen to the brain and producing loss of memory and the typical 'senile' changes. As with arteriosclerosis elsewhere in the body, that hardening comes from smooth-muscle scarring followed by calcification and fatty cholesterol deposits. He flags elevated blood cholesterol as a risk factor because cholesterol is a building block of vitamin D, which he considers angiotoxic (toxic to blood vessels) -- in his view it scars arterial smooth muscle and drives further calcification. He adds that magnesium deficiency causes 'malignant calcification' of the cerebral arteries, so optimal magnesium (with all nutrients) is protective. In Wallach's words: "CEREBROVASCULAR DISEASE (senile dementia) is more common in the United States than anywhere else. The “hardening” of the middle cerebral artery (the main blood supply to the brain) results in poor oxygen supply to the brain, which causes loss of memory and typical “senile” changes."

- **claim:** Wallach defines Alzheimer's disease as a 'physician-caused' disease -- one he blames on the medical campaign against dietary cholesterol and saturated fat (what he calls 'cholesterol and saturated fat bashing').
