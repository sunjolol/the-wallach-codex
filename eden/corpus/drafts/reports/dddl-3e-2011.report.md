### WAL-CLM-DDDL-000135 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Selenium's job is to keep the fats in your cells from oxidizing; vegetable oils are polyunsaturated fats that oxidize easily — especially once heated or hydrogenated into trans fats. So Wallach's warning is about the combination: flood the body with rancidity-prone oils while selenium (the antioxidant that would protect them) runs low, and the oils go rancid inside your cells, driving free-radical damage. He calls high vegetable-oil intake concurrent with selenium deficiency “the quickest route to a heart attack and cancer,” and ties the same pairing — including too much fish oil — to birth defects, arteriosclerosis, cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death. The practical point: to him these oils aren't just empty calories, they actively consume the protection selenium is meant to provide.

### WAL-CLM-DDDL-000136 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** To Wallach, fibromyalgia isn't an unexplained chronic-pain condition but a nutritional muscle disease under a modern name — the same thing veterinarians call white muscle disease or stiff lamb disease, and what he terms adult-onset muscular dystrophy. He notes it was eliminated from lambs, calves, and pigs back in 1957 through nutrition. In humans he frames it as a multiple-deficiency disease with a dietary trigger: a high intake of fried foods and vegetable oils (margarine, cooking oils) combined with deficiencies of selenium, vitamin E, and the sulfur-bearing amino acids. The logic mirrors his selenium thesis throughout — rancidity-prone oils flooding in while the antioxidants that would protect the muscle are missing — so his answer to the pain is to correct the deficiencies and cut the oils, not to mask symptoms with muscle relaxants.
