### WAL-CLM-DDDL-000174 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach describes calcium as essential to all living organisms and the fifth most abundant mineral in the earth's crust and biosphere. It is also the most abundant mineral in the human body: about 99 percent of it forms the hydroxyapatite crystal that gives bones and teeth their structure, while the remaining one percent works as a cofactor and activator — regulating the electrochemical functions of cells, releasing energy from ATP (adenosine triphosphate) for muscle contraction, driving blood clotting, triggering neurotransmitter release, and helping regulate the heartbeat. He argues that humans are biochemically designed for high-calcium diets, that modern American diets are critically deficient in it, and that supplementation is the only practical way to get enough.
- **verbatim** (offset 495410): “Calcium is essential for all organisms and is found in the cell walls of
plants, all calcareous tissues, and mammalian bones. Calcium also helps
regulate electrochemical functions in cells and activates several enzymes.
Calcium is the fifth most abundant mineral element in the earth’s crust and
biosphere.”

### WAL-CLM-DDDL-000181 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach argues there is clear evidence humans are designed to consume high-calcium diets, pointing to the late Paleolithic (35,000 to 10,000 years ago) as the last time humans lived in the environment they were biochemically designed for. The agricultural revolution 10,000 years ago narrowed the variety of wild foods while raising calorie intake, permanently lowering dietary minerals, trace minerals, and rare earths. Wild plants and game would have supplied Stone Age humans roughly 1,600 mg of calcium at rest and 2,000–3,000 mg at working energy levels, whereas 20th-century American adults take in only one-fifth to one-third as much — a median of 300–508 mg a day for women and just 680 mg for men.
- **verbatim** (offset 495717): “There is evidence that clearly shows humans are designed to
consume and use high calcium diets. The late Paleolithic period of 35,000
to 10,000 years ago was the most recent time that our human forebears
lived in the environment for which they had been biochemically designed.
The agricultural revolution occurred 10,000 years ago and it reduced the
wide variety of wild foods in the human food chain while increased food
energy (calories). These dietary changes universally and forever decreased
man’s dietary intake of minerals, trace minerals, and rare earths. The
uncultivated food plants and wild game commonly available to Stone Age
humans would supply 1600 mg at basal energy intakes and between 2,000
and 3,000 mg of calcium at the energy levels required to support hunting
and work.
During the 20th century, American adults have a calcium intake of only
one-fifth to one-third as much as did Stone Age humans. The National
Health & Nutrition Examination Survey II reported a median calcium
intake for American women of between 300 and 508 mg per day and only
680 mg for men.”
