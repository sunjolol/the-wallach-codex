### WAL-CLM-DDDL-000275 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Conventional nutrition recognizes nine classically essential amino acids -- histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, and valine -- which the body cannot make and must take in daily. Wallach argues that list is incomplete. He adds three more, arginine, taurine, and tyrosine, on the grounds that over the long haul a deficiency of each produces a specific disease: cancer for arginine, macular degeneration for taurine, and goiter for tyrosine. So while tyrosine is technically 'non-essential' because the body can synthesize it, Wallach treats it as essential in practice, because going without it long-term has real disease consequences.
- **verbatim** (offset 493170): “To the classic list of essential amino
acids, I would add arginine, taurine, and tyrosine. Over the long haul, these
three amino acids help prevent certain specific diseases. Respectively, those
diseases are cancer, macular degeneration, and goiter.”

### WAL-CLM-DDDL-000276 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** The thyroid is Wallach's main reason for calling tyrosine essential. In combination with the mineral iodine, tyrosine is the amino acid the body manufactures into the thyroid hormone thyroxin, which governs metabolism. Because of that, Wallach lists goiter -- an enlarged, struggling thyroid -- as a simple deficiency disease caused by a shortage of nutrients including iodine, copper, molybdenum, and the amino acid tyrosine. Supply the missing raw materials, tyrosine among them, and the thyroid has what it needs to make its hormone.
- **verbatim** (offset 779958): “GOITER (colloid goiter, hypothyroid) is caused by a simple deficiency of
nutrients (i.e. iodine, copper, molybdenum, the amino acid tyrosine, etc.).”
