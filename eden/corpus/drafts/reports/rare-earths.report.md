- **claim:** Silver nitrate — historically called 'lunar caustic,' a name rooted in silver's ancient association with the moon (the same link behind words like 'moonstruck' and 'lunacy') — has a long medicinal history. In 1637 Angelus Sala used it as a purgative and for brain and uterine illness, and Robert Boyle rolled silver nitrate and nitre into bread-crust pills the size of small peas as a laxative. Beginning in 1826, John Higginbottom of Nottingham, England used lunar caustic to treat wounds and ulcers (from needles, hooks, bayonets, saws, leech and rabid-animal bites, and insect stings), to remove tumors, and to cauterize surgical wounds, and later described its use against erysipelas, varicose ulcers, diabetic (senile) gangrene, and burns. Its most enduring application came in 1884, when the German obstetrician Carl Sigmund Crede began placing a single drop of 2% silver nitrate in each newborn's eyes at birth to prevent gonorrheal conjunctivitis (ophthalmia neonatorum); a diluted 1/2% solution became a standard burn treatment only after Carl A. Moyer's work in 1965.
