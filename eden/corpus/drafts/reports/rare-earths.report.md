### WAL-CLM-RARE-000381 · mechanism

- **claim:** Tyrosine is the starting material the body uses to make melanin, the pigment that colors hair and skin. The enzyme tyrosinase acts on tyrosine to build the pigment, and this reaction requires copper as a cofactor. Wallach points out the practical consequence: when copper is deficient, the reaction stalls and hair turns white, silver, or gray. He also notes that red hair is a different case -- its pigment (tricosiverin) is an iron-based compound rather than ordinary melanin.

### WAL-CLM-RARE-000382 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** The bond between tyrosine and the thyroid has a discovery story. Wallach recounts that in the first quarter of the twentieth century, after low iodine content had been tied to endemic goiter, a chemist he names as Kenall isolated a crystalline complex of iodine and the amino acid tyrosine -- roughly 65% iodine -- and named it thyroxin, the thyroid hormone. It was direct chemical proof that the thyroid's hormone is built from iodine bonded to tyrosine, which is why Wallach counts a tyrosine shortage among the causes of goiter.
