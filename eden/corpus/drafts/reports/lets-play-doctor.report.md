- **claim:** Wallach defines malnutrition as occurring either as an overt nutritional deficiency, secondary to extended use of medications that interfere with nutrient absorption, or as malabsorption from celiac disease or low stomach acid (hypochlorhydria). ### WAL-CLM-LETS-000353 · protocol
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** measles · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Measles (rubeola) is a highly contagious viral disease of sudden onset with cough, nasal drainage, conjunctivitis, Koplik's spots (eruptions on the mouth and lip lining), and a pimple-like rash starting on the head and neck and spreading to the body, arms and legs; fever to 104°F is common, and pneumonia and encephalitis are unusual but potentially fatal complications. Wallach notes it now affects mainly teenagers and young adults, incubates 7-14 days (the diagnostic Koplik's spots appear about four days after the fever), and that having had measles confers lifelong immunity. His treatment is topical over-the-counter anti-itch products (for example Caladryl) plus herbs: salves of marigold (Calendula officinalis), and taken orally columbine (Aquilegia vulgaris), yarrow (Achillea millefolium) and pleurisy root (Asclepias tuberosa).

### WAL-CLM-LETS-000354 · protocol
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** rubella · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** German measles (rubella, three-day measles) is a contagious viral disease that causes mild symptoms in children, including swollen lymph nodes of the head and neck; its incubation is 14-21 days and overt symptoms may be absent in teenagers and adults. The typical rash appears first on the face and spreads quickly to the body and limbs with a general flush like scarlet fever; adults may have enlarged head and neck lymph nodes and adult males may have brief testicular pain. Wallach's treatment is the same symptomatic therapy used for ordinary (nine-day) measles. He states emphatically (in capitals) that rubella is a dangerous disease in pregnant women that can induce spontaneous abortions and birth defects, and gives his editorial opinion that rubella vaccines will cause similar birth defects and so should be avoided during pregnancy.

### WAL-CLM-LETS-000356 · protocol
