### WAL-CLM-LETS-000473 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach's guidance on when and how to use a medical specialist. One of your responsibilities as your own primary health care provider is recognizing when you genuinely need a specialist (e.g. an orthopedic surgeon if you are run over by a concrete truck) versus when you do not (you don't need an ear-eye-nose-and-throat specialist for an occasional earache). If a specialist's skills are required, he advises getting three or more opinions from three or more different specialists — including one from a totally different field, who is more likely to give an objective view — just as you would get three bids for car body repair. Ask the specialist to let you speak with ten of his patients who went through the procedure (to learn whether they survived and whether he has even done it before). Start at the conservative end; the more 'exotic' the specialist, the greater the risk. Your chiropractor, naturopathic physician, or even your veterinarian can refer you.
