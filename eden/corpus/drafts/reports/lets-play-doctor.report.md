### WAL-CLM-LETS-000358 · protocol
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** meningitis · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Meningitis (infection of the covering of the brain and spinal cord) can be caused by many organisms — bacteria (Neisseria meningitidis, Haemophilus influenzae, Streptococcus/Diplococcus pneumoniae, Listeria monocytogenes), fungi, and hundreds of viruses. Wallach stresses it is especially dangerous because infants often show only fever and lethargy, and the blood-brain barrier keeps most medications out of the meninges and brain. Symptoms vary but usually include sore throat, fever, headache, stiff neck and vomiting, and children and adults can become critically ill within 6-24 hours of the first symptoms. He warns emphatically that this is NOT a self-help disease: if you suspect meningitis, get the patient to an emergency room immediately and demand help, because patients often die after waiting hours in a waiting room. Treatment requires rapid diagnosis (spinal tap) and injection of antibiotics into the subdural space (under the meninges), total intravenous fluid and electrolyte support, and frequently mechanical respirators.

### WAL-CLM-LETS-000360 · protocol
- **essentials:** copper · **conditions:** menkes_syndrome · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Menkes kinky hair syndrome is considered a genetic disease by conventional pediatricians, but Wallach argues it is a copper-deficiency malabsorption problem of early infancy (for example from celiac disease); he cites the veterinary parallel that copper deficiency causes 'kinky wool' disease. Features include retarded growth, anemia, progressive brain degeneration, sparse brittle hair, loss of hair color, arterial aneurysms and scurvy-like bone disease; hair analysis shows very low copper, and blood shows low copper and low ceruloplasmin. His treatment addresses the malabsorption (avoid wheat, cow's milk and soy) and gives copper intravenously at 200 micrograms per kilogram per day, then copper orally at 1-2 milligrams per day after symptoms improve.
