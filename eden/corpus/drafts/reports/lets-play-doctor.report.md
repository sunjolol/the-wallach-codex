- **claim:** Ornithosis (parrot fever, also called psittacosis) is an infectious disease caused by the bacterium Chlamydia psittaci. Symptoms include fever, chills, weakness, loss of appetite, and a dry cough that becomes productive; early on it can be mistaken for flu, Legionnaires' disease, or Q fever, so exposure to birds (especially sick ones) is the key diagnostic clue, confirmed by a blood test. Treatment is the antibiotic tetracycline by mouth at 250-500 milligrams four times a day for 10-14 days, with the cough controlled by codeine and strict, enforced bed rest — untreated, parrot fever can be fatal.

- **claim:** Osteitis fibrosa (nutritional secondary hyperparathyroidism), also known as Paget's disease of bone, is a bone disease — more common in men — in which hard bone is lost and replaced by fibrous connective tissue. Mainstream medicine calls the cause unknown, but in animals it comes from a reversed dietary calcium-to-phosphorus ratio together with relatively large amounts of vitamin D. It is diagnosed by x-ray and by elevated blood alkaline phosphatase and parathyroid hormone.
