### WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000101 · mechanism
- **essentials:** copper · **conditions:** enzootic_ataxia · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** One of the earliest proofs that copper deficiency causes neurological disease. In 1937, 'enzootic ataxia' in newborn lambs — also called sway back or lamkruis, a loss of muscle coordination — was recognized as a copper deficiency in pregnant sheep. Giving the ewes copper supplements prevented it. The disease was marked by demyelination (loss of the protective myelin sheath around nerves) in the cerebellum (the brain's coordination center) and the spinal cord. In his words: "Neonatal 'enzootic ataxia' (sway back, lamkruis) was recognized as a clinical and disease entity in 1937 as a copper deficiency in pregnant sheep. Copper supplements prevented the syndrome, which was characterized by demyelination of the cerebellum and spinal cord."

### WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000107 · mechanism
- **essentials:** copper · **conditions:** kawasaki_disease, cerebral_palsy, cerebellar_hypoplasia, spider_veins, varicose_veins, hemorrhoids, aneurysm · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** The mechanism behind copper's cardiovascular effects. Copper activates numerous 'cupro-enzymes' (copper-dependent enzymes) that build and maintain the heart and blood vessels. In a copper-deficient pregnancy this causes congenital defects of the heart and vessels (Kawasaki disease) and of the brain (cerebral palsy, plus atresia — failure to form — and hypoplasia, or underdevelopment, of the cerebellum). Copper deficiency also lowers the activity of lysyl oxidase, the enzyme that converts proelastin into elastin; with less elastin, the walls of veins and arteries lose tensile strength — producing spider veins, varicose veins, hemorrhoids and aneurysms. In his words: "Copper functions as a cofactor and activator of numerous cupro-enzymes that are involved with the development and maintenance of the cardiovascular system. Deficiency of copper in the pregnant female results in congenital defects of the heart and vascular system (Kawasaki disease) and brain (cerebral palsy, atresia and hypoplasia of the cerebellum). Deficiency of copper also results in reduced lysyl oxidase activity causing a reduction in conversion of proelastin to elastin causing a decrease in the tensile strength of venous and arterial walls (spider veins, varicose veins, hemorrhoids and aneurysms)."

### WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000110 · definition
- **essentials:** copper · **conditions:** menkes_syndrome · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Menkes' Kinky Hair Syndrome is the clearest genetic proof of copper's importance. Wallach describes it as a sex-linked recessive genetic defect (carried on the X chromosome) that blocks copper absorption. Affected infants show stunted growth, defective keratin in the hair (the 'kinky' hair), loss of hair pigment, low body temperature (from low thyroid function) and anemia, degeneration and fractures of the aortic elastin (aneurysms), arthritis in the growth plates of the long bones, and progressive mental deterioration — the last because the brain is left without cytochrome oxidase, a vital copper-dependent enzyme. Because copper can't be absorbed normally, treatment uses injected copper plus liver extract and a celiac-type diet. In his words: "Menkes' Kinky Hair Syndrome is thought to be a sex-linked recessive genetic defect of copper absorption. The affected infants exhibit retarded growth, defective keratin formation of the hair, loss of hair pigment, low body temperature (low thyroid function, anemia), degeneration and fractures of aortic elastin (aneurysms), arthritis in the growth plates of long bones, and a progressive mental deterioration. Mental deterioration results from brain tissue being totally devoid of the essential copper dependent enzyme cytochrome oxidase."
