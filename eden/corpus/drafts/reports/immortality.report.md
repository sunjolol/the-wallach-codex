### WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000021 · mechanism
- **essentials:** calcium · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Are common calcium pills a good deal? Wallach says no - metallic mineral supplements like calcium carbonate are barely absorbed (about 8-12% in the young, falling to 3-5% after age 35-40). Worked example: a 1,000mg calcium carbonate tablet is only 400mg of actual (elemental) calcium, and at roughly a 10% absorption rate you net just ~80mg usable - so to truly get 2,000mg of usable calcium you'd have to swallow about 25 tablets a day (a 100-count bottle would last only 4 days). In his words: "Two 1,000 mg calcium carbonate capsules or tablets do not give you 2,000 mg of calcium - they only supply 800 mg of elemental calcium and at a 10% availability rate you will net only 80 mg of available calcium. Therefore, to get 2,000 mg of usable calcium from a 1,000 mg capsule or tablet of calcium carbonate, you would have to take 25 capsules each day!"

### WAL-CLM-IMMORT-000023 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Chelated minerals - where an amino acid, protein, or enzyme is wrapped ('claw,' from the Latin) around each mineral - absorb far better than plain metallic ones: about 40-65%, which Wallach calls a 400% improvement over metallic minerals (8-12%). He notes the form was a 1940s livestock-industry invention (the original chelating agent, calcium EDTA, was a German pre-WWI antidote for arsenic and lead exposure, still used today for intravenous chelation of clogged arteries), and that you can spot chelated forms on a label as selenomethionine, chromium picolinate, or 'amino acid chelate.' In his words: "Chelated minerals are approximately 40 to 65% bioavailable, a 400% increase over the availability of metallic minerals - certainly, a significant improvement over the amounts elemental minerals that are absorbed."
