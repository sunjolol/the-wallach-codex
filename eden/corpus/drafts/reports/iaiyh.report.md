### WAL-CLM-IAIYH-000005 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** anosmia · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach explains that the olfactory (first cranial) nerve carries the sense of smell from the lining of the nose through the cribriform plate of the skull to the brain. When osteoporosis of the skull narrows that bony channel, the nerve is pinched and the sense of smell is reduced or lost entirely (anosmia). In his words: "The olfactory nerve originates in the nasal mucosa and travels through the cribriform plate of the ethmoid bone to the olfactory lobe of the brain to produce the sense of smell. Osteoporosis of the skull will reduce the caliber of the tunnels resulting in the reduction of or the total loss of the sense of smell."

### WAL-CLM-IAIYH-000012 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** tinnitus, deafness, vertigo, menieres_disease · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach maps the vestibulocochlear (eighth cranial) nerve, which carries hearing and balance. When its cochlear branch is incarcerated by skull osteoporosis a tinnitus (ringing tone) is produced and gradual deafness can follow; when its vestibular branch is incarcerated it produces vertigo. Wallach renames this vertigo 'Wallach's Vertigo' and (in his disease table) equates it with Meniere's disease -- recasting Meniere's as a treatable nerve-incarceration rather than an unexplained inner-ear disorder. In his words: "When the cochlear branch is incarcerated a “tinnitus” or tone is produced (gradual deafness can occur); when the vestibular nerve is incarcerated it produces a vertigo (Wallach's Vertigo)."
