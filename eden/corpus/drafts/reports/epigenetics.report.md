### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000077 · deficiency_sign
- **essentials:** cobalt · **conditions:** pernicious_anemia, anemia · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Cobalt is essential because it is the metal at the center of vitamin B12 (cobalamin): a single cobalt atom sits in B12's tetrapyrrole ring, and the vitamin acts as a cofactor for transferring methyl groups -- with folic acid, choline, and methionine -- to build RNA and DNA, and for myelin formation and red-blood-cell synthesis. Wallach recounts that cobalt's essentiality was discovered when veterinarians found the fatal 'bush sickness' in Australian and New Zealand livestock (emaciation, listlessness, anorexia, microcytic anemia) could be cured by cobalt supplementation, and that pernicious anemia and demyelination of the spinal cord are the classic signs of B12/cobalt deficiency in humans -- common in vegans, after stomach or ileum surgery, and with hypochlorhydria (low stomach acid). He prefers 250 to 400 micrograms a day (above the 3-to-4 microgram conventional allowance), notes calcium is required for B12 to cross the intestinal wall, and warns that cobalt excess (20 to 30 milligrams a day) can drive erythropoiesis; cobalt is also a cofactor for thyroid-hormone production.
