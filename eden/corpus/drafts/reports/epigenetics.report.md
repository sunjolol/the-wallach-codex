### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000091 · toxicity_sign
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Mercury occurs universally in living things and is a well-known toxic element; Wallach traces its accumulation to industry, mining, agriculture, dental amalgam fillings, and micro-organisms that convert it to methyl mercury in aquatic sediments. Methyl mercury (the form in fish) crosses the placenta easily and has a biological half-life of about 70 days; heavy fish eaters, especially of large predators such as shark, tuna and swordfish, can reach tissue levels up to 400 micrograms per kilogram. Direct mercury poisoning is primarily neurological -- tremors, vertigo, irritability, moodiness, depression, salivation, inflammation of the mouth (stomatitis) and diarrhea -- while poisoning with the more toxic alkyl mercury progresses to loss of coordination, vision and hearing. Wallach notes selenium and mercury are mutually antagonistic: selenium protects the kidney from mercury-induced tissue death (necrosis) and limits placental transfer. He cites the 1962 Minamata Bay disaster in Japan and a mass poisoning in Iran (bread from mercury-treated seed grain) as producing congenital brain defects, cerebral palsy and infant deaths, and relays the theory that dental-amalgam vapor is linked to multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, Lou Gehrig's disease) and Parkinson's disease.
- **verbatim** (offset 1249243): “The manifestations of direct Hg poisoning are primarily neurological
(ie, tremors, vertigo, irritability, moodiness [suicidal], depression, salivation,
inflammation of the mouth [stomatitis] and diarrhea).”

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000093 · deficiency_sign
- **essentials:** iodine · **conditions:** hypothyroidism, hashimotos_disease, goiter, cretinism · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Iodine is an essential mineral concentrated in the thyroid gland; combined with the amino acid tyrosine it is manufactured into the thyroid hormone thyroxin, which regulates digestion, heart rate, body temperature, sweat-gland activity, the nervous and reproductive systems, general metabolism and body weight. Wallach argues iodine intake is usually low to begin with, and that as Americans restrict their salt intake on medical advice, physician-caused goiter and hypothyroidism have become epidemic; copper is a required cofactor for using iodine, so copper-poor soils also drive goiter. Low iodine and thyroid function produce hypothyroidism (Hashimoto's disease) -- fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, hair loss, depression, constipation, dry skin, poor memory, goiter, infertility, and congenital birth defects (cretinism in the newborn). He notes many foods act as goitrogens that interfere with thyroid function (dietary and waterborne nitrates, cruciferous vegetables, gluten intolerance) and that about 11 million Americans have an under- or over-active thyroid; the average American takes in only 170 to 250 micrograms of iodine per day, and metallic iodine is not toxic up to 2,000 micrograms per day.
- **verbatim** (offset 1251635): “since Americans have begun restricting their salt intake at their doctors
instruction, the rate of “allopath” caused goiter and hypothyroidism has become
epidemic.”

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000094 · deficiency_sign
- **essentials:** potassium · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Potassium is essential to all organisms and is the major positively-charged ion (cation) inside cells, driving electrochemical and enzyme functions; it makes up about five percent of the body's total mineral content. With sodium, the other main electrolyte, potassium maintains normal water balance, osmotic equilibrium and acid-base balance, and works with calcium to regulate neuromuscular activity. It is easily absorbed but barely stored -- about 90 percent of ingested potassium is excreted in the urine -- so Wallach states a significant daily intake of 5,000 milligrams is required. Chronic potassium deficiency shows as muscular weakness and mental apathy, and its most serious event is hypokalemic (low-potassium) cardiac failure. Diuretics (both natural and prescribed), sweating from colds, flu or exercise, vomiting and diarrhea all increase the loss of potassium and other minerals.
- **verbatim** (offset 1255641): “Muscular weakness and mental apathy are features of chronic K deficiency;
hypokalemic cardiac failure is the most serious K deficiency event.”

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000095 · mechanism
- **essentials:** lanthanum · **conditions:** chronic_fatigue · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Lanthanum is a 'light' rare-earth metal. Beyond its natural distribution, Wallach highlights that the yeast Candida albicans accumulates lanthanum heavily -- up to 370 parts per million per day -- and proposes that this is how Candida infestations may cause a debilitating, energy-sapping 'chronic fatigue-like' disease, by stealing lanthanum from the patient.
