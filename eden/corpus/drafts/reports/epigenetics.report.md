### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000193 · personal_anecdote
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Much of Wallach's conviction about zinc and birth defects came from his years as a zoo and veterinary pathologist. He describes a constant stream of congenital birth defects in animals that he traced directly to nutritional deficiencies in the mother before and during pregnancy: cerebral palsy in a llama from copper and zinc deficiency, spina bifida in a monkey from folic acid and/or zinc deficiency, cleft palates in arctic foxes from vitamin A and/or zinc deficiency, and a ventricular septal heart defect in a kangaroo. Once the mothers' diets were adjusted, he says, the defects were prevented — the same logic he applies to human pregnancy.

in a kangaroo (from a congenital vitamin A and or a zinc deficiency).”

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000195 · toxicity_sign
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach's book presents magnesium as very safe: if the kidneys are healthy, he states there is no evidence of toxicity at intakes up to 6,000 mg per day. For reference he cites the conventional RDA at 350 mg/day for adult men, 300 mg/day for women, and 450 mg/day in pregnancy and lactation. The one caveat he flags is that an actual excess of magnesium will inhibit bone calcification — a reminder that magnesium and calcium must stay in balance rather than either one running away.
- **verbatim** (offset 1267483): “The RDA for Mg is 350 mg/day for adult males, 300 mg/day for adult females
and 450 mg/day for pregnant and lactating females. If kidneys are healthy there
is no evidence of toxicity at up to 6,000 mg/day.”
