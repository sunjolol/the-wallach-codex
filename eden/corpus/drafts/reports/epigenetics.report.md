### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000444 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Citrulline is the raw material the body uses to make the amino acid arginine. Wallach's Epigenetics explains that arginine is synthesized from citrulline through two cytosolic enzymes — argininosuccinate synthetase (ASS) and argininosuccinate lyase (ASL). The book notes this conversion is energy-expensive, because producing each molecule of argininosuccinate along the way requires spending ATP (the cell's energy currency, hydrolyzed to AMP); one reason it gives for supplementing arginine directly is that doing so leaves more ATP available to fuel other cell functions. In short, citrulline sits one step upstream of arginine in the body's biochemistry.
- **verbatim** (offset 1110124): “Arginine (Arg) was first isolated in 1886 from a lupin seedling by the Swiss
chemist Ernst Schultze and is synthesized from citrulline by the sequential
employment of the cytosolic enzymes argininosuccinate synthetase (ASS) and

argininosuccinate lyase (ASL).”

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000445 · mechanism

- **claim:** Nitric oxide (NO) is a compound the body produces from the amino acid arginine. According to Wallach's Epigenetics, oral arginine supplementation is required for nitric oxide synthesis, and it lowers blood pressure and increases blood flow through obstructed (narrowed or blocked) blood vessels. The same passage also credits oral arginine with reducing healing time after trauma — and being particularly useful for bone trauma — alongside supporting cell division, wound healing, and immune function. In Wallach's framing, nitric oxide is the molecule behind arginine's circulation benefits: easier blood flow and lower blood pressure.

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000446 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Serotonin is a neurotransmitter — a chemical that carries signals in the nervous system. The body builds it from the essential amino acid tryptophan using the enzyme tryptophan hydroxylase, which is why tryptophan is called serotonin's biochemical precursor. Serotonin can then be converted a step further into the neurohormone melatonin. In the same discussion Wallach notes that conditions cutting tryptophan absorption — fructose malabsorption, lactose intolerance, and gluten intolerance — lower blood tryptophan and are tied to clinical depression, and that a tryptophan metabolite (5-HTP) rapidly crosses into the brain where it is converted to serotonin (also called 5-hydroxytryptamine, or 5-HT).
- **verbatim** (offset 1121330): “Tryptophan is employed as a building block of protein
biosynthesis and is a biochemical precursor of serotonin (a neurotransmitter),
synthesized via tryptophan hydroxylase. Serotonin can be converted to melatonin
(a neurohormone)”

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000449 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Acetylcholine is one of the body's most common neurotransmitters — a chemical messenger that carries a signal across the gap between nerve cells. It works by binding to receptors on the receiving cell and altering that membrane's permeability to specific ions, which either fires the cell (depolarizing it) or quiets it (hyperpolarizing it). The body assembles acetylcholine in the central nervous system, drawing on choline as a structural component, and its production also relies on the acyl-transfer reactions run by coenzyme A, the active form of vitamin B5.

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000451 · mechanism
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** DHA (docosahexaenoic acid) is an omega-3 fatty acid. Wallach explains that DHA together with EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid) is required for the enzyme pathways the body uses to process long-chain polyunsaturated fats. He ties DHA directly to mental health: a low level of DHA in the blood is a marker for low brain levels of 5-HIAA (a serotonin breakdown product), and low brain 5-HIAA is directly associated with higher rates of depression and suicide. Elsewhere he places DHA in the "conditionally essential" group of fats and lists DHA and EPA among the nutrients that support memory and cognition.

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000452 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Arsenic trioxide — the arsenic compound that 18th-century physician Thomas Fowler turned into a medicine — has been used medicinally for roughly 500 years, most often against cancer. In 2000 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved it to treat acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), an often-fatal cancer of the blood and bone marrow. A 1998 New England Journal of Medicine report from Memorial Sloan-Kettering researchers found that low doses of arsenic trioxide destroyed the cancerous cells in 12 seriously ill APL patients and brought them into remission; Chinese researchers had reported the same result in 1997 (journal Blood), and their survivors were still leukemia-free ten years later. Arsenic is also used, as "Fowler's solution," to treat psoriasis.
- **verbatim** (offset 1156058): “The doctors were the first in Western medicine to show that low
doses of arsenic trioxide are effective in destroying such cancerous cells.”
