### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000285 · definition
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Vitamin K is one of the fat-soluble vitamins in Wallach's essential-nutrient framework. Isolated in 1939, it is required by the liver to produce prothrombin and at least five other proteins (clotting factors VII, IX and X and proteins C and S), so the blood can clot normally. It also drives the proper deposition of calcium into bone — through the vitamin K-dependent protein osteocalcin — which is why Wallach calls it as essential to healthy bone as vitamin D. It comes in two forms: K1 (phylloquinone) from green plants and K2 (menaquinones) made by friendly bacteria in the colon, which supply roughly half of the body's requirement.

for the production of prothrombin and at least five other proteins (factors VII, IX,
and X, and proteins C and S) and other biologically active substances essential
for proper blood clotting and for the proper deposition of calcium in bones.”

### WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000289 · toxicity_sign
- **essentials:** — · **conditions:** — · **symptoms:** —
- **claim:** Wallach describes signs of vitamin K overdose: jaundice (kernicterus) in newborn infants, and — with rapid intravenous injection of phylloquinone in adults — flushing, shortness of breath, chest pain, and death. Large doses of the synthetic form menadione (not phylloquinone) given to animals produced hemolytic anemia, polycythemia (too many red blood cells), an enlarged spleen, kidney and liver damage, and death. He draws a clear line between the natural form and the greater toxicity of synthetic menadione.
- **verbatim** (offset 1079909): “Signs of overdose: Symptoms include jaundice (kernicterus) in newborn

(hyperbilirubinemia) and death.”

- **claim:** Flavonoids (also called bioflavonoids) are a diverse group of biologically active carbon compounds, and they are the plant pigments responsible for the yellow, red, and blue colors of flowers, colors that attract pollinating insects.
