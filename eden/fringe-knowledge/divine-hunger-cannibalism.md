## The core thesis — cannibalism as "the ultimate extension of pica"

Wallach adds a **fourth theory** to the three standard anthropological theories of
cannibalism's origin (psycho-sexual needs; utilitarian adaptation to famine; cultural
"mortuary"/endocannibalism). His addition:

> "Statistically cannibalism can be tied to hunger, but hunger for calories and protein alone cannot be tied to cannibalism, so we will add a fourth category of cannibalism; 4) the ultimate extension of pica (bizarre cravings and behavior resulting from extreme mineral deficiencies)."

The whole chapter argues that cannibalism, the **vampire** legend, the **werewolf** legend,
and **modern serial killers** are all expressions of extreme mineral deficiency — a craving
for blood, flesh, and bone (mineral sources) that cannot be satisfied otherwise. This is
the same pica mechanism captured legitimately in the corpus (WAL-CLM-RARE-000035..040),
pushed to an extreme, off-mission claim.

## How he applies it

- **Aztec cannibalism.** Minerally depleted soil in the Valley of Mexico depleted wild game
  and domestic animals; "to satisfy the craving for minerals required meat and bone!!!
  Cannibalism was the logical solution in the eyes of the Aztec." (cites ~250,000 eaten
  annually during the Spanish conquest era.)

- **Dracula / vampires.**
  > "Thus the legend of the 'vampire' Count Dracula was born out of the true recorded history of a serial killer who drank the blood and ate the flesh of his victims to satisfy his bizarre cravings for minerals (pica) he couldn't get any other way."
  Also **Gilles de Rais** ("after many years of indulging in rich French pastries… Gilles had
  developed pica and began a life as a depraved pedophile, murderer, serial killer and
  vampire!") and **Elizabeth Báthory** (craving for blood; believed virgin blood conferred
  immortality).

- **Werewolves.** Casts North American "cannibal monster" legends as werewolves — the
  **Windigo** (Algonkian) and a Cree account of famine-driven "a more than canine hunger
  (werewolves)." Notes: "in ancient times 'vampires' were dispatched with wooden stakes
  through the heart; 'werewolves' were destroyed with silver bullets or silver spear heads."

- **Modern serial killers = "the vampires and werewolves of our time."**
  > "Like the ancient vampires and werewolves, their modern counterparts have flagrant histories of nutritionally deficient diets and awful eating habits which of course lead to sociopathic behavior."
  He builds a list of "common behavioral threads" connecting vampires/werewolves/serial
  killers recognizable early (ritualistic behavior; bizarre cravings/pica; bed-wetting;
  nightmares; smoking/alcohol/drug abuse framed as pica). Individual cases he attributes to
  pica / mineral deficiency include **James Maybrick** (arsenic pica → his "Jack the Ripper"
  theory; he reads Maybrick's terminal symptoms as chromium/vanadium, B-vitamin, and calcium
  deficiencies), **Andrei Chikatilo**, **Ed Gein**, and **Waneta Hoyt**.

- **Conclusion / the soil link.**
  > "Since the 'Jack the Ripper' killings it seems there appears a new serial killer each week (the soils are depleted of minerals so the rate of pica and depraved attempts to quench the cravings with human blood and flesh increase at a geometric rate!)"
  Closing rhetorical question: "will jails, more police, social workers, Prozac and
  psychiatrists be effective against these legendary monsters today?"

## Cross-reference — Epigenetics (2014), Ch.18 "Lithium"

The cannibalism-as-pica thesis recorded above from Rare Earths is **restated** in the later
book Epigenetics, inside the Lithium entry. Wallach again adds cannibalism as a fourth
category to the three standard anthropological theories, calling it "the ultimate extension
of pica (bizarre cravings and behavior resulting from extreme mineral deficiencies, such as
Fe, P, Li, Ca, etc)," and illustrates the over-population / food-collapse fear with the 1973
film *Soylent Green*. No new thesis content beyond the Rare Earths "Divine Hunger" material;
recorded 2026-07-01 so the second-book restatement is not lost. The non-graphic clinical
lithium claim is sealed as WAL-CLM-EPIGEN-000096.
